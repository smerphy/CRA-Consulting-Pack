## Key Question 1 — Authority guidance aimed at implementation (Commission Art 26 guidance and FAQs; ENISA technical guidance; BSI TR-03183 Parts 1–3 and H; other national agencies)

### Takeaway
By October 2026 there is a usable, layered stack of official implementation guidance: the Commission's living FAQ (v1.0 3 Dec 2025 → v1.4 4 Sep 2026) and its ~80-page Article 26 guidance C(2026) 5252 (27 Jul 2026, non-binding, formally still a draft Communication pending language versions); ENISA's SME-oriented technical advisories (Secure-by-Design playbook Jul 2026, SBOM advisory v1.10 Oct 2026, security.txt/CVD/advisory kit Oct 2026, draft Update Mechanisms advisory May 2026) plus the SRP user manual (v1.2, 21 Oct 2026); and Germany's BSI TR-03183 series (Part 1 v1.0.0 31 Jul 2026 "living document"; Part 2 SBOM v2.1.0 20 Aug 2025; Part 3 vulnerability reports v1.0.0 20 Aug 2025; Part H Module H v1.1.0 30 May 2026). None of these gives presumption of conformity; no harmonised standard is yet cited in the OJEU.

- Third-party summary: the guidance is about 80 pages, built around 67 practical examples, targets microenterprises/SMEs first, and was approved as a draft that applies only after formal adoption in all EU languages — [cyberresilienceact.eu Commission guidance page](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/commission-guidance.html); [cyberresilienceact.eu news 27 Jul 2026](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/commission-cra-application-guidance-published-27-july-2026.html) (independent site operated by i46 s.r.o., not official).

- ENISA "Technical Advisory for Software Bill of Materials", v1.10, October 2026 (listed 22 Oct 2026 by a tracker). - ENISA "Technical Advisory on security.txt, CVD Policies and Security Advisories – SME Vulnerability Kit", October 2026 (public consultation version): explains security.txt, CVD, advisories; "common mistakes" table; ready-to-use templates for security.txt, a 10-section CVD policy and a security advisory; explicitly supports the CRA and the draft harmonised standard EN 40000-1-3 — [ENISA security.txt/CVD advisory PDF](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/Technical%20Advisory%20on%20security.txt%20CVD%20Policies%20and%20Security%20Advisories%20Public%20Consultation.pdf).
- ENISA "Technical Advisory on Secure Update Mechanisms", May 2026, Version 0.1 (draft; the consultation note is a "<To-do>" placeholder). Maps to Annex I Part II 2.2/2.7/2.8/2.4 and Part I 2(c)/2(f); covers update lifecycle, delivery architectures (in-band, platform-managed, out-of-band, enterprise-staged), threats per stage, controls (preparation/publication, discovery/retrieval, verification/installation, observability/recovery), and an end-to-end example; technology-agnostic but "consistent with" TUF, Uptane and NIST SP 800-40 — [ENISA update mechanisms draft PDF](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-05/Draft%20-%20ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Update%20Mechanisms%20-%20v0.6.pdf).
- ENISA/JRC "Cyber Resilience Act Requirements Standards Mapping" (EUR 31892 EN, 2024): bottom-up mapping of each Annex I requirement to existing standards (e.g. Part I(1) → ISO/IEC 27002, 27005, IEC 62443-3-2, 62443-4-1, ETSI EN 303 645; Part II(1) SBOM → ISO/IEC 27036 only, with explicit gap; Part II(2) → ISO/IEC 30111, 29147, IEC 62443-4-1; Part II(5) CVD → ISO/IEC 29147/30111/ETSI EN 303 645), with gaps noted for SBOM creation/exchange, remediation/patch management, secure update installation and user notification, testing frequency, disclosure timelines/formats, logging, hardware — [ENISA standards mapping PDF](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-11/Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20Requirements%20Standards%20Mapping%20-%20final_with_identifiers_0.pdf).

- **Part 1 (General requirements) v1.0.0**, dated 31.07.2026 (changelog 2025-07-31; the PDF's own dates are inconsistent), "State: Living Document". Structure: CRA overview incl. §3.11 "How to prepare for the CRA"; §4 Usage (evaluator, scope, PASS/FAIL/N/A verdicts, §4.8 Assessment Report); §5 Risk-based approach with activities RH_RA.1 (risk assessment incl. RH_RA.1.1.2 threat modelling), RH_RT.1 (treatment), RH_DOC.1 (documentation), RH_UPD.1 (update); §5.14 Decision criteria; §6 Adaptable Risk-based Controls (ARC) with predefined risk profiles; §7 PwDE controls (OSCAL); Annex B essential requirements; Annex C worked example (network camera: PwDE description, risk context, assets, threat modelling, evaluation, acceptance, mitigation, update); Annex D risk scoring (environment scoring, acceptance criteria) — [BSI TR-03183-1 v1.0.0 PDF](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-1_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).
- Part 1 explicitly does NOT establish obligations, provide presumption of conformity, or "describe the only way" to meet Annex I — [BSI TR-03183-1 §2](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-1_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).
- Part 1 §3.11 five-point preparation: honest risk assessment of assets and threats; mitigate to acceptable level with controls based on essential requirements; communicate transparently with users; maintain security across lifecycle and cooperate with authorities; "use existing best practices and do not reinvent the wheel" — [BSI TR-03183-1 §3.11](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-1_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).

- Third-party description of Part 1: risk process follows ISO 31000; assets in three categories (data, functional, security); impact rated 1–5 on C/I/A; likelihood factors are interface restriction, access restriction and user capability; Appendix D "Environment Indicator" scoring is experimental; the author's verdict is "a guide, not a recipe" — [IBF Solutions, 27 Aug 2026](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/news-and-knowledge/technical-papers-and-news-on-ce-marking/the-new-technical-guideline-bsi-tr-03183-1) [consultancy].
- **Part 2 (SBOM) v2.1.0**, dated 2025-08-20 (BSI page shows 03.09.2025). Formats: JSON or XML, CycloneDX ≥1.6 or SPDX ≥3.0.1 (v2.0.0 had required CycloneDX ≥1.5 / SPDX ≥2.2.1). Transitional rule: use the most recent version; the immediately preceding version may be used for six months after a new version — [BSI TR-03183-2 v2.1.0 PDF](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-2_v2_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6). (Field details in Key Question 5.)
- **Part 3 (Vulnerability Reports and Notifications) v1.0.0**, dated 2025-08-20 (draft 0.9.0 was 2024-09-20). Scope: requirements for *receiving* vulnerability reports — manufacturer website, RFC 9116 security.txt (location, canonical URI, contacts, OpenPGP keys, acknowledgements, preferred languages, CVD policy link, CSAF advisories, expiry, signature, crawler visibility), preliminary CVD measures (roles, encryption, web form, reporting web page), CVD policy content (national CSIRT, contacts, assurances, vulnerability guideline, code of conduct, communication, guaranteed response times, anonymous reporting, disclosure, end of CVD), and a dedicated web page. Annex references ISO/IEC 29147, 30111, 20153:2025, CERT CVD guide, SecureDrop, CSAF provider — [BSI TR-03183-3 v1.0.0 PDF](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-3_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4). (Normative detail in Key Question 5.)
- **Part H (Conformity based on full quality assurance, Module H) v1.1.0**, dated 30/05/2026: describes how to demonstrate CRA conformity via an ISO/IEC 27001-compliant ISMS extended to cover design, development, production and vulnerability handling of PwDE; not a new management-system standard; specifies CRA-specific refinements of ISO/IEC 27001 clauses (4.2, 4.3, 5.2, 6.1.2, 6.1.3, 6.3, 7.4, 7.5.1, 8.1) and CRA-specific guidance on ISO/IEC 27002 controls (5.7 threat intelligence, 5.8 project security, 8.8 technical vulnerabilities, 8.25 secure development life cycle, 8.29 security testing, 8.32 change management); applies additional ISO 9001 requirements; a single ISMS may cover several product lines — [BSI TR-03183-H v1.1.0 PDF](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-H_v1_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).
- Part H Appendix B: Module H under the CRA is "not just a quality system certification" — it includes review of technical documentation for one model of each product category (Annex VII) and the audit team must include a product-field-experienced assessor; conversely Module B+C includes regular audits of Annex I Part II vulnerability handling, so the two modules are "not as distinct" as elsewhere; critical products should use a CSA scheme where mandated under Art 8(1) — [BSI TR-03183-H Appendix B](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-H_v1_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).

### Inferences
- The practical "canon" for an engineering team in late 2026 is: Commission guidance + FAQ for legal interpretation (awareness, substantial modification, support period, families), BSI TR-03183-1 for a risk-assessment-to-technical-file method, TR-03183-2 for SBOM content, TR-03183-3 + ENISA Oct 2026 kit for CVD/PSIRT set-up, ENISA SBOM/update advisories for pipeline practice, and TR-03183-H if choosing Module H via ISO/IEC 27001.
- Because all of this is non-binding and no hEN is cited, teams should document *which* guidance they followed and why, and expect to re-map to EN 40000-1-3 (vulnerability handling, expected in OJEU) when it is cited.

## Key Question 2 — Notified-body and testing-lab readiness programmes (TÜV SÜD, TÜV Rheinland/TÜViT, DEKRA, Bureau Veritas, UL, SGS/Brightsight, BSI, Nemko, Applus, Eurofins)

### Takeaway
As of late October 2026 no notified body appears to be designated in NANDO for the CRA (independent tracker as of 8 Sep 2026; no designation news found in Sep–Oct 2026 feeds), although Chapter IV applied from 11 June 2026 and Member States should strive for sufficient capacity by 11 December 2026. Labs (TÜViT/TÜV NORD, DEKRA, Brightsight/SGS, UL, Applus, Nemko) sell gap assessments, pre-assessments/readiness checks, workshops and Module B/H preparation; evidence expectations converge on: technical documentation, cybersecurity risk assessment, SBOM, vulnerability handling process/CVD policy, test evidence, and mappings from existing certifications (IEC 62443, EUCC/CC, SESIP, EN 18031). No lab publishes lead times or prices; the only figures found are unsourced vendor estimates.

### Cited Findings
- As of late June 2026 NANDO was "effectively empty for the CRA"; an 8 Sep 2026 update says no notified body had been designated; each Member State must first designate a notifying authority (Art 36), and designation under Art 43 "takes months per body"; Art 35(2) asks Member States to strive for sufficient notified bodies by 11 Dec 2026 — [cyberresilienceact.eu, notified bodies 11 Jun 2026](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-notified-bodies-rules-apply-11-june-2026.html) (independent site).
- A vendor tracker's Sep–Oct 2026 feed lists no NANDO designations, only: ENISA SBOM advisory (22 Oct 2026), SRP launch (11 Sep 2026), a Slovenian corrigendum (9 Oct 2026), and a Commission public-procurement proposal amending the CRA (9 Sep 2026) — [RedComply regulatory updates](https://redcomply.com/resources/cra-regulatory-updates) [vendor].
- Commission FAQ: notified bodies are listed in NANDO; they must be independent of manufacturers and market surveillance authorities — [Commission FAQ §6.9](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- **TÜViT / TÜV NORD**: offers a Gap Assessment measuring maturity of risk management, vulnerability management, supply chain, SBOM and documentation; Security-by-Design checks and pre-evaluations of architecture; Compliance Strategy Workshop to choose the conformity route across a portfolio; two-day workshop with a "personalised CRA roadmap"; Module H requires an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS extended by BSI TR-03183-H covering development, production, vulnerability handling and RDPS; Module B needs technical documentation, risk analysis and evidence of suitability of measures; post-market services (leak detection for Art 14, periodic pentest/code review/SAST/DAST); TÜV NORD "will apply to the BSI" for Module H designation; no lead times or prices — [TÜViT CRA page](https://www.tuvit.de/en/topics/regulations/cyber-resilience-act/) [vendor].
- **DEKRA**: training, evaluation against draft/reference standards mapped to essential requirements, "will be a Notified Body for CRA" leveraging RED DA notified-body and EUCC experience, CRA Pilot Program (early testing, product assessments, readiness checks); notification "starts in June 2026"; no lead times — [DEKRA CRA services](https://www.dekra.com/en/cyberresilienceact/) [vendor].

- Lab offerings are converging on a two-step pattern: (1) portfolio classification + gap assessment on a representative product, (2) module-specific preparation (B: product file; H: ISMS extension per TR-03183-H).

## Key Question 3 — The cybersecurity risk assessment (Art 13 and Annex VII): accepted methodologies, documenting Annex I applicability, keeping it current

### Takeaway
The Commission confirms no methodology is mandated, but the risk assessment must (a) cover the whole product incl. remote data processing, (b) state for each Annex I Part I(2) requirement whether and how it applies, with written justification for non-applicability, (c) be driven by intended purpose/reasonably foreseeable use/misuse and expected time in use, and (d) be updated through the support period. Residual risk is judged against "appropriate level of cybersecurity", not corporate risk appetite or cost. BSI TR-03183-1 supplies a concrete method (asset-based, ISO 31000-style, STRIDE-compatible threat modelling, decision criteria, ARC controls) and a report structure; EN 18031's decision-tree/decision-record approach and IEC 62443-3-2/4-1 are the most-cited reusable methods.

### Cited Findings
- The CRA "does not mandate a specific cybersecurity risk assessment methodology"; the methodology should support documenting that all relevant risks have been addressed so market surveillance can verify identification, evaluation and mitigation; threat modelling must reflect intended purpose/foreseeable use (e.g. critical-infrastructure products may need to treat nation-state/APT threats; consumer products may use a different model) — [Commission FAQ §4.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Per Art 13(3) the assessment must indicate whether and how each Part I(2) requirement applies and how it is implemented; where not applicable the manufacturer "should include a clear justification" in the risk assessment in the technical documentation (e.g. no personal data processing in intended use; interoperability-standard constraints per recital 55, then mitigate by other means such as limiting intended purpose to trusted environments or informing users) — [Commission FAQ §4.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Part II vulnerability-handling requirements must all be complied with throughout the support period; only Part I requirements are subject to risk-based applicability — [Commission FAQ §4.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- The risk assessment must cover the entire product incl. remote data processing and supporting functions; it covers identification/analysis/evaluation during design and the treatment measures through production, delivery and maintenance; a single multi-legislation risk assessment is allowed if compliance with each act can be demonstrated — [Commission FAQ §4.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Commission guidance §7.1: residual risk is assessed against "appropriate level of cybersecurity based on the risks", and "internal risk tolerance, commercial strategy or mere cost considerations are not relevant"; the CRA does not allow transfer of risk to users to compensate for design shortcomings; if risks cannot be addressed, design/functionality/intended purpose may have to change — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §7.1, paras 156–163](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Commission guidance §7.2: Annex I Part I(1) is a catch-all for risks not covered by other Part I items; in most cases compliance with the other requirements yields compliance with (1) — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §7.2](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Commission guidance §7.3: external risks (networks, back-ends outside RDPS scope) must be identified and mitigated at product level (e.g. cryptographic authentication of remote commands, integrity checks on config, logging/alerts, fail-secure on outage); integrated third-party components are handled through due diligence (Art 13(5)) — define what the product needs from the component and verify via documentation, assurance evidence or tests — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §7.3, paras 167–173](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Commission guidance §7.4: product families sharing architecture, security-relevant design, intended purpose and risks may rely on a single risk assessment, a single technical documentation set, a single conformity assessment and a single DoC listing variants; differences in interfaces, software stacks, update mechanisms or remote connectivity must be reflected — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §7.4, paras 174–177](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Keeping it current: Art 13(7) requires documentation and update "as appropriate" during the support period; information/instructions relying on the risk assessment must be updated too; the risk assessment must be updated after regular tests and reviews where relevant information emerges (Annex I Part II(3)); technical documentation must reflect all versions (Blue Guide §4.3) — [Commission FAQ §4.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Commission guidance §9.2.3: "regular tests and reviews" does not mean mechanical repetition at fixed intervals; it means regularly reviewing whether new threats/vulnerabilities require updating the test set and executing accordingly, proportionate to risk profile — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §9.2.3, paras 238–240](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- BSI TR-03183-1 method: RH_RA.1.1.2 threat modelling — identify threats to assets and affected components; use a structured approach based on an existing threat catalogue, supplemented by a data-flow model; split threat modelling per reusable component (e.g. companion app or RDPS shared across products) — [BSI TR-03183-1 §5.8.1.2](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-1_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).
- BSI TR-03183-1 RH_DOC.1: the manufacturer MUST document risk context, evaluated risks with implemented controls, shared risks and applicable essential requirements; tools/templates advisable. RH_UPD.1: MUST update when the risk context changes — at least on a security-relevant product update, a newly known vulnerability, or newly known threats/attack methods — and regular-interval updates are advisable — [BSI TR-03183-1 §5.12–5.13](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-1_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).
- BSI TR-03183-1 §6.3 ARC workflow: top-down — identify high-level assets/environments and combined risk profile (highest C/I/A impact; interface restriction local/remote), select all controls matching the profile, implement, refine the profile where a control is infeasible, re-evaluate assets; predefined risk profiles may seed the assessment but cannot replace it — [BSI TR-03183-1 §6.3–6.4](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-1_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).
- ENISA playbook: STRIDE presented as an illustrative method; threat modelling should follow the Threat Modeling Manifesto; anti-patterns: one-off compliance exercise, over-engineered models that don't change design, failing to review after substantial modifications; AI/ML products should add prompt injection, model poisoning, adversarial inputs; SMEs should aim for a "minimum viable model" tied to release gates, organised around Shostack's four questions with a one-page scope note deliverable — [ENISA playbook §2.3](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf).
- JRC/ENISA standards mapping places IEC 62443-3-2, IEC 62443-4-1, ISO/IEC 27005 and ETSI EN 303 645 against Annex I Part I(1) (risk-based design) — [ENISA standards mapping PDF](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-11/Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20Requirements%20Standards%20Mapping%20-%20final_with_identifiers_0.pdf).
- EN 18031 approach: decision trees determine whether each requirement applies, yielding PASS/FAIL/NOT APPLICABLE; each path should be recorded as a decision record (requirement, asset, interface, path, rationale, evidence); EN 18031 is "asset-based", not a flat checklist — [QIMA webinar guide, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.qima.com/blog/cyberexpert/red-en-18031-cra-readiness-guide) [vendor]. EN 18031-1/-2/-3 are cited in the OJEU for RED Delegated Regulation 2022/30, which Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/339 repeals with effect from 11 Dec 2027; EN 18031 is described as the basis for the EN 40000-1-4 generic requirements standard — [CRA Evidence standards tracker, 17 Oct 2026](https://craevidence.com/cra-compliance/harmonised-standards-status) [vendor]; [UL Solutions](https://www.ul.com/resources/cyber-resilience-act-why-manufacturers-must-act-now) [vendor].
- Industrial-sector research (KONE authors, 12 companies, Jun–Aug 2024): IEC 62443-4-1 (SDL), 4-2 (technical requirements) and 3-2 (risk assessment) are the most relevant; firms already using 62443 are better placed, but it does not cover CRA's fixed notification timelines, mandatory public disclosure or detailed user documentation — [arXiv 2505.14325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14325).
- Consultancy record structures aligned to the guidance: a "risk-to-control register" (threat, affected function/interface, deployment assumption, measure, evidence of effectiveness, residual-risk decision, related user information) and a per-release "security delta assessment" (updated threat model, SBOM delta, test results, approval, documentation delta) — [SCHUTZWERK, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.schutzwerk.com/en/blog/cra-guidance-2026/) [vendor].
- Early-adopter observation: organisations performed risk assessments but "could not show the decision trail"; recommends baseline TARAs and continuous reassessment — [OmniTrust Tales #3, 9 Jul 2026](https://omnitrust.com/blog/eu-cra-tales-from-the-trenches-3-why-security-maturity-is-not-enough-for-cra-readiness/) [consultancy].

### Inferences
- A defensible structure for the Annex VII risk-assessment section is: (1) product/RDPS boundary and intended purpose/foreseeable use/misuse statement; (2) asset list (data/functional/security); (3) threat model (STRIDE or IEC 62443-3-2 zones/conduits) with data-flow diagram; (4) risk evaluation against product-appropriate acceptance criteria; (5) Annex I Part I(2)(a)–(m) applicability matrix with justification and implementing controls; (6) due-diligence records for integrated components; (7) residual-risk statement; (8) change log and update triggers.
- The "update triggers" named by BSI (security-relevant update, new vulnerability, new threat) map directly onto PSIRT and release-gate events, so the risk assessment should be a living artefact wired into those processes rather than a document re-issued annually.

### Gaps
- No authority publishes a reference template for the Annex VII risk assessment; ENISA's SME survey shows templates are the single most-requested support (73%), but none from an authority was found.
- No source quantifies how notified bodies will judge threat-model depth; expectations remain anecdotal.

## Key Question 4 — SDLC changes: frameworks that map well, testing cadence, release gating, security/functionality update separation, update infrastructure, support-period policy and end-of-support communication

### Takeaway
Authorities point to IEC 62443-4-1, NIST SSDF, OWASP SAMM, ISO/IEC 27034 and the ENISA playbook as acceptable SDLC scaffolding; IEC 62443-4-1 is the most complete (covers 36 of 42 SSDF tasks fully and adds post-release practices SSDF lacks), but none covers CRA's fixed reporting timelines, SBOM depth, or user-documentation obligations. Concrete SDLC changes evidenced in guidance: threat model before development and re-run on substantial change; SBOM generated at build and stored per release; release gate recording tests, SBOM hash, "no known exploitable vulnerabilities" decision; separation of security from functionality updates where technically feasible (Commission gives worked examples); signed, role-separated, anti-rollback update infrastructure (TUF/Uptane-consistent); declared support period per product and per substantially-modified software version with end-date shown at purchase and EoS notification.

### Cited Findings
- ISAGCA/ISASecure comparison: 36 of 42 NIST SSDF (SP 800-218) tasks are fully covered by IEC 62443-4-1, 4 partially, 2 not (upper-management commitment; secured-component library); 62443-4-1 covers the whole lifecycle incl. 11 post-release practices SSDF lacks — [ISAGCA blog, late 2025](https://gca.isa.org/blog/new-resource-comparison-of-isa/iec-62443-4-1-and-nist-sp-800-218-secure-software-development-framework).
- IEC 62443-4-1 and -4-2 are not expected to be referenced in the OJEU for the CRA; amendments are in development — [IBF Solutions, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/news-and-knowledge/technical-papers-and-news-on-ce-marking/current-status-of-standardisation-for-the-cyber-resilience-act) [consultancy].
- ENISA playbook references OWASP SAMM for maturity and lists secure-coding/verification, logging, configuration/change management, and vulnerability/patch management playbooks each with "checklists for developers" and per-release evidence (SBOM, test results, release records) — [ENISA playbook](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf).
- BSI TR-03183-H maps CRA needs onto ISO/IEC 27002 controls 8.25 (secure development life cycle), 8.29 (security testing in development and acceptance), 8.32 (change management), 8.8 (technical vulnerabilities), 5.7 (threat intelligence), 5.8 (security in project management) — and treats post-market updates as "changes to the PwDE" subject to the design/development/production process — [BSI TR-03183-H](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-H_v1_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).
- "Known exploitable vulnerabilities" at placement on market: a vulnerability is "known" when listed in EUVD or other prominent databases, when disclosed to the manufacturer privately (incl. by AI-powered tooling), or prominently reported in reliable media; the manufacturer must confirm exploitability/applicability; late-stage findings require a risk-based release decision weighing severity, exploitability, impact and the risks of postponing release — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §9.2.2, paras 230–237](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Separation of security and functionality updates: manufacturers "are not to bundle" a security fix with functionality updates (e.g. fix SSL validation routine separately); bundling is allowed where a functionality change is needed to deliver the fix (e.g. replacing a parser with stricter behaviour) or where disabling a vulnerable interface is itself the fix — [Commission FAQ §4.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Software updates as substantial modifications: assess case-by-case whether an update introduces new/increased risks not covered by the risk assessment; changes to intended purpose (dashboard gaining control capability; app gaining automated profiling) are substantial; features foreseen and assessed in the original risk assessment are not; even small features (persistent login tokens; unencrypted diagnostic log export) can be substantial; security updates that reduce risk are generally not, even if technically significant — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §4.3, paras 103–108, Examples 40–48](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- ENISA update advisory maps CRA requirements: timely remediation (Annex I II.2.2), integrity protection and corruption reporting (I.2(f)), secure distribution (II.2.7), prompt dissemination (II.2.8), separation of security/functional updates (II.2.2), automatic security updates with user controls (I.2(c)), user notification (I.2(c)/II.2.8), remediation guidance (II.2.4); recommends role-separated signing (offline root, operational key), freshness/anti-rollback metadata (timestamp, expiry, rollback counter), recording update outcomes, recovery to known-good, signed provenance and separation of duties for high-impact releases; cites TUF, Uptane and NIST SP 800-40 — [ENISA update mechanisms draft](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-05/Draft%20-%20ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Update%20Mechanisms%20-%20v0.6.pdf).
- Support period: at least five years unless expected use is shorter; five years is "not to be considered as the default" — longer expected use needs longer support; end date (at least month/year) must be indicated at purchase and an end-of-support notification displayed where technically feasible (Art 13(19)); each substantially modified software version needs a declared support period; Art 13(10) lets manufacturers remediate only the latest version if users can upgrade free of charge without "additional costs" (new hardware, infrastructure replacement — not routine testing/configuration); a substantial modification requires reassessing but does not automatically reset the support period — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §5, paras 125–134](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Reporting obligations continue after end of support and for products placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027, but vulnerability-handling obligations do not — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §9.1, para 210](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Tailor-made products may deviate only from secure-by-default (Annex I Part I 2(b)) and free security updates (Part II(8)) under explicit contractual terms; minor customisation of a multi-tenant product does not qualify; evidence of tailor-made status belongs in the technical documentation — [Commission FAQ §4.2](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Due diligence on open-source components: check CE marking where applicable, review update history, confirm no registered vulnerabilities in EUVD; level of diligence should match component risk — [UL Solutions](https://www.ul.com/resources/cyber-resilience-act-why-manufacturers-must-act-now) [vendor]; Commission guidance: identify what the product needs from the component and verify via documentation/assurance evidence or tests — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §7.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- OpenChain CRA checklist (PA5 draft, 5 Aug 2026) SDLC items: threat model done before development and updated over lifecycle; no default credentials and default-deny network exposure; SAST on every release candidate with critical/high findings resolved or risk-accepted; SAST/DAST or fuzzing results; pentest/security review record; a "release security gate record"; SHA-256+ checksums for all release artefacts; secure update mechanism with integrity verification, rollback protection and automatic delivery by default with opt-out; EoS notification ideally ≥12 months before final security update; transition guide and legacy-version owner — [OpenChain CRA checklist](https://github.com/OpenChain-Project/CRA-Compliance/blob/main/CRA_Checklist_Requirement_latest.md) (community draft).
- Industrial research: hardest IEC 62443-4-1 practices were security management, secure-by-design and security update management; industrial devices ship full firmware images with intermittent connectivity, making automatic updates with opt-out difficult; firms recommended DevSecOps pipelines, CycloneDX SBOMs, CVSS-based scoring and prepared reporting templates — [arXiv 2505.14325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14325).
- Vendor pitfall: generating the SBOM from the build manifest rather than the shipped binary missed an outdated embedded web server (CVE-2002-1819) in a consumer camera teardown; recommends binary SCA and reachability analysis as release-gate inputs, and checking artefacts pre-release for supply-chain compromise — [Finite State, 13 Aug 2026](https://finitestate.io/blog/cra-compliance-mistakes) [vendor].
- ONEKEY survey (200 German industrial firms, Sep 2026): >60% expect development of new/updated products to take longer under the CRA, 28% "significantly longer" — [ONEKEY press release 9 Sep 2026](https://www.onekey.com/press-release/onkey-iot-ot-cybersecurity-report-2026-half-of-all-companies-have-formed-cra-teams) [vendor survey].

### SDLC change list (deliverable b) — synthesised from the cited findings
1. **Product scoping gate**: define product boundary incl. RDPS; classify (default/important I/II/critical) using IR 2025/2392; record rationale; re-check on each release — [C(2026) 5252 §6, §8](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456); [OmniTrust #2](https://omnitrust.com/blog/eu-cra-tales-from-the-trenches-2-the-first-problem-isnt-security-its-scope/) [consultancy].

3. **Annex I Part I applicability matrix** as a design requirement source (controls traced to requirements) — [Commission FAQ §4.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
4. **Component intake / due-diligence gate** for third-party and OSS components (needs defined, evidence or tests, EUVD check, maintenance status) — [C(2026) 5252 §7.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
5. **Build-time SBOM generation** per release, stored with release artefacts, signed; known-unknowns declared — [ENISA SBOM advisory §4.1](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Software%20Bill%20of%20Materials%20-%20v1.10.pdf).
6. **Security testing cadence**: SAST per release candidate, DAST/fuzzing, pentest/security review; regression and new tests when threat inputs change (not fixed-interval repetition) — [C(2026) 5252 §9.2.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456); [OpenChain checklist](https://github.com/OpenChain-Project/CRA-Compliance/blob/main/CRA_Checklist_Requirement_latest.md).
7. **Release gate record**: no known exploitable vulnerabilities decision (severity/exploitability/impact), test evidence, SBOM hash, secure-default config check, substantial-modification ("security delta") assessment, documentation delta — [C(2026) 5252 §9.2.2, §4.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456); [SCHUTZWERK](https://www.schutzwerk.com/en/blog/cra-guidance-2026/) [vendor].
8. **Update pipeline**: security updates released separately from feature updates where technically feasible; signed with role-separated keys; anti-rollback; automatic security updates with user opt-out where appropriate; outcome recorded; recovery path — [Commission FAQ §4.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307); [ENISA update advisory](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-05/Draft%20-%20ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Update%20Mechanisms%20-%20v0.6.pdf).
9. **Support-period policy**: declared per product and per substantially modified software version; end date shown at purchase; EoS notification; Art 13(10) latest-version-only remediation only where free upgrade without additional costs — [C(2026) 5252 §5](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
10. **Post-market loop**: continuous SBOM-driven vulnerability monitoring feeding PSIRT; risk assessment update triggers (security-relevant update, new vulnerability, new threat); advisory publication after fix — [BSI TR-03183-1 §5.13](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-1_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).
11. **Machine-processable evidence**: attestations (signed build logs, test results, SBOM) generated in pipeline to keep Annex VII documentation current — [ENISA playbook §5.1](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf).

### Inferences
- Teams already certified to IEC 62443-4-1 or running SSDF/SAMM programmes need to add, not replace: the Annex I applicability matrix, SBOM depth/quality per TR-03183-2, the "security delta"/substantial-modification check per release, declared support periods, and the Art 14 clock.
- "Separate security from functionality updates" is best implemented as a branching/release policy (maintenance branches per supported version + hotfix channel), with the exceptions documented per the Commission's examples.

### Gaps
- No authority document found that prescribes a security-testing frequency; the Commission explicitly declines to.
- No dedicated TUF/Uptane CRA implementation case studies were located before the search budget ran out; the ENISA advisory is still a v0.1 draft.
- No evidence found of how notified bodies will assess the "technically feasible" separation of updates.

## Key Question 5 — SBOM, VEX and CSAF tooling and practice; CVD intake tooling; PSIRT practices; publishing to EUVD/CVE/CSAF

### Takeaway
Practice has coalesced on: CycloneDX ≥1.6 or SPDX ≥3.0.1 (BSI), generated at build with Syft/Trivy/cdxgen or build-system plugins (ENISA lists these), quality-checked (sbomqs incl. a BSI TR-03183-2 check), signed/attested (Sigstore/cosign, GitHub attestations), uploaded to Dependency-Track or similar for continuous monitoring against NVD/GHSA/OSV, with vulnerability status communicated separately in VEX/CSAF (BSI forbids embedding vulnerability data in the SBOM). CVD intake follows RFC 9116 security.txt + anonymous web form + PSIRT/CSIRT functional mailboxes (TR-03183-3), with response SLAs (5/10 working days per BSI; ≤7 days acknowledgement in draft EN 40000-1-3) and 90-day disclosure defaulting to EUVD. Depth: the CRA minimum is top-level dependencies; BSI requires recursion to the first component outside the delivery scope; CISA 2025/2026 elements add hash, license, tool name and generation context.

### Cited Findings

**SBOM content, depth and formats**
- BSI TR-03183-2 required SBOM-level fields: creator (email or URL), timestamp (UTC recommended); additional if existing: SBOM-URI. Required per component: creator, name (else filename), version (SemVer/CalVer recommended; else file modification date-time), filename, dependencies (with completeness clearly indicated), distribution licences, SHA-512 hash of the deployable component, executable property, archive property, structured property. Additional if existing: source code URI, URI of deployable form, other identifiers (CPE, purl), original licences. Optional: effective licence, source-code hash, security.txt URL — [BSI TR-03183-2 v2.1.0 §5.2](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-2_v2_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6).
- BSI depth rule: recursive dependency resolution at least for each component in the scope of delivery, down each path "at least up to and including the first component that is outside the scope of delivery" (a "delivery item SBOM"); multiple instances with different metadata must be listed separately; SBOMs of used components may be referenced instead of merged only if themselves compliant; the SBOM must contain the information available during the build — [BSI TR-03183-2 §5.1](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-2_v2_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6).
- BSI: vulnerability information "is not contained in an SBOM" because it changes over time while SBOM data is static; the recommended distribution format for vulnerability information is CSAF, "including also VEX as a profile" — [BSI TR-03183-2 §8.1.14](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-2_v2_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6).
- CISA "2025 Minimum Elements for SBOM" (August 2025 public-comment draft): 11 data fields — SBOM Author, Software Producer, Component Name, Component Version, Software Identifiers (incl. OmniBOR), Component Hash, License, Dependency Relationship (pedigree for backports/forks), Tool Name, Timestamp (ISO 8601), Generation Context; "Depth" replaced by "Coverage" (all components incl. transitive, no minimum depth); Known Unknowns must distinguish unknown from redacted; new vs NTIA 2021: Component Hash, License, Tool Name, Generation Context — [CISA 2025 SBOM Minimum Elements draft PDF](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/2025_CISA_SBOM_Minimum_Elements.pdf). A 2026 final version adds component hash algorithm, license, tool name and generation context and renames Supplier Name to Component Producer, referencing OMB M-26-05 (Jan 2026) — [DevOps.com](https://devops.com/cisas-2026-sbom-guidance-adds-hash-requirements-and-ai-coverage/) (secondary; exact date not given). ENISA's Oct 2026 advisory cites the "2026 Minimum Elements" and includes a BSI↔CISA field mapping table (e.g. BSI has SBOM-URI but no SBOM Version/Format/Signature/Tool Name; CISA has those) — [ENISA SBOM advisory §2.1.1](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Software%20Bill%20of%20Materials%20-%20v1.10.pdf).
- ENISA SBOM advisory: demand/supply gap — 36% need primary components + direct dependencies with declared known unknowns but only 29% receive them; 24% need full-depth SBOMs but 14% receive them; 27% need depth + known unknowns, 12% receive them — [ENISA SBOM advisory §2.2](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Software%20Bill%20of%20Materials%20-%20v1.10.pdf).
- OpenChain checklist: SBOM must cover at least top-level dependencies, with a documented depth decision tied to the ability to make a 24-hour exploited-CVE determination; cites BSI TR-03183-2 for implementation — [OpenChain CRA checklist §3.1](https://github.com/OpenChain-Project/CRA-Compliance/blob/main/CRA_Checklist_Requirement_latest.md).

**SBOM tooling and pipeline**
- ENISA's open-source tooling landscape table: generation — cyclonedx-maven-plugin, @cyclonedx/cyclonedx-npm, cyclonedx-gomod, CycloneDX dotnet tool, Syft, Trivy; SPDX — spdx-gradle-plugin, spdx-maven-plugin, spdx/tools-python/java/golang, Linux "make spdx", Zephyr "west spdx"; validation — CycloneDX CLI, tools.spdx.org validate/ntia_checker; quality — sbomqs, sbom-scorecard (eBay); signing — GitHub CLI attestation, Cosign/Sigstore; storage — actions/upload-artifact; vulnerability management — Grype, Trivy, OWASP Dependency Check, OWASP Dependency-Track, GitHub Dependency Review, OWASP dep-scan, Fossology, sbomify, sbom-cve-check — [ENISA SBOM advisory §2.2.3](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Software%20Bill%20of%20Materials%20-%20v1.10.pdf).
- ENISA recommended patterns: build-time integration immediately after compilation (capture direct + transitive while build context exists; Yocto, Kubernetes, Linux, Zephyr have built-in generation); event-triggered generation via webhooks; dedicated SBOM directory, naming conventions, pipeline templates, validation checkpoints (format, licence policy, signatures), vulnerability checks linked to remediation timelines/severity thresholds/escalation; retain generation logs, validation results, signatures, timestamps, release links; document known gaps/exceptions/"known unknowns" — [ENISA SBOM advisory §4.1](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Software%20Bill%20of%20Materials%20-%20v1.10.pdf).
- Engineering guide [vendor/independent blog]: trigger SBOM on tags/releases after image build; favour CycloneDX 1.6 natively (avoid converting); BSI fields for executable/archive/structured and filename are not emitted by default — post-process with BSI's CycloneDX taxonomy then validate; run sbomqs (has a TR-03183-2 check) before signing; sign with GitHub Artifact Attestations (Sigstore keyless) bound to the image digest; upload to Dependency-Track (polls NVD, GHSA, OSV, Snyk) and emit SARIF to GitHub Security; start VEX with OpenVEX (vexctl) then move to CSAF; pin third-party Actions to commit SHAs after the March 2026 Trivy Actions compromise — [CRA Decoded, updated 24 Oct 2026](https://cra-decoded.com/blog/posts/005_cra_sbom_pipeline/) [vendor-adjacent].
- OpenSSF practitioner guidance (10 Sep 2026): generate SBOMs during the build (post-build SBOMs are less accurate), use VEX (OpenVEX) to determine affectedness, SLSA provenance, OpenSSF Scorecard and Best Practices Badge for dependency selection; OSPS Baseline covers only ~35% of what the CRA asks of manufacturers; "CRA Baselines" for manufacturers and for OSS consumption (12 requirements) via ORBIT Launchpad — [OpenSSF Tech Talk recap](https://openssf.org/?p=11738).
- Linux Foundation 2026 report (843 respondents): only 32% produce SBOMs for all products; 51% passively rely on upstream projects for security fixes (up from 46%); CVEs published in Q1 2026 up 394% YoY with high-severity up 811% across 12,000+ LFX-indexed projects — [LF 2026 CRA Awareness and Readiness report](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/hubfs/Research%20Reports/lfr_2026_cra_awareness_report_060826.pdf?hsLang=en); [LF blog, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/blog/the-cra-readiness-reality-what-changed-and-what-didnt-between-2025-and-2026).

**VEX / CSAF**
- BSI recommends CSAF (with VEX profile) for vulnerability information and lists "CSAF Provider" in TR-03183-3's references; security.txt should carry a `CSAF:` field pointing to the provider-metadata — [BSI TR-03183-2 §8.1.14](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-2_v2_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6); [BSI TR-03183-3 §4.2.8](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-3_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4).
- CycloneDX 1.7 natively supports VEX metadata and CSAF linkage per ENISA's format comparison table — [ENISA SBOM advisory Table 2](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/ENISA%20Technical%20Advisory%20-%20Software%20Bill%20of%20Materials%20-%20v1.10.pdf).
- Commission guidance: a third-party component vulnerability that "cannot be exploited in its product (e.g. because the vulnerable code is not reachable)" is not an actively exploited vulnerability for that manufacturer — the legal hook for "not_affected" VEX statements — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §9.1 para 218](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Vendor pitfall: raw CVE counts without reachability context are not a risk score; apply reachability (code present in shipped build, execution can reach it, externally triggerable) — [Finite State, 13 Aug 2026](https://finitestate.io/blog/cra-compliance-mistakes) [vendor].
- (CSAF 2.0 VEX profile justification labels could not be extracted from the OASIS page via fetch — see Gaps.)

**CVD intake, PSIRT and disclosure**
- BSI TR-03183-3 MUSTs: public website with security info without login; `/.well-known/security.txt` per RFC 9116 over HTTPS, canonical URI, contacts (first PSIRT mailbox, second CSIRT mailbox, then web form URI), OpenPGP keys, preferred languages incl. English, policy link, optional `CSAF:` field, expiry ≤1 year and quarterly checks, OpenPGP signature, crawler-visible — [BSI TR-03183-3 §4.1–4.2](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-3_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4).
- BSI roles: at least two roles — PSIRT (products/services) and CSIRT (own infrastructure) — not assigned to a single person unless microenterprise; functional mailboxes (prefixes "psirt"/"csirt" recommended) with dedicated OpenPGP keys (validity ≤5 years), authorisations and tasks defined, English processing, daily automated mailbox reachability tests recommended — [BSI TR-03183-3 §4.3.1](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-3_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4).
- BSI web form: must allow anonymous submission, no third-party components/tracking, minimised logging, English version, structured guidance, availability monitoring; a central vulnerability-reporting web page linked from the home page — [BSI TR-03183-3 §4.3.3–4.5](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-3_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4).
- BSI CVD policy: reviewed at least yearly; names the corresponding national CSIRT (per Art 14(7), must stay with it) and commits to notify it without undue delay; assurances (confidentiality, no disclosure of reporter data, no criminal charges, no NDA requirement); non-automated simple response within 5 working days and detailed feedback within 10 working days (confirm/reject, meaningful queries, or explanation plus commitment to update within 10 working days); reports cannot be closed by a single analyst; validated vulnerabilities publicly disclosed within 90 days, extendable once by 90 days in consultation with the national CSIRT, published at least on EUVD — [BSI TR-03183-3 §4.4](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-3_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4).
- ENISA Oct 2026 kit: CVD policy template sections — introduction/commitment, scope, how to report, rules for responsible testing, required information, what to expect, safe harbour, coordinated disclosure timeline (default embargo "[x] days"; critical vulnerabilities disclosed after remediation; accelerated process if actively exploited), recognition, data protection; footnote: draft prEN 40000-1-3 sets a maximum of 7 calendar days for acknowledgement of receipt; advisory template: affected products/versions, summary, remediation, timeline, credit; "common mistakes" include not checking every report for active exploitation (missing the 24-hour early warning) and not forwarding third-party component issues upstream (Art 13(6)) — [ENISA security.txt/CVD advisory](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/Technical%20Advisory%20on%20security.txt%20CVD%20Policies%20and%20Security%20Advisories%20Public%20Consultation.pdf).

- Commission guidance §9.1 user information (Art 14(8)) is risk-based and proportionate — detailed information may be limited to affected customers until a fix exists; Annex I Part II(4) requires public disclosure of fixed vulnerabilities once an update is available — [C(2026) 5252 Annex paras 219–221](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- FIRST PSIRT Services Framework v1.1: service areas 1 Stakeholder Ecosystem Management (1.1–1.7 incl. finder engagement, downstream stakeholders, metrics), 2 Vulnerability Discovery (incl. 2.1 intake with on-call/follow-the-sun guidance), 3 Vulnerability Triage and Analysis (3.1 qualification, 3.2 established finders, 3.3 reproduction — dedicated full/part-time resource recommended), plus Remediation, Disclosure, Training areas; organisational models distributed/centralised/hybrid; operational foundations (charter, executive sponsorship, budget, staffing, tools, policies) — [FIRST PSIRT Services Framework v1.1](https://www.first.org/standards/frameworks/psirts/psirt_services_framework_v1.1).
- Vendor "Minimum Viable PSIRT" (CVD Portal, updated 21 Aug 2026): four functional roles — Triage Coordinator, Vulnerability Analyst/Product Champion, Remediation Lead, Incident Commander (declares active exploitation; CTO/VP Eng/CISO level); example SLAs: Critical triage 4h/plan 24h/patch <7 days; High triage 24h/patch next sprint <30 days; Medium/Low triage 48h/patch 90 days; "Active Exploitation" protocol suspends normal SLAs; move to CSAF 2.0 advisories; dedicated intake portal rather than support ticketing — [CVD Portal blog](https://cvdportal.com/blog/building-psirt-from-scratch) [vendor].

### Inferences
- A CRA-grade SBOM pipeline = build-time generation → enrichment to BSI fields → validation (schema + sbomqs/TR-03183-2) → signing/attestation → storage with release → Dependency-Track/OSV monitoring → VEX/CSAF for affectedness → advisory publication; the ENISA advisory and vendor guides describe essentially the same chain.
- Because BSI forbids vulnerability data inside SBOMs and the Commission ties reportability to reachability, a VEX/CSAF "not_affected with justification" record is simultaneously an engineering artefact and legal evidence that no Art 14 report was due.

### Gaps
- CSAF 2.0 §4.5 VEX profile text (product_status categories and `flags` justification labels) and §7.2 provider roles could not be extracted from the OASIS HTML via the fetch tool; cite the OASIS spec directly if needed.
- GUAC, HackerOne/Intigriti/YesWeHack CRA-specific features, and EUVD/CVE-publishing workflows (ENISA as CVE Root) were not researched before the search budget expired.
- No independent benchmark of SBOM tool accuracy against TR-03183-2 was found.

## Key Question 6 — Reporting readiness: 24/7 triage for the 24-hour early warning, "actively exploited" decision criteria, templates, dry runs, ENISA SRP registration

### Takeaway
Since 11 Sep 2026 manufacturers must notify AEVs and severe incidents via ENISA's SRP (web only, English only, no API, no sandbox) within 24h/72h/14 days (AEV) or one month (incident) of "awareness", which the Commission defines as the point after an immediate initial assessment where the manufacturer has a "reasonable degree of certainty". Readiness practice: register Primary + Secondary Assigned Representatives (EU Login + MFA) with the correct CSIRT coordinator; define awareness/escalation triggers in the PSIRT runbook; record UTC timestamps for observation/awareness/escalation/submission; pre-draft field content for each stage; run tabletop exercises (one AEV, one severe incident) without submitting synthetic reports; test weekend coverage; the SRP's current 72-hour counter quirk and the 20-notification cap before CSIRT validation are known platform limitations.

### Cited Findings
- SRP operational 11 Sep 2026 at portal.cra-srp.enisa.europa.eu; Assigned Representatives (ARs) need personal EU Login with MFA; one Primary AR and up to 20 Secondary ARs per manufacturer; the CSIRT designated as coordinator (CDaC) validates the association but validation runs in parallel — until mandatory, an AR may submit up to 20 notifications; drafts are stored only in the individual AR's account; web interface only, no API at launch ("may come in a later phase"); English only; only mandatory notifications (AEVs, SIs) — voluntary reporting later; the 72-hour counter currently starts 48 hours after the early warning (ENISA plans a fix); wrong CDaC choice "may invalidate the notification"; exploitation known before 11 Sep 2026 need not be reported retrospectively; FAQ last updated 15 Oct 2026 — [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/vulnerability-services/eu-incident-response-and-cyber-crisis-management/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions).
- ENISA SRP document set: AR User Registration guide (10 Sep 2026), AR Notification Submission and Update (9 Sep 2026), AR Interface Functions (9 Sep 2026), PEC guidance (10 Sep 2026), AR User Manual (v1.2, 21 Oct 2026 per press page), SRP Glossary, Factsheet v1.0, Terms and Conditions v1.0; platform upgraded to v1.2.1 on 20 Oct 2026 (changes how Secondary ARs/Backups are invited) — [ENISA SRP page](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/vulnerability-services/eu-incident-response-and-cyber-crisis-management/single-reporting-platform-srp); [ENISA press release 11 Sep 2026](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/node/19455).
- Independent step list (31 Jul–10 Sep 2026 ENISA guidance): create EU Login + MFA → select AR role → choose designated CSIRT → accept legal agreement → confirm personal details → enter manufacturer details → await CDaC validation; Secondary AR invitation links expire after 7 days; early-warning mandatory fields: notification type and level, title and summary, manufacturer name, product name and version, date/time of awareness, and (incidents) whether unlawful/malicious acts are suspected; 72-hour stage adds nature of vulnerability/exploit and corrective measures; final adds full description, severity, impact, update details; submitted notifications cannot be edited; no sandbox — rehearse on paper — [cyberresilienceact.eu SRP step-by-step](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/enisa-srp-step-by-step-instructions-31-july-2026.html) (independent).
- Commission guidance on awareness: assess suspicious events "immediately"; the manufacturer is aware when, after initial assessment, it has "a reasonable degree of certainty" that an AEV exists or a severe incident compromised product security; aligned with recital 31 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690 (NIS2) and EDPB Guidelines 9/2022 (GDPR breach notification); notifications are updated progressively — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §9.1, paras 211–215](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Commission FAQ on triggers: zero-days are reportable only with reliable evidence of malicious exploitation; vulnerabilities found by ethical hackers/bug bounty or by a test lab with no evidence of exploitation are not AEVs (voluntary under Art 15); awareness channels include customers/partners, threat-intel reports, government agencies, researchers, telemetry/honeypots, dark-web monitoring — monitoring these is not itself mandated; component AEVs must be reported by both the integrating manufacturer and the component manufacturer; non-exploitable-in-product component vulnerabilities are not reportable — [Commission FAQ §5](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Reporting applies to all in-scope products incl. those placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027 (Art 69(3)); for legacy products the manufacturer must notify but is not bound by other vulnerability-handling obligations; Art 14(8) user information still applies, and CSIRTs may inform users if the manufacturer does not — [Commission FAQ §5.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Irish NCSC guidelines: early warning must not wait for detailed analysis; the 72-hour detailed notification is due within 48 hours of the early warning if one was submitted; no pre-registration is needed (registration at first early warning); email is not a formal channel unless ENISA announces SRP unavailability — [NCSC Ireland National CRA Guidelines](https://www.ncsc.gov.ie/pdfs/National_CRA_Guidelines_v1.pdf).
- BSI counter-examples (reported from an 8 Sep 2026 BSI webinar): a published exploit without observed exploitation is not an AEV; a customer's ransomware incident is not automatically a severe incident; for third-party components ask: is the code contained, reachable, and actually exploited in this product — [SCHUTZWERK, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.schutzwerk.com/en/blog/cra-guidance-2026/) [vendor; second-hand].
- SCHUTZWERK PSIRT case-file fields: intake/source, product/version/support status, component analysis, affected functionality and reachability, exploitation/incident evidence, timestamp of "reasonable certainty", remediation decision, user information, CRA/NIS2 routing — [SCHUTZWERK](https://www.schutzwerk.com/en/blog/cra-guidance-2026/) [vendor].
- Wavect operational playbook (24 Aug 2026, reviewed 5 Oct 2026): record separate UTC timestamps for first observation, organisational awareness, internal escalation and each submission; incident lead + deputy; named owners for open unknowns; two tabletop exercises (AEV and severe incident); never submit synthetic incidents to the live channel; test weekend coverage, loss of platform access, supplier delays, receipt retention; pitfalls: postponing awareness pending approval/CVE, treating 24h and 72h as consecutive, applying the 14-day rule to incidents, treating scanner findings as AEVs, assuming remediation pauses the clock — [Wavect playbook](https://wavect.io/blog/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-playbook-2026/) [vendor].
- ONEKEY (15 Sep 2026): designate primary and deputy compliance officers with EU Login; pre-draft 24h/72h templates (24h: suspected malicious intent, cross-border impact; 72h: impact assessment, CVSS, interim mitigations); no API, so a human completes submission; run tabletop dry-runs — [ONEKEY blog](https://www.onekey.com/resource/how-should-manufacturers-prepare-for-cra-vulnerability-and-incident-reporting-through-the-enisa-single-reporting-platform-and-can-it-currently-be-automated) [vendor].
- Clarysec (9 Jun 2026): triage record should capture active-exploitation status, affected product/version, SBOM dependency match, severe-incident indicator and regulatory decision; document "not reportable" outcomes with the same rigour; tabletop with a simulated actively exploited dependency vulnerability involving engineering, security, legal, privacy, product, comms, supplier management and customer-facing staff — [Clarysec blog](https://blog.clarysec.com/posts/eu-cra-2026-vulnerability-reporting-readiness/) [vendor].
- OpenChain checklist §4.5 RACI: Notification Owner (24h, with named backup and on-call schedule), Full Notification Owner (72h, escalation to legal/exec documented), Final Report Owner (14 days/1 month, signs off post-incident review); confirm SRP mechanics with ENISA/national CSIRT; keep an awareness-timestamp procedure and incident log; re-confirm RACI annually and on personnel change — [OpenChain CRA checklist §4.5](https://github.com/OpenChain-Project/CRA-Compliance/blob/main/CRA_Checklist_Requirement_latest.md).
- Microenterprises and small enterprises are exempt from fines for missing the 24-hour early warning deadline; OSS stewards are exempt from fines — [LF 2026 report (citing CRA)](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/hubfs/Research%20Reports/lfr_2026_cra_awareness_report_060826.pdf?hsLang=en).
- BSI TR-03183-3 recommends daily automated reachability tests of PSIRT/CSIRT mailboxes and web-form availability monitoring, and sufficient resources "to compensate for any absences" — [BSI TR-03183-3 §4.3.1(m),(q)](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-3_v1_0_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4).

### Inferences
- "24/7" in practice means an on-call PSIRT lead + deputy with authority to declare awareness and submit the early warning without legal sign-off, since the Commission's "immediately assess" plus 24-hour window leaves no room for committee approval; legal review belongs at the 72-hour stage.
- Because the SRP has no API, no sandbox and stores drafts per AR account, organisations should keep their own authoritative case record and treat the SRP as a submission surface; the "awareness timestamp" is the single most important piece of evidence to log.

### Gaps
- No official template for the SRP fields beyond the glossary/user manual (not downloaded); the manual's field-by-field content was not extracted.
- No published statistics on notifications received since 11 Sep 2026.
- The Irish guidance says the 72-hour notification is due 48 hours after an early warning, while ENISA's FAQ describes the 48-hour-offset counter as a platform bug to be fixed — the two positions were not reconciled in any source found.

## Key Question 7 — Programme structure: roles, RACI, governance, cost/effort benchmarks, pitfalls, sequencing to 11 December 2027

### Takeaway
Evidence on cost and effort is thin and mostly vendor- or survey-based: the 2024 industrial survey found 50% expected <20% cost increase; ONEKEY's 2026 survey found 61% budgeting and only 18% expecting no external help; an independent calculator gives ~€78k one-off/€13.5k annual for three default-class products at medium maturity (illustrative); unsourced vendor ranges put Module B at €15k–80k. Roles that recur across BSI, OpenChain and vendor material: accountable CRA programme owner, product security/compliance lead, PSIRT (distinct from infrastructure CSIRT), Notification/Full-Notification/Final-Report owners for Art 14, SBOM owner per released product, classification/scope owner, legacy-version owner, and SRP Assigned Representatives. Pitfalls reported by early adopters: scope undercount (5x+), mature-but-unprovable security, evidence scattered across tools, CVD policies nobody staffs, manifest-based SBOMs, treating certifications as CRA compliance, planning only for Dec 2027.

**Pitfalls and early-adopter lessons**
- Scope undercount: detailed inventories often show "a 5x or larger increase" in products in scope (versions, OEM-branded, cloud, mobile apps); legacy documentation may need recreating; acquisitions produce inconsistent classifications; classification treated as one-time; one security model for all products; ignoring supplier components; mis-tiering in either direction — [OmniTrust #2](https://omnitrust.com/blog/eu-cra-tales-from-the-trenches-2-the-first-problem-isnt-security-its-scope/) [consultancy].
- "Being secure and being CRA-ready are not the same thing": mature programmes could not prove controls consistently; evidence scattered across ticketing, SharePoint, spreadsheets, test platforms and email; existing certifications assumed to satisfy the CRA; post-market duties planned late — [OmniTrust #3](https://omnitrust.com/blog/eu-cra-tales-from-the-trenches-3-why-security-maturity-is-not-enough-for-cra-readiness/) [consultancy].
- Five mistakes: planning only around Dec 2027; manifest-based SBOMs; CVE count as a score; assuming compromise starts after shipment; publishing a disclosure policy nobody staffs — [Finite State](https://finitestate.io/blog/cra-compliance-mistakes) [vendor].
- Reporting-channel without internal routing; SBOM without ownership; ISMS scope excluding firmware/build pipelines/update infrastructure; supplier contracts lacking vulnerability clauses; incident plans without product logic — [Clarysec](https://blog.clarysec.com/posts/eu-cra-2026-vulnerability-reporting-readiness/) [vendor].
- Industrial research pitfalls: transition period encouraging delay; notified-body and sector-standard uncertainty; CRA seen only as burden; tools cannot fix governance gaps; staff shortages — [arXiv 2505.14325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14325).
- ENISA "common mistakes" for CVD/security.txt/advisories (wrong file location, inaccessible file, expired contacts, no owner, assuming the file is enough, no acknowledgement, no prioritisation, no records, not checking for active exploitation, not reporting upstream, advisories without affected versions or fix instructions) — [ENISA security.txt/CVD advisory §2](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/Technical%20Advisory%20on%20security.txt%20CVD%20Policies%20and%20Security%20Advisories%20Public%20Consultation.pdf).
- VDMA positions: warns against over-broad reading of "substantial modification" for OEM-made modifications and against excessive component testing ("over-engineering"); publishes a supplier self-disclosure guideline (Jan 2026) and a vulnerability handling guideline — [VDMA CRA page](https://vdma.eu/en/cyber-resilience-act).

## Key Question 8 — Software-only products: CE marking placement, publishing the DoC, delivering Annex II information

### Takeaway
For software, the CE marking is affixed either to the EU declaration of conformity or on the website accompanying the software, in a section "easily and directly accessible to consumers" (Art 30(1)); the DoC may be full (Annex V) or simplified (Annex VI — a sentence with the URL of the full DoC) and must accompany the product; a single DoC may cover a product family and multiple EU acts; a new version "might need a new declaration", especially after a substantial modification. Annex II information must include intended purpose, security properties, support end date (month/year), vulnerability-reporting contact, and known/foreseeable circumstances (incl. misuse) leading to significant risk.

### Cited Findings
- CE marking must be visible, legible and indelible, generally >5 mm; "Software products also need to bear the CE marking"; per Art 30(1) it is affixed to the EU DoC or on the website accompanying the software, with the relevant website section easily and directly accessible to consumers; it cannot be affixed without a positive conformity assessment — [Commission FAQ §6.7](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- DoC: full form per Annex V template or simplified per Annex VI (a sentence incl. the internet address of the full DoC); must accompany the product; linked to the product not just the type but need not include a unique identifier; a new version "might need a new declaration of conformity, especially when it implements a substantial modification"; where several Union acts apply, a single DoC (possibly a dossier of individual DoCs) is required under Art 28(3) — [Commission FAQ §6.8](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- A single DoC may cover a product family if it clearly identifies the variants and they share risks/architecture — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §7.4 para 175](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Technical documentation may be in any language but must be provided in a language easily understood by a requesting market surveillance authority; it need not be given to customers or the public except for FOSS important products self-attesting under Art 32(5) — [Commission FAQ §6.6](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Annex II / Art 13(18): users must receive clear, understandable instructions for secure installation, operation and use, including assumptions/requirements for secure deployment; known or foreseeable circumstances incl. reasonably foreseeable misuse leading to significant cybersecurity risks must be stated (Annex II point 5); where instructions are relied on to treat risks they must be kept updated — [Commission FAQ §4.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).
- Support end date must be indicated at purchase at least by month and year, and an end-of-support notification displayed where technically feasible — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §5 para 127](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- OpenChain checklist user-facing documentation items: intended use, security properties, configuration, end-of-support date, vulnerability reporting; DoC kept current and retained 10 years; "CE or digital CE marking before market placement" — [OpenChain CRA checklist §7.3](https://github.com/OpenChain-Project/CRA-Compliance/blob/main/CRA_Checklist_Requirement_latest.md).
- Software support-period mechanics (per-version declaration; Art 13(10) latest-version remediation if free upgrade without additional costs) — [C(2026) 5252 Annex §5 paras 128–131](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456).
- Vendor "version and support register" record: per version end-of-support date, what was communicated at purchase, update channel, migration path, legacy-version owner — [SCHUTZWERK](https://www.schutzwerk.com/en/blog/cra-guidance-2026/) [vendor].

### Inferences
- A practical pattern for SaaS/downloadable software: a public "/compliance" or "/security" web section hosting the CE mark, the DoC (full, or simplified with link), the Annex II information, the support-period table per version, the CVD policy, security.txt pointers and advisories — satisfying Art 30(1), Annex VI, Annex II and TR-03183-3 in one place.
- Because each substantially modified software version is a new placing on the market with its own DoC/support period, release engineering must know which releases cross the "security delta" threshold.

### Gaps
- No authority example of a software CE-marking web page or DoC was found; Annex V/VI text itself was not quoted in the sources read.
- How app-store-distributed software should present the CE mark/DoC was not addressed in any source found.

## Deliverable (d) — Pitfalls list (consolidated; sources as cited above)
1. Planning only for 11 Dec 2027 while Art 14 reporting has applied since 11 Sep 2026 to all in-scope products incl. legacy ([Finite State](https://finitestate.io/blog/cra-compliance-mistakes) [vendor]; [Commission FAQ §5.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)).
2. Scope undercount (versions, OEM variants, cloud/RDPS, mobile apps) — often 5x+ ([OmniTrust #2](https://omnitrust.com/blog/eu-cra-tales-from-the-trenches-2-the-first-problem-isnt-security-its-scope/) [consultancy]).
3. Assuming IEC 62443/ISO 27001/EN 18031 certification equals CRA compliance; ISMS scope that excludes firmware, build pipelines and update infrastructure ([OmniTrust #3](https://omnitrust.com/blog/eu-cra-tales-from-the-trenches-3-why-security-maturity-is-not-enough-for-cra-readiness/); [Clarysec](https://blog.clarysec.com/posts/eu-cra-2026-vulnerability-reporting-readiness/) [vendor]).
4. Risk assessment without a decision trail; risk accepted on cost/appetite grounds, which the Commission says is irrelevant ([C(2026) 5252 §7.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)).
5. Manifest-derived SBOMs that miss shipped binaries; SBOMs without an owner; SBOMs embedding vulnerability data contrary to BSI ([Finite State](https://finitestate.io/blog/cra-compliance-mistakes) [vendor]; [TR-03183-2 §8.1.14](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Publications/TechGuidelines/TR03183/BSI-TR-03183-2_v2_1_0.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6)).
6. Treating CVE counts or scanner hits as risk or as "actively exploited"; no reachability/VEX discipline ([Finite State](https://finitestate.io/blog/cra-compliance-mistakes); [Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-playbook-2026/) [vendor]).
7. CVD policy/security.txt published but unstaffed, unmonitored, expired, or not routed to legal/PSIRT; no check of every report for active exploitation ([ENISA Oct 2026 kit](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/Technical%20Advisory%20on%20security.txt%20CVD%20Policies%20and%20Security%20Advisories%20Public%20Consultation.pdf); [Clarysec](https://blog.clarysec.com/posts/eu-cra-2026-vulnerability-reporting-readiness/) [vendor]).
8. Reporting-clock errors: postponing "awareness" pending approval or CVE publication; treating 24h/72h as consecutive; applying 14 days to incidents; assuming remediation pauses the clock; choosing the wrong CSIRT coordinator (may invalidate the notification) ([Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-playbook-2026/) [vendor]; [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/vulnerability-services/eu-incident-response-and-cyber-crisis-management/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)).
9. Not recording UTC awareness timestamps and submission receipts; relying on SRP drafts stored only in one AR's account ([Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-playbook-2026/) [vendor]; [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/vulnerability-services/eu-incident-response-and-cyber-crisis-management/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)).
10. Bundling security fixes with feature updates when separation is technically feasible; treating every feature release as (or never as) a substantial modification without a documented security-delta assessment ([Commission FAQ §4.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307); [C(2026) 5252 §4.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)).
11. Treating five years as the default support period; failing to declare per-version support for software; EoS not shown at purchase ([C(2026) 5252 §5](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)).
12. Evidence scattered across tools with manual audit-trail assembly; no product system of record ([OmniTrust #3](https://omnitrust.com/blog/eu-cra-tales-from-the-trenches-3-why-security-maturity-is-not-enough-for-cra-readiness/) [consultancy]).
13. Waiting for notified-body designation or hEN citation before preparing the technical file; no hedging across 2–3 candidate bodies ([cyberresilienceact.eu](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-notified-bodies-rules-apply-11-june-2026.html); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-cyber-resilience-act-getting-ready-for-cra-compliance-in-2026)).
14. Supplier contracts without vulnerability-notification, SBOM, and disclosure-coordination clauses; passive reliance on upstream (51%) ([Clarysec](https://blog.clarysec.com/posts/eu-cra-2026-vulnerability-reporting-readiness/) [vendor]; [LF 2026](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/hubfs/Research%20Reports/lfr_2026_cra_awareness_report_060826.pdf?hsLang=en)).
15. Mis-tiering products (too low → compliance risk for years; too high → unnecessary cost); one security model for all products ([OmniTrust #2](https://omnitrust.com/blog/eu-cra-tales-from-the-trenches-2-the-first-problem-isnt-security-its-scope/) [consultancy]).
