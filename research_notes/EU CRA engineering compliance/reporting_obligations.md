## Key Question 1 — Art 14 scope: definitions, triggers, "becomes aware", suspected exploitation, internal vs third-party discovery

### Takeaway
The duty bites on two triggers only: an "actively exploited vulnerability" (Art 3(42): reliable evidence of exploitation by a malicious actor without the system owner's permission) and a "severe incident having an impact on the security of the product" (Art 14(3), with the severity test in Art 14(5)). The Commission's 27 July 2026 guidance fixes the clock at the moment the manufacturer, after an immediate initial assessment, has "a reasonable degree of certainty" that the trigger is met; mere suspicion therefore starts the assessment, not the 24-hour clock, but the assessment must be prompt. Good-faith research findings and unexploited third-party component CVEs are not mandatory reports.

### Cited Findings
- Art 3(40) "vulnerability": "a weakness, susceptibility or flaw of a product with digital elements" exploitable by a cyber threat. Art 3(41) "exploitable vulnerability": one with potential to be effectively used by an adversary under practical operational conditions. Art 3(42) "actively exploited vulnerability": a vulnerability for which there is "reliable evidence that a malicious actor has exploited it" in a system without the owner's permission — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 3](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_3.html); confirmed verbatim in Commission FAQ 5.2 which quotes Art 3(42) — [Commission FAQ v1.4, PDF](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- Art 3(43) "incident" is defined by reference to NIS2 Art 6(6); Art 3(44) "incident having an impact on the security of the product with digital elements" is one that "negatively affects or is capable of negatively affecting the ability" of the product to protect availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of data or functions; Art 3(45) "near miss" refers to NIS2 Art 6(5); Art 3(46) "cyber threat" refers to Regulation 2019/881 Art 2(8); Art 3(51) "CSIRT designated as coordinator" refers to a CSIRT designated under NIS2 Art 12(1) — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 3](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_3.html)
- "Severe incident" is not a standalone Art 3 definition; the test is Art 14(5): an incident is severe where (a) it negatively affects or is capable of negatively affecting the product's ability to protect the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of "sensitive or important data or functions", or (b) it has led or is capable of leading to malicious code being introduced or executed in the product or in users' network and information systems — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/); [springlex Art 14](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-14/)
- Recital 68: actively exploited vulnerabilities are instances where the manufacturer establishes that a security breach affecting users resulted from a malicious actor using a flaw in its product; vulnerabilities discovered with no malicious intent for good-faith testing, investigation, correction or disclosure should not be subject to mandatory notification; a severe incident may concern the manufacturer's development, production or maintenance processes, e.g. malicious code inserted into a security update channel — [Commission FAQ v1.4, 5.2](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331); [springlex Art 14 linked recitals](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-14/)
- Commission FAQ 5.1: the CRA does not specify how a manufacturer must become aware and does not require it to monitor specific channels (footnote 9 notes Annex I Part II still requires a single point of contact and a CVD policy). Examples given: customer/partner informs of compromise with reliable evidence; threat-intelligence reports of a zero-day used in targeted attacks; a government agency notifies; ethical hackers report in-the-wild exploitation; internal telemetry, honeypots or dark-web monitoring — [Commission FAQ v1.4, 5.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- Commission FAQ 5.2: zero-days are reportable "when the manufacturer has reliable evidence" of malicious exploitation; a zero-day found by ethical hackers via a bug bounty, or by a test lab working for the manufacturer, with no evidence of exploitation, is not an actively exploited vulnerability and may be notified voluntarily under Art 15 — [Commission FAQ v1.4, 5.2](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- Commission Guidance C(2026) 5252, para 213: where a manufacturer detects a suspicious event or a third party (individual, customer, authority, media) brings a potential incident/vulnerability to its attention, it "should assess the suspicious event immediately"; it is regarded as aware when, after that initial assessment, it has "a reasonable degree of certainty" that (i) a vulnerability in its product is being actively exploited, or (ii) a severe incident has occurred and compromised product security — [Commission Guidance annex, PDF](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Guidance para 212: the "becoming aware" concept is deliberately aligned with recital 31 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690 (NIS2) and Section II(A) of EDPB Guidelines 9/2022 on GDPR breach notification, so one awareness test can serve NIS2, GDPR and CRA — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Guidance para 214: timing depends on the circumstances; some cases are clear from the outset, others take time to establish, but "the emphasis should be on prompt action" to perform the initial assessment, particularly where the vulnerability may pose significant risk — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Guidance para 217 and FAQ 5.3: no retroactive reporting of exploitation the manufacturer already knew about before 11 September 2026; but if a vulnerability was known pre-11 Sept and exploitation occurs or becomes known after that date, it becomes reportable — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456); [ENISA SRP FAQ 13](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)
- Guidance para 218 / FAQ 5.4 (third-party components): the product manufacturer must notify an actively exploited vulnerability originating from an integrated component; the component manufacturer must also notify if the component was placed on the market separately; but if the component vulnerability cannot be exploited in the product (e.g. vulnerable code not reachable) or has not been exploited in it, it is not an AEV for that manufacturer — voluntary Art 15 notification is possible, and Art 13(6) upstream reporting still applies — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456); [Commission FAQ v1.4, 5.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- ENISA SRP FAQ 5 describes AEVs as vulnerabilities "with reliable evidence of exploitation by a malicious actor" and SIs as incidents severely affecting a product's security per Art 14(5) — [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)
- BSI (Germany) states a ransomware incident at a manufacturer is not automatically a severe incident under the CRA; it must affect product security, e.g. by enabling manipulation of software, updates, signing keys or product functions — [BSI CRA-SRP page](https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Themen/Unternehmen-und-Organisationen/Informationen-und-Empfehlungen/Cyber_Resilience_Act/Single_Reporting_Platform-CRA/single_reporting_platform-cra.html) (as summarised; also reported by [Nexvyra, 12 Sept 2026](https://nexvyra.de/news/2026-09-12-cra-meldepflichten-anwendbar-cra-srp-enisa.html))
- Noerr (15 Sept 2026): CISA KEV, EUVD and CVE entries are "strong indicators" of active exploitation but do not replace a case-by-case assessment of whether the vulnerability is exploitable in the specific product — [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/cra-single-reporting-platform-launched-reporting-requirements-apply-with-immediate-effect)
- Reporting obligations continue after a product's support period ends (guidance para 210), unlike vulnerability-handling obligations — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

### Inferences
- "Suspected exploitation" is not itself a trigger. The runbook should therefore have two clocks: (1) an internal triage SLA starting at first signal (the guidance's "immediately"), and (2) the statutory 24h clock starting at the documented "reasonable degree of certainty" decision. The decision time and its evidence must be recorded because the SRP early-warning form requires a UTC "date and time became aware" field (see KQ2) and that timestamp is auditable.
- Source of discovery (internal telemetry vs third-party report) is irrelevant to the duty; what matters is whether reliable evidence of malicious exploitation exists. For third-party reports via CVD/bug bounty with no exploitation evidence, the duty is not triggered.
- Because the awareness test is intentionally harmonised with NIS2 and GDPR guidance, a single incident-assessment record can serve all three regimes.

### Gaps
- No authority has published a quantitative or evidential threshold for "reliable evidence" (e.g. whether a single credible third-party report suffices). ENISA's glossary does not define "actively exploited" (it only appears in field names).
- No published enforcement or MSA interpretation yet exists (MSAs' powers apply from 11 Dec 2027).

## Key Question 2 — Timelines and content of each stage (exact Art 14 paragraph references)

### Takeaway
Art 14(2) governs AEVs (early warning 24h; vulnerability notification 72h; final report no later than 14 days after a corrective/mitigating measure is available) and Art 14(4) governs severe incidents (early warning 24h; incident notification 72h; final report within one month after the 72h notification). The first two stages are "without undue delay and in any event within" the stated period of becoming aware. Art 14(6) allows the coordinating CSIRT to request intermediate status updates. ENISA's SRP glossary turns this into concrete mandatory/optional fields per stage.

### Cited Findings
**Statutory content (Art 14)**
- Art 14(2)(a) early warning (AEV): "without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours" of becoming aware, indicating, where applicable, the Member States on whose territory the manufacturer is aware the product has been made available — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/); [springlex Art 14](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-14/)
- Art 14(2)(b) vulnerability notification: unless already provided, within 72 hours of becoming aware; general information about the product concerned, the general nature of the exploit and the vulnerability, any corrective or mitigating measures taken, corrective or mitigating measures users can take, and, where applicable, how sensitive the manufacturer considers the notified information — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/)
- Art 14(2)(c) final report: unless already provided, "no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available"; description of the vulnerability including severity and impact, information on any malicious actor that has exploited or is exploiting it where available, and details of the security update or other corrective measures made available — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/)
- Art 14(4)(a) early warning (SI): within 24 hours, including at least whether the incident is suspected of being caused by unlawful or malicious acts, and, where applicable, the Member States where the product is available — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/)
- Art 14(4)(b) incident notification: within 72 hours; general information on the nature of the incident, an initial assessment, corrective/mitigating measures taken and that users can take, and where applicable the sensitivity assessment — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/)
- Art 14(4)(c) final report (SI): within one month after the incident notification under (b); a detailed description including severity and impact, the type of threat or root cause likely to have triggered it, and applied and ongoing mitigation measures — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/)
- Art 14(6): the CSIRT designated as coordinator that first receives the notification may request intermediate status updates where necessary — [springlex Art 14](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-14/)
- Guidance para 215 confirms the progressive-update structure and the same deadlines, and FAQ 7 of ENISA restates them — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456); [ENISA SRP FAQ 7](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)
- Art 64(10)(a): microenterprises and small enterprises are exempt from fines for missing the 24-hour early-warning deadlines of Art 14(2)(a) or 14(4)(a) — [springlex Art 64](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-64/)

**ENISA SRP field model (glossary, Sept 2026) — R = required, O = optional, M = mandatory, EW = early warning, FR = final report**
- Common fields: Notification type (R at EW); Title (R, max 255 chars); Summary (R, max 4000); Manufacturer name (system-generated); Member States where product available (R at EW — CDaC pre-populated, add others, keep updated); Product name and version (R); Product type/class/category (O, per Annexes III/IV); End-of-support indicator (O); Component name (O); Mitigating measure expected shortly (O, Y/N); User action able to reduce impact (O, max 4000); Considered sensitivity of information (O at EW and 72h); Corrective or mitigating measures taken (O at EW/72h, R at FR); Corrective or mitigating measures users can take (O at EW/72h, R at FR); Attack vector (O at 72h); AR Note and CSIRT Note (free text) — [ENISA SRP Glossary](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/cra-srp-glossary2)
- AEV-specific fields: CVE ID and EUVD ID (O, only if assigned); General information (O at EW, R at 72h); Date corrective/mitigating measure became available (R at FR); Details of the security update (R at FR); Description of severity and of impact (R at FR; "assessed severity rating, classification and rationale", no mandated scale); Date/time manufacturer became aware of AEV (M at EW, UTC, estimate allowed if labelled); Date/time AEV occurred (O); Malicious actor (R at FR if available; state "unknown" if unconfirmed, max 100 chars); PEC indicator, PEC Delay Reason and Further information (72h only) — [ENISA SRP Glossary](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/cra-srp-glossary2)
- SI-specific fields: Incident suspected of unlawful or malicious acts (R at EW: Yes/No/Unknown); General information on nature of incident (O at EW, R at 72h); Applied and ongoing mitigation (R at FR); Detailed severity and impact (R at FR); Type of threat or root cause (R at FR, mark preliminary if incomplete); Date/time became aware of incident (M at EW, UTC); Date/time incident occurred (O); Initial assessment (R at 72h) — [ENISA SRP Glossary](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/cra-srp-glossary2)
- Date/time fields use UTC in the form "2026-08-24 09:15 UTC"; estimates must be labelled — [ENISA SRP Glossary](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/cra-srp-glossary2)
- Platform counter quirk (ENISA FAQ 26): the 72-hour counter currently runs 48 hours after the early-warning submission, so a notification can show as overdue before 72h from awareness; a future release will compute from the "date/time became aware" field; the final-report counter exists only for SIs; counters are "visibility aids" not legal deadlines — [ENISA SRP FAQ 26](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)
- Ireland's NCSC frames the detailed notification as "within 48 hours of the early warning (72 hours from initial awareness)" — [NCSC-IE CRA page](https://www.ncsc.gov.ie/cra/)
- Skadden (3 Sept 2026) notes its own Annex 1 table reversed the final-report deadlines (14 days for incidents, one month for vulnerabilities) contrary to the body text; the Regulation is clear: 14 days for AEVs, one month for SIs — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/get-ready-reporting-faqs-and-checklist); [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/)

### Inferences
- Runbook timeline table (trigger → deadline → recipient → content):

- Because the AEV final report has no SRP counter and its trigger is the availability of a fix, the team must track patch-availability dates internally.

### Gaps
- No implementing act under Art 14(10) exists to legally fix the format; the ENISA glossary field set is the de facto standard (see KQ5).
- Whether the counter bug (FAQ 26) has been fixed since September could not be verified.

## Key Question 3 — Recipients and mechanics: single reporting platform, simultaneous submission, Member State determination, delayed dissemination, confidentiality

### Takeaway
Art 14(7) routes every notification through the ENISA-run single reporting platform (Art 16(1)) to the CSIRT designated as coordinator (CDaC) of the Member State of the manufacturer's main establishment, with simultaneous ENISA access; non-EU manufacturers follow a strict four-step fallback (authorised representative → importer → distributor → most users). The CDaC disseminates to other concerned CSIRTs and to its market surveillance authorities (Art 16(2)-(3)); it may delay dissemination on cybersecurity grounds under Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/881, and in "particularly exceptional circumstances" (Art 16(2) third subpara) ENISA receives only limited information for the 72h AEV notification.

### Cited Findings
- Art 14(1) and 14(3): the manufacturer notifies "simultaneously" the CSIRT designated as coordinator and ENISA via the single reporting platform (guidance para 209 restates) — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456); [springlex Art 14](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-14/)
- Art 14(7), first subpara: notifications are submitted via the SRP using one of the electronic notification end-points under Art 16(1), to the CDaC of the Member State where the manufacturer has its main establishment, and are "simultaneously accessible to ENISA" — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/)
- Art 14(7) main establishment: the Member State where decisions related to the cybersecurity of its products are predominantly taken; if that cannot be determined, the Member State where the establishment with the highest number of employees in the Union is located — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/); [ENISA SRP FAQ 18](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)
- Art 14(7) non-EU manufacturers (no main establishment in the Union): the CDaC of the first applicable Member State in this order: (a) where the authorised representative acting on behalf of the highest number of its products is established; (b) where the importer placing the highest number of its products on the market is established; (c) where the distributor making available the highest number of its products is established; (d) where the highest number of users of its products are located. Under (d), subsequent notifications may go to the same CSIRT first used — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/); [ENISA SRP FAQ 18](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)
- ENISA FAQ 8/18: the manufacturer selects the CDaC and is responsible for the correct choice; only one notification is needed per AEV/SI even with multiple EU branches or a non-EU parent; "a wrong CDaC selection may invalidate the notification", requiring resubmission — [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)
- Art 16(1): ENISA establishes and operates the SRP for Art 14(1) and (3) and Art 15(1) and (2) notifications; it is designed so that Member States and ENISA can operate their own electronic notification end-points — [springlex Art 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-16/)
- Art 16(2), first subpara: the CDaC first receiving the notification disseminates it "without undue delay" to the CDaCs of the Member States the manufacturer indicated the product was made available in — [springlex Art 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-16/)
- Art 16(2), second subpara: in exceptional circumstances (e.g. CVD process under NIS2 Art 12(1)) the receiving CSIRT may delay dissemination on justified cybersecurity-related grounds for no longer than strictly necessary, and must immediately inform ENISA of the delay, reasons and expected dissemination date — [springlex Art 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-16/)
- Art 16(2), third subpara (particularly exceptional circumstances): where the manufacturer indicates that (a) the vulnerability is actively exploited but exploitation is limited to the CDaC's Member State, (b) further dissemination would compromise essential national security or defence interests, or (c) further dissemination poses an imminent high cybersecurity risk that cannot yet be mitigated, ENISA receives only limited information initially: the fact of notification, general product information, the general nature of the exploit and the fact that security grounds were raised; ENISA may recommend full dissemination if it identifies a systemic risk to the internal market — [springlex Art 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-16/); conditions (a)-(c) also in [ENISA SRP Glossary, field v28](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/cra-srp-glossary2)
- Art 16(3): after receiving a notification, CDaCs provide their national market surveillance authorities with the information needed for their tasks — [springlex Art 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-16/)
- Art 16(4): ENISA must secure the platform and notify the CSIRTs network and Commission without undue delay of any security incident affecting it; Art 16(5): ENISA with the CSIRTs network sets the platform specifications and where no fix is available information is shared only on a need-to-know basis; Art 16(6): a CDaC learning of an AEV through CVD may delay dissemination until the parties consent, without preventing voluntary manufacturer notification — [springlex Art 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-16/)
- Art 15(5): CSIRTs and ENISA must ensure confidentiality and appropriate protection of information provided by notifiers (stated for voluntary reports) — [springlex Art 15](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-15/)
- Recital 71: manufacturers should indicate how sensitive they consider the information; the CSIRT uses this when deciding whether to delay dissemination, withhold the full notification from ENISA, and what mitigation to take — [EUR-Lex recitals via WebFetch](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/881 (C(2025) 8407), adopted 11 Dec 2025 under Art 14(9), published in the OJ 20 April 2026, in force 10 May 2026 — [EUR-Lex ELI](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/881/oj); [lovguiden.dk, 20 Apr 2026](https://lovguiden.dk/en/det-offentlige/eur-lex-eu-tidende/2026-04-20-eu-fastsaetter-regler-for-udskydelse-af-cybersikkerhedsnotifikationer-under-cra); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/39318-commission-delegated-regulation-eu-2026881-supplementing-the-cyber-resilience-act-on-notification-dissemination-delay-conditions-enters-into-force). Note: the Commission's own factpage (last updated 27 July 2026) still described it as "adopted, publication pending objection period" — [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation)
- Content of 2026/881: Art 3 (nature of information) — delay only where risks of dissemination outweigh benefits and cannot be managed via TLP/PAP handling, and at least one condition applies: (a) a fix/guidance "is expected to be made available within 72 hours" (if not, disseminate); (b) information sufficient to create an exploit, esp. for low-skill actors (disseminate once mitigation available); (c) CSIRT can share enough for others to mitigate (full dissemination once mitigation available); (d) CSIRT acts as trusted intermediary in a CVD (disseminate once no longer necessary and consent given). Art 4 — delay to a specific CSIRT that has suffered a confidentiality-affecting incident or has inadequate capabilities. Art 5 — delay where ENISA has notified under Art 16(4) that the platform's confidentiality is in doubt. Recital 7: no dissemination needed if the product is only sold in the initial CSIRT's Member State — [EUR-Lex C(2025) 8407 HTML](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=PI_COM:C(2025)8407)
- ENISA PEC guidance (updated 9 Sept 2026): PEC applies only to the 72-hour notification of an AEV; the reporter toggles the PEC indicator, selects a "PEC Delay Reason" and may add justification; the dissemination status becomes "72h Submitted under PEC"; the CDaC decides whether and when to share the full notification with ENISA and other CSIRTs manually — [ENISA PEC guidance](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/cra-srp-guidance-particular-exceptional-circumstances-pec)
- ENISA submission guidance (updated 12 Sept 2026): on early warning submission the CDaC, the manufacturer's ARs and ENISA are notified; concerned CSIRTs receive it only after the CDaC disseminates manually; on 72h submission ENISA is notified unless PEC is invoked; on update the CDaC, ENISA and previously-informed CSIRTs are notified — [ENISA AR notification submission guidance](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/cra-srp-guidance-ar-notification-submission-and-update)
- CRA Facts (9 Oct 2026) stresses the delay mechanism is the CSIRT's decision, not the manufacturer's, and does not extend any Art 14 deadline — [CRA Facts, 9 Oct 2026](https://cra-facts.com/blog/cra-reporting-first-weeks-srp-lessons-disclosure-delays)
- Hogan Lovells (11 Sept 2026): the CDaC passes information to market surveillance authorities and may inform the public, or require the manufacturer to do so, after consulting the manufacturer — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-cyber-resilience-act-vulnerability-and-incident-reporting-obligations-now-apply)

### Inferences
- For a UK/non-EU headquartered group with EU subsidiaries, the "main establishment" test (where cybersecurity decisions for the products are predominantly taken) may point to a non-EU location; if no EU establishment takes those decisions, the Art 14(7) fallback (authorised representative first) applies. The CDaC choice and its rationale should be documented before an incident because a wrong choice can invalidate the filing.
- The "Member States where product available" field is what drives dissemination; keeping a current distribution map per product is a prerequisite for a 24h filing.

### Gaps
- There is no published list of which CSIRTs have operated their own national "electronic notification end-points" under Art 16(1); at launch all submissions go through the ENISA web portal and BSI confirms national portals "may be connected later".
- No published cases of PEC or 2026/881 delays being invoked.

## Key Question 6 — User notification duty (Art 14(8)): form and expectations

### Takeaway
Art 14(8) requires the manufacturer, after becoming aware, to inform impacted users and, where appropriate, all users, "without undue delay" about the AEV/SI and about any risk-mitigating or corrective measures, where appropriate in a structured machine-readable format; if the manufacturer fails to do so in a timely manner, the notified CSIRTs may inform users where proportionate and necessary. The Commission's guidance makes the duty risk-based and proportionate: it does not require public disclosure of technical details, and initial disclosure may be limited to affected customers, with broader disclosure once mitigated.

### Cited Findings
- Art 14(8): after becoming aware, inform impacted users and, where appropriate, all users of the AEV or severe incident and, where necessary, of risk-mitigating and corrective measures they can deploy; "where appropriate, in a structured, machine-readable format" easily processed automatically; where the manufacturer fails to inform users in a timely manner, the notified CDaCs may do so when proportionate and necessary — [streamlex Art 14](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-14/); [springlex Art 14](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-14/)
- Recital 67: users should be informed, e.g. via the manufacturer's website or by direct contact where justified — [springlex Art 14 linked recitals](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-14/)
- Guidance para 219 restates the duty and the CSIRT backstop; para 220: the duty applies "in a risk-based and proportionate manner"; information need not be made public or disclosed indiscriminately; manufacturers may limit detailed disclosure to relevant users/customers, especially for products used in sensitive or essential environments where public technical detail could facilitate exploitation; para 221: once mitigated, broader disclosure may be appropriate, with proportionate detail and timing; Annex I Part II point (4) separately requires public disclosure of fixed vulnerabilities once a security update is available — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission FAQ 5.3 and 1.4: Art 14(8) applies also to legacy products placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027 — [Commission FAQ v1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- Skadden: no strict deadline or required format for customer notification; CSIRTs may notify users if the manufacturer has not done so "in a timely manner" — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/get-ready-reporting-faqs-and-checklist)
- AFME asks for alignment of user/client communications across DORA Art 14(1) and CRA Art 14(8) to avoid duplication — [AFME briefing, July 2026](https://www.afme.eu/our-work/digital-innovation/ensuring-appropriate-and-workable-cyber-and-operational-resilience-requirement/guidance-on-the-interplay-between-dora-and-the-cyber-resilience-act/)

### Inferences
- A practical form: a customer-facing security advisory (targeted to affected users first) plus a machine-readable CSAF/VEX document where the product supports automated consumption; public advisory once a fix is available. The SRP fields "corrective or mitigating measures users can take" and "user action able to reduce impact" can be re-used as the advisory content.

### Gaps
- No authority has specified what "machine-readable" format is expected (CSAF is an obvious candidate but is not named in any primary source found).

## Key Question 7 — Voluntary reporting (Art 15), importers/distributors (Arts 19-20), open-source stewards (Art 24)

### Takeaway
Art 15 allows manufacturers and any natural or legal person to notify vulnerabilities, cyber threats, incidents and near misses voluntarily to a CDaC or ENISA; CDaCs may prioritise mandatory over voluntary notifications and must inform the manufacturer when a third party reports an AEV/SI; voluntary reporting cannot create new obligations. Voluntary reporting is not yet available in the SRP. Importers (Art 19(5)) and distributors (Art 20(4)) must inform the manufacturer without undue delay on becoming aware of a vulnerability, and MSAs immediately if there is a significant cybersecurity risk. Open-source stewards are subject to Art 14(1), (3) and (8) under Art 24(3) only from 11 December 2027 and only to the extent they are involved in developing the product.

### Cited Findings
- Art 15(1): manufacturers and other natural or legal persons may notify voluntarily any vulnerability in a product and cyber threats that could affect its risk profile to a CDaC or ENISA; Art 15(2): same for incidents having an impact on product security and near misses; Art 15(3): processed under Art 16; the CDaC may prioritise mandatory notifications; Art 15(4): where a person other than the manufacturer notifies an AEV or SI, the CDaC "shall without undue delay inform the manufacturer"; Art 15(5): confidentiality and no additional obligations from voluntary reporting (subject to criminal law) — [springlex Art 15](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-15/)
- Recital 74: manufacturers and other parties may voluntarily report vulnerabilities, cyber threats, incidents and near misses to a CSIRT or ENISA — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- ENISA FAQ 4, 6, 27: voluntary notifications under Art 15 are planned for a future SRP phase; only mandatory AEV/SI can be submitted now — [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions)
- Commission FAQ 5.2 and 5.4: non-exploited zero-days and non-exploitable component vulnerabilities may be notified voluntarily under Art 15 — [Commission FAQ v1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- Art 19(5), second subpara (importers): on becoming aware of a vulnerability, inform the manufacturer "without undue delay"; where the product presents a significant cybersecurity risk, "immediately inform the market surveillance authorities" of the Member States where made available, with details of non-compliance and corrective measures; Art 19(3): pre-market, inform manufacturer and MSAs of significant risk; Art 19(8): inform MSAs and users if the manufacturer ceases operations — [springlex Art 19](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-19/)
- Art 20(4), second subpara (distributors): on becoming aware of a vulnerability, inform the manufacturer without undue delay; if significant cybersecurity risk, immediately inform MSAs of the Member States where made available; Art 20(3): pre-market significant risk → inform manufacturer and MSAs; Art 20(6): manufacturer ceased operations → inform MSAs and users — [springlex Art 20](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-20/)
- Art 24(3): Art 14(1) applies to open-source software stewards "to the extent that they are involved in the development" of the products; Art 14(3) and (8) apply to the extent severe incidents also affect network and information systems the steward provides for development; Art 24(1): stewards must have a documented cybersecurity policy including voluntary reporting under Art 15; Art 24(2): cooperation with MSAs — [springlex Art 24](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-24/)
- Commission FAQ 5.5 and Guidance para 216: Art 24(3) applies from 11 Dec 2027 (Art 71(2)); the steward's obligation arises on becoming aware of active exploitation, typically via downstream reports from manufacturers, users or researchers — [Commission FAQ v1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331); [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Art 64(10)(b): open-source software stewards are exempt from administrative fines for any infringement — [springlex Art 64](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-64/)
- Art 13(6) requires manufacturers to report vulnerabilities in integrated components upstream to the component's manufacturer or maintainer (Guidance Section 9.2.1) — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- ENISA FAQ 1/4/19/29 and the launch release: stewards will also use the SRP from 11 Dec 2027 — [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions); [ENISA news](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/the-cra-single-reporting-platform-is-launched)

### Inferences
- The importer/distributor duties (in force from 11 Dec 2027, as Arts 18-23 are not among the early-application provisions) create an inbound channel the manufacturer's PSIRT must be ready to receive; their "vulnerability" trigger is broader than the manufacturer's "actively exploited" trigger, so most inbound notices will not themselves start the Art 14 clock but must be triaged.
- Because Art 15(4) obliges CSIRTs to pass third-party AEV/SI reports to the manufacturer, a CSIRT contact can be a trigger source; the runbook should route CSIRT inbound mail to the triage queue.

### Gaps
- Timing for the SRP's Art 15 voluntary-reporting phase is not announced.

## Key Question 8 — Transitional provision: Art 69(3) wording; Art 71(2) application dates

### Takeaway
Art 69(3) derogates from Art 69(2) so that Art 14 applies to all in-scope products placed on the market before 11 December 2027; Art 71(2) sets 11 September 2026 as the date Art 14 applies. The Commission confirms no retroactive reporting for exploitation known before 11 September 2026 and that reporting (but not vulnerability handling) continues after end of support.

### Cited Findings

## Key Question 9 — Penalties for Art 14 non-compliance (Art 64 tier)

### Takeaway
Non-compliance with Art 14 sits in the top tier under Art 64(2): administrative fines up to EUR 15,000,000 or, for undertakings, up to 2.5% of total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher. Micro and small enterprises are exempt from fines for missing the 24-hour early-warning deadlines; open-source stewards are exempt from fines altogether.

### Cited Findings
- Art 64(2): non-compliance with Annex I essential requirements and with Articles 13 and 14 → up to EUR 15 000 000 or 2.5% of worldwide annual turnover, "whichever is higher"; Art 64(3): Articles 18-23, 28, 30-33(5), 39, 41, 47, 49, 53 → up to EUR 10 000 000 or 2%; Art 64(4): incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to notified bodies/MSAs → up to EUR 5 000 000 or 1%; Art 64(5): factors (nature, gravity, duration; prior fines; size and market share); Art 64(10): exemptions for micro/small enterprises on Art 14(2)(a)/14(4)(a) deadlines and for open-source stewards — [springlex Art 64](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-64/)
- Confirmed as the highest tier by CSA (16 Sept 2026) and Jones Day (July 2026) — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-cra-single-reporting-platform-20260916-csa/); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/07/eu-cyber-resilience-act-24hour-reporting-duties-start-september-11-2026)
- Art 64(1): Member States lay down penalty rules; Art 64(6)-(9): notification of fines between MSAs, public-body treatment, courts, and cumulation with corrective measures — [springlex Art 64](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-64/)
- Market surveillance and enforcement provisions apply only from 11 Dec 2027 (Commission FAQ 7.1) — [Commission FAQ v1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)

### Inferences
- Although Art 14 is binding from 11 Sept 2026, the MSA enforcement machinery (Chapter V) applies only from 11 Dec 2027, so fines for late 2026 failures would in practice be imposed after that date, by national MSAs under national penalty laws. Failures now also create evidence for later enforcement and reputational/contractual exposure.

### Gaps
- National penalty laws implementing Art 64 (e.g. Germany's CRA implementation act, Ireland's) were not verified; several sources mention a German "CRA-Durchführungsgesetz" being in progress but no status detail was retrieved.

## Key Question 10 — Digital Omnibus (COM(2025) 837, 19 Nov 2025) and other proposals touching CRA reporting; legislative status at 30 October 2026

### Takeaway
The Commission's Digital Omnibus (COM(2025) 837, procedure 2025/0360(COD)) does not amend the CRA. It inserts a new NIS2 Art 23a obliging ENISA to build a "single-entry point" for incident reporting that "may" build on the CRA SRP, mandates its use for NIS2, GDPR (with a 72h→96h deadline change), DORA, eIDAS and CER reporting, and adds NIS2 Art 23(12) so that a CRA Art 14(3) severe-incident notification counts as the NIS2 Art 23(4) report where it contains the required information. The Council's mandate (Coreper, 21 Oct 2026; ST 14756/26) replaces the ENISA single-entry point with national entry points (new Art 23b) and an ENISA "incident reporting information point" that cannot receive notifications, moves application to 30 months, but keeps the Art 23(12) CRA/NIS2 deeming rule. Parliament (ITRE/LIBE, rapporteurs Salla and Kaljurand) has a draft report (22 June 2026) and amendments (27 July 2026) but no committee vote; trilogues have not begun. Nothing changes CRA Art 14 timelines or recipients.

## Key Question 11 — Relationship between CRA Art 14 and NIS2 Art 23, GDPR Art 33 and DORA Art 19 for multi-regime organisations

### Takeaway
The regimes are cumulative today: different triggers (product AEV/SI vs. service "significant incident" vs. personal-data breach vs. "major ICT-related incident"), different recipients (CDaC+ENISA vs. national CSIRT/competent authority vs. DPA vs. financial competent authority) and partially overlapping clocks (24h/72h shared by NIS2 and CRA; 72h GDPR; DORA's own initial/intermediate/final cadence). The Commission has deliberately aligned the CRA "becoming aware" test with NIS2 and GDPR guidance, and the Omnibus (both Commission and Council texts) would let a CRA severe-incident report count as the NIS2 report; no such deeming exists for GDPR or DORA. AFME has asked that financial entities report only under DORA with onward sharing, but that is an industry request, not law.

### Cited Findings
- Guidance para 212: CRA "becoming aware" aligned with NIS2 Implementing Regulation 2024/2690 recital 31 and EDPB Guidelines 9/2022 on GDPR breach notification "to ensure the concept ... is interpreted consistently" across regimes — [Commission Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Comparative table (Kennedys): GDPR Art 33 — 72h to DPA; NIS2 Art 23 — early warning 24h, notification 72h, final report 1 month to CSIRT/competent authority, intermediate on request; DORA Art 19 — initial/intermediate/final to financial competent authority (final when root-cause analysis complete); CRA Art 14 — 24h/72h, final 14 days (AEV) or 1 month (SI) to designated CSIRT and ENISA; clocks run separately; "a single report is not confirmed" — [Kennedys](https://kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2025/comparative-overview-of-reporting-requirements-under-eu-data-protection-and-cybersecurity-laws)
- Proposed NIS2 Art 23(12) (Commission and Council texts): a CRA Art 14(3) severe-incident report containing the NIS2 Art 23(4) information "shall constitute reporting" under NIS2 — [proposal PDF](https://table.media/assets/europe/com_2025_837_1_en_act_part1_v7_v15itxw1bummxvxqje8koqqgw1y_121742.pdf); [Council doc 14756/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-14756-2026-INIT/en/pdf)
- Proposed GDPR Art 33(1): 72h → "not later than 96 hours", and (Council) only breaches likely to result in high risk — [Council doc 14756/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-14756-2026-INIT/en/pdf); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/eu-council-agrees-4-month-cookie-re-ask-wait-for-digital-omnibus-talks/)
- AFME (July 2026) to DG FISMA: a financial entity "should only be required to report under DORA" with the competent authority forwarding to CSIRTs and ENISA; also asks for aligned user/client communications; notes the CRA Art 26 interplay guidance was not in the Commission's 2026 guidance and is expected as a standalone instrument — [AFME briefing](https://www.afme.eu/our-work/digital-innovation/ensuring-appropriate-and-workable-cyber-and-operational-resilience-requirement/guidance-on-the-interplay-between-dora-and-the-cyber-resilience-act/)
- BSI FAQ addresses dual reporting under NIS2 and the CRA and notes the Omnibus proposal could remove duplication, as a proposal only — [BSI CRA-SRP page](https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Themen/Unternehmen-und-Organisationen/Informationen-und-Empfehlungen/Cyber_Resilience_Act/Single_Reporting_Platform-CRA/single_reporting_platform-cra.html)
- ICTRecht (NL, 13 Aug 2026): a manufacturer that is also a NIS2 essential/important entity may owe reports under several regimes for one incident; both NIS2 and CRA use a 24h early warning but content and addressee differ; recommends one integrated incident-response process — [ICTRecht](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/help-ook-van-de-cra-moet-ik-van-alles-melden-maar-wat-precies-en-bij-wie)
- Skadden: NIS2 and GDPR regulators are "other EU regulators to consider notifying" when a CRA report is made; DORA not addressed — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/get-ready-reporting-faqs-and-checklist)
- Recital 66 / FAQ 5.4: CRA reporting exists so CSIRTs and ENISA can fulfil NIS2 tasks and support essential/important entities — [Commission FAQ v1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- NIS2 Art 12(1) CVD processes are referenced in CRA Art 16(2) and 16(6) as grounds for delaying dissemination — [springlex Art 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-16/)

### Inferences
- For a financial-services group that is a NIS2 entity, a DORA financial entity, a GDPR controller and (via e.g. a mobile banking app) a CRA manufacturer, one compromised product can start four clocks with four recipients. A practical design: a single awareness/classification decision record (the Commission has aligned the awareness test), a shared fact base (nature, impact, mitigation are common to all four), then regime-specific filings with the CRA SRP filing (24h) and NIS2 early warning (24h) as the earliest hard deadlines, GDPR at 72h (96h if the Omnibus passes), and DORA per its RTS. Nothing today lets one filing discharge another.
- The CRA trigger is narrower (product-security impact or active exploitation) than NIS2/DORA service-impact triggers; a NIS2 significant incident at the manufacturer's own IT is not a CRA severe incident unless it compromises product security (BSI's ransomware example).

### Gaps
- DORA Art 19 initial-notification timing (4h from classification / 24h from awareness under the RTS) was not verified from a primary source in this research; the Kennedys page did not state it.
- No regulator has published a cross-regime mapping for CRA; the Omnibus Council text would require the Commission to produce one within 6 months of entry into force (Art 23c), i.e. not before 2027.
