Scope note: these notes cover Arts 7, 8, 12, 13(8)/(12)/(13)/(18)-(22), 27-33, 35-51, 69, 71 and Annexes III-VIII of the CRA, plus the secondary legislation and guidance that existed on 30 Oct 2026. Primary-source article text was read via EUR-Lex where it would load and otherwise via verbatim legal-text mirrors (springlex.eu, streamlex.eu, cyberresilienceact.eu, european-cyber-resilience-act.com). The mirrors reproduce the OJ text; they are cited where EUR-Lex HTML truncated. All quotes under 15 words. Vendor/commentary material is labelled as such.

Key dates (Art 71, confirmed by Commission Communication C(2026) 5252): entry into force 10 Dec 2024; Chapter IV (Arts 35-51, notified bodies) applies from 11 June 2026; Art 14 reporting from 11 Sept 2026; everything else from 11 Dec 2027 — [Commission Communication C(2026) 5252, 27 July 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131455); [Commission FAQ s.7.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307).

## KQ1. Art 7 important products: Annex III lists, Art 7(2) criteria, Art 7(3) delegated acts, Art 7(4) technical-descriptions implementing act

### Takeaway
Annex III has 19 Class I and 4 Class II categories; the Art 7(4) implementing act was adopted on time as Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 (28 Nov 2025, OJ L 1 Dec 2025, in force 21 Dec 2025) and gives a per-category technical description plus "core functionality" rules; no Art 7(3) delegated act amending Annex III has been adopted or consulted on as of 30 Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Art 7 text**
- Art 7(1): products that "have the core functionality of a product category set out in Annex III" are important products and are subject to the Art 32(2)-(3) conformity assessment procedures; integrating an important product into another product "shall not in itself render the product in which it is integrated" subject to those procedures — [Springlex, CRA Art 7](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-7/)
- Art 7(2): Annex III categories are divided into Class I and Class II and must "meet at least one of the following criteria": (a) the product "primarily performs functions critical to the cybersecurity of other products, networks or services" (examples in the text: "securing authentication and access", "intrusion prevention and detection", "end-point security or network protection"); or (b) it performs a function carrying "a significant risk of adverse effects in terms of its intensity" and ability to disrupt, control or damage "a large number of other products" or the health/security/safety of users "through direct manipulation" — [Springlex, CRA Art 7](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-7/)
- Art 7(3): the Commission may adopt delegated acts (Art 61) amending Annex III "by including in the list a new category within each class", "moving a category of products from one class to the other", or withdrawing a category; such acts shall where appropriate provide "a minimum transitional period of 12 months" before Art 32(2)-(3) procedures apply to newly added/reclassified categories, shorter only "on imperative grounds of urgency" — [Springlex, CRA Art 7](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-7/)
- Art 7(4): by 11 December 2025 the Commission shall adopt an implementing act (examination procedure, Art 62(2)) specifying the technical descriptions of the Annex III Class I/II categories and the Annex IV categories — [Springlex, CRA Art 7](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-7/); [cyberresilienceact.eu full text](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html)

**Annex III, Class I (19 categories, numbering as in the OJ text)** — [Streamlex, CRA Annex III](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-iii/)
1. Identity management systems and privileged access management software and hardware, including authentication and access control readers, including biometric readers
2. Standalone and embedded browsers
3. Password managers
4. Software that searches for, removes, or quarantines malicious software
5. Products with digital elements with the function of virtual private network (VPN)
6. Network management systems
7. Security information and event management (SIEM) systems
8. Boot managers
9. Public key infrastructure and digital certificate issuance software
10. Physical and virtual network interfaces
11. Operating systems
12. Routers, modems intended for the connection to the internet, and switches
13. Microprocessors with security-related functionalities
14. Microcontrollers with security-related functionalities
15. Application specific integrated circuits (ASIC) and field-programmable gate arrays (FPGA) with security-related functionalities
16. Smart home general purpose virtual assistants
17. Smart home products with security functionalities, including smart door locks, security cameras, baby monitoring systems and alarm systems
18. Internet connected toys covered by Directive 2009/48/EC that have social interactive features (e.g. speaking or filming) or that have location tracking features
19. Personal wearable products to be worn or placed on a human body that have a health monitoring (such as tracking) purpose and to which Regulation (EU) 2017/745 or (EU) 2017/746 do not apply, or personal wearable products intended for the use by and for children

**Annex III, Class II (4 categories)** — [Streamlex, CRA Annex III](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-iii/)
1. Hypervisors and container runtime systems that support virtualised execution of operating systems and similar environments
2. Firewalls, intrusion detection and prevention systems
3. Tamper-resistant microprocessors
4. Tamper-resistant microcontrollers

**The Art 7(4) implementing act: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392**
- Full title: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 of 28 November 2025 on the technical description of the categories of important and critical products with digital elements pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2024/2847; adopted Brussels 28 Nov 2025; published OJ L series dated 1 Dec 2025; legal basis Art 7(4) CRA; Art 3: enters into force on the twentieth day after publication (i.e. 21 Dec 2025); CELEX 32025R2392; ELI reg_impl/2025/2392/oj — [EUR-Lex CELEX:32025R2392](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R2392); [EUR-Lex ELI page](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/2392/oj/eng) (ELI metadata confirms adoption 28 Nov 2025 and entry into force 21 Dec 2025); confirmed by [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation) and [TÜV Rheinland regulatory note (vendor)](https://www.tuv.com/regulations-and-standards/en/eu-commission-implementing-regulation-eu-2025-2392-on-the-technical-description-of-the-categories-of-important-and-critical-products-with-digital-elements.html)
- Structure: Art 1 Definitions ("Common Criteria" and "Common Evaluation Methodology" by reference to EUCC Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482); Art 2(1) Annex I of the IR = technical descriptions of CRA Annex III Class I and II; Art 2(2) Annex II of the IR = technical descriptions of CRA Annex IV; Art 3 entry into force — [EUR-Lex CELEX:32025R2392](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R2392)
- Recital clarifications: a product's core functionality determines whether it meets a category description and which procedure applies; embedding a component that matches a category does not make the host product subject to that category's procedure (example: news app with embedded browser), though the whole product's security incl. integrated components must still be assessed; extra/ancillary functions do not remove a product from a category (operating systems with ancillary tools; routers with firewall functions); a product that merely can perform a category's functions but whose core functionality is different is not in the category (examples: SOAR software is not a SIEM; a smartphone is not an operating system or password manager); all manufacturers must do the Art 13(2)-(3) risk assessment regardless of class; examples in the IR are illustrative, not exhaustive; AVA_VAN levels distinguish tamper-resistant and secure-element categories, and certificates based on older Common Criteria versions are acceptable per IR 2024/482 (older versions accepted until end-2027) — [EUR-Lex CELEX:32025R2392](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R2392)

**Per-category technical descriptions in IR 2025/2392, Annex I (CRA Annex III)** — all from [EUR-Lex CELEX:32025R2392](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R2392) (summaries; category numbers match CRA Annex III)
- Class I.1 Identity management / PAM: systems providing authentication or authorisation and privileged-access software; includes authentication and biometric readers, SSO, federated identity, OTP and MFA software.
- Class I.2 Standalone and embedded browsers: software for accessing and rendering web content; includes browsers with AI-agent integration.
- Class I.3 Password managers: products storing passwords locally or remotely; includes browser extensions and hardware-based managers.
- Class I.4 Anti-malware: software that detects, removes or quarantines malicious code; includes rootkit detection and rescue disks.
- Class I.5 VPN: products creating encrypted logical tunnels; includes VPN clients, servers and gateways.
- Class I.6 Network management systems: systems that monitor and configure network elements; includes SDN controllers.
- Class I.7 SIEM: products that collect and correlate security data into actionable information.
- Class I.8 Boot managers: software managing system start-up; includes UEFI firmware and boot loaders.
- Class I.9 PKI / certificate issuance: products managing digital certificates or cryptographic keys; includes key management systems and OCSP responders.
- Class I.10 Physical and virtual network interfaces: hardware/software connecting devices to networks; includes NICs, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, virtual NICs and VPN interfaces.
- Class I.11 Operating systems: software that abstracts hardware and manages execution; includes real-time and general-purpose OS.
- Class I.12 Routers, internet modems, switches: routers select network paths; modems convert signals for internet communication; switches forward packets and have a management plane; includes wireless access points acting as bridges.
- Class I.13 Microprocessors with security functions: ICs for central processing that also provide security features (e.g. encryption, secure boot).
- Class I.14 Microcontrollers with security functions: programmable ICs with similar security features.
- Class I.15 ASICs/FPGAs with security functions: custom or reprogrammable ICs with similar security features.
- Class I.16 Smart home general-purpose virtual assistants: products processing natural-language requests and controlling connected home devices; includes smart speakers with integrated assistants.
- Class I.17 Smart home security products: products protecting physical security in homes — smart door locks, security cameras, baby monitors, alarms — including central control hardware and software.
- Class I.18 Internet-connected toys: toys under Directive 2009/48/EC with social interactive features (microphone, camera, speaker) or location tracking; proximity sensing alone does not count as location tracking.
- Class I.19 Personal wearables: health-monitoring wearables that are not medical devices under Reg. 2017/745 or 2017/746, and wearables for children under 14; includes fitness trackers and smartwatches.
- Class II.1 Hypervisors and container runtimes: type 1, type 2 and hybrid hypervisors, and container runtime systems.
- Class II.2 Firewalls / IDS / IPS: network and application firewalls incl. web application firewalls and anti-spam gateways; intrusion detection and prevention systems, network-based and host-based.
- Class II.3 Tamper-resistant microprocessors: Class I.13 microprocessors with tamper features, designed to AVA_VAN level 2 or 3.
- Class II.4 Tamper-resistant microcontrollers: Class I.14 microcontrollers with tamper features, designed to AVA_VAN level 2 or 3.

**Consultation history**: the draft IR was put to public consultation in 2025 (CMS note on the consultation opening; Cybersecurity Coalition comments) — [CMS, "Consultation on the Technical Description Opens"](https://cms.law/en/fra/legal-updates/the-cyber-resilience-act-consultation-on-the-technical-description-opens); [Cybersecurity Coalition comments](https://www.centerforcybersecuritypolicy.org/insights-and-research/cybersecurity-coalition-comments-on-cra-implementing-regulation-on-technical-descriptions-of-products-with-digital-elements) (not opened; search-result titles only — see Gaps)

**Commission guidance C(2026) 5252 (27 July 2026) on "core functionality" (section 6.1)** — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Point 139: core functionality = the product's main features and technical capabilities "without which it would not be able to meet its intended purpose", assessed in context using instructions for use, promotional/sales materials and technical documentation.
- Point 140-141: performing additional functions, or integrating a component that is itself an important/critical product, does not prevent the product having a core functionality; mere integration does not make the host important/critical (Example 58: smartphone integrating an OS is not an OS).
- Point 142: a product may "substantially exceed or fall substantially short" of a category (Example 59: SOAR exceeds SIEM, not a SIEM; Example 60: log collection/visualisation tools without correlation fall short of SIEM, not a SIEM); classification is objective, "not on the way in which the product ... is described or marketed".
- Point 143: manufacturers may not misrepresent core functionality to escape the regime; inconsistency between marketing, instructions and technical documentation is an indicator.
- Point 144: a product "may not have more than one core functionality" for determining the regime; core functionality should be clearly identified in the Annex VII technical documentation.
- Point 145 / Example 61: modules sold separately (separate purchase, licence or subscription) are standalone products classified by their own core functionality — a security suite's SIEM module = Class I, its IDS module = Class II, its analytics module = default; modules supplied only as components of an integrated product are classified at the level of the integrated product.
- Point 146: non-compliance with Art 32 may trigger fines under Art 64(3).
- Commission FAQ s.3.1-3.4 gives the same reading (core functionality; news app with embedded browser; laptop with secure element not critical; OS with calculator still OS; router with firewall still router; SOAR not SIEM; smartphone not OS/password manager) — [Commission FAQ (Markdown), s.3](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)

**Art 7(3) delegated acts**: the Commission's implementation factpage (last updated 27 July 2026) lists only two adopted secondary acts (IR 2025/2392 and Delegated Regulation 2026/881 on CSIRT dissemination delays) and one planned delegated act (EUCC presumption of conformity, Q4 2026); no delegated act amending Annex III is listed as adopted, drafted or planned — [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation)

### Inferences
- Because IR 2025/2392 ties Class II.3/II.4 to AVA_VAN 2-3 and Annex IV secure elements to AVA_VAN.4+, hardware teams can use their Common Criteria vulnerability-assurance target as a direct classification discriminator between Class I (13/14), Class II (3/4) and critical (Annex IV.3).
- The "one core functionality per product" rule (guidance pt 144) plus the "separately-sold modules are separate products" rule (pt 145) means product-packaging and licensing decisions change classification; SKU-level classification records are needed.

## KQ2. Art 8 critical products: Annex IV list, delegated-act certification mechanism, status

### Takeaway
Annex IV lists three critical categories; Art 8(1) lets the Commission by delegated act make an EUCC-type certificate (assurance "substantial" or higher) mandatory for some of them, but no such delegated act has been adopted or published for consultation as of 30 Oct 2026, so Annex IV products currently follow the Class II third-party routes (Art 32(4)(b)).

### Cited Findings
- Annex IV (critical products) — [Streamlex, CRA Annex IV](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-iv/):
  1. Hardware Devices with Security Boxes
  2. Smart meter gateways within smart metering systems as defined in Article 2, point (23) of Directive (EU) 2019/944 and other devices for advanced security purposes, including for secure cryptoprocessing
  3. Smartcards or similar devices, including secure elements
- Art 8(1): the Commission may by delegated act (Art 61) require that certain Annex IV categories obtain a European cybersecurity certificate at assurance level at least "substantial" under a scheme adopted under Regulation (EU) 2019/881 (Cybersecurity Act); precondition: a relevant scheme exists; the Commission must first assess market impact and consult stakeholders; such acts give "a minimum transitional period of six months" unless urgency; where no such act applies, Annex IV products follow the Art 32(3) procedures — [cyberresilienceact.eu full text, Art 8](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html)
- Art 8(2): the Commission may by delegated act amend Annex IV; a category must meet at least one criterion: critical dependency of essential entities (NIS2 Art 3(1)) on it, or potential for serious disruption of critical supply chains across the internal market; six-month minimum transitional period also applies — [cyberresilienceact.eu full text, Art 8](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html)
- Art 32(4): critical products use (a) a certification scheme under Art 8(1) or (b) where the Art 8(1) conditions are not met, any Art 32(3) procedure — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 32](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_32.html)
- IR 2025/2392 Annex II technical descriptions for Annex IV — [EUR-Lex CELEX:32025R2392](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R2392):
  - IV.1 Hardware devices with security boxes: multi-component hardware storing/processing sensitive data with a physical enclosure giving tamper protection; includes payment terminals, HSMs and tachographs.
  - IV.2 Smart meter gateways: gateways controlling communication in smart metering systems, handling encryption, firewalling and device control; includes electricity gateways and, if they meet the description, gas and heat gateways.
  - IV.3 Smartcards and secure elements: secure elements = microcontrollers/microprocessors with tamper protection designed to at least AVA_VAN.4, including TPMs and embedded UICCs; smartcards = secure elements in card or other carrier form, including ID documents, payment and access cards, digital tachograph cards.
- Status of Art 8 delegated acts: the Commission's implementation factpage (updated 27 July 2026) lists no Art 8 delegated act as adopted, in consultation or planned; the only planned delegated act is the EUCC presumption-of-conformity act (Q4 2026) — [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation)
- The Commission's conformity-assessment page (updated 31 July 2026) says for critical products "The use of a notified body is mandatory in any case", that certified products "might not need the participation of a notified body", and "The exact conditions need to be specified by the Commission via a delegated act" (none reported adopted) — [Commission, CRA conformity assessment page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-conformity-assessment)
- Commission FAQ s.6.2: Module B+C or H is mandatory for critical products "unless the use of a European cybersecurity certification scheme is made mandatory in the future in accordance with Article 8(1)" — [Commission FAQ s.6.2](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Germany's BSI notification page (updated 6 Aug 2026) states EUCC certificates would establish presumption of conformity only after the Commission adopts a delegated act, which "is not yet in place" — [BSI, Notification CRA](https://www.bsi.bund.de/EN/Themen/Unternehmen-und-Organisationen/Informationen-und-Empfehlungen/Cyber_Resilience_Act/Notifizierung-CRA/notifizierung-cra.html)
- ENISA's analysis report "Cyber Resilience Act implementation via EUCC and its applicable technical elements" (26 Feb 2025) proposes how EUCC could cover CRA essential requirements; ENISA states it is exploratory and that establishing a presumption "would require a formal legal act" under Art 27(9); EUCC is not mandatory for CRA even for important/critical products — [ENISA certification site](https://certification.enisa.europa.eu/publications/cyber-resilience-act-implementation-eucc-and-its-applicable-technical-elements_en); report PDF: https://certification.enisa.europa.eu/document/download/b51f00ee-d2c3-47e7-af85-059b5e06acef_en?filename=CRA%20implementation%20via%20EUCC.pdf
- Searches for a "Have your say" consultation or draft delegated act under Art 8(1) returned no such initiative (results were only the CRA text and commentary) — [WebSearch results, Art 8 delegated act](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_8.html)

### Inferences
- For Annex IV products the operative route today is identical to Class II (Module B+C or H via a CRA notified body, or an EUCC certificate at "substantial"/"high" once Art 27(9) delegated act exists); planning should assume that a mandatory-certification delegated act, if adopted, would come with at least 6 months' transition (Art 8(1)).
- Because IR 2025/2392 pegs secure elements to AVA_VAN.4, EUCC "high" certifications already common for smartcards/SEs are the natural evidence base once the Art 27(9) act lands.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for any Art 8(1) delegated act draft, impact assessment or stakeholder consultation; absence is inferred from the Commission factpage list and failed searches, not from an explicit Commission statement.

## KQ3. Art 12: high-risk AI systems that are products with digital elements

### Takeaway
A high-risk AI system in CRA scope that meets Annex I Parts I and II and shows the Art 15 AI Act protection level in its EU declaration is deemed to meet AI Act Art 15 cybersecurity; the AI Act Art 43 procedure governs conformity assessment, except that important/critical products under AI Act Annex VI internal control follow the CRA procedures for the cybersecurity requirements.

### Cited Findings
- Art 12(1): products with digital elements classified as high-risk AI systems under Art 6 AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689) are "deemed to comply with the cybersecurity requirements set out in Article 15" AI Act (without prejudice to accuracy/robustness) where (a) the product meets Annex I Part I; (b) the manufacturer's processes meet Annex I Part II; (c) the Art 15 AI Act protection level is demonstrated in the EU declaration of conformity — [Springlex, CRA Art 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-12/)
- Art 12(2): the AI Act Art 43 conformity assessment procedure applies to such products for the CRA essential requirements; AI Act notified bodies may also assess compliance with CRA Annex I provided their compliance with CRA Art 39 was assessed during the AI Act notification procedure — [Springlex, CRA Art 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-12/)
- Art 12(3): by derogation from (2), for important products under Art 32(2)(a)-(b) or Art 32(3), and critical products under Art 8(1) or (absent that) Art 32(3), that are high-risk AI systems subject to the AI Act Annex VI internal-control procedure, the CRA conformity assessment procedures apply for the essential cybersecurity requirements — [Springlex, CRA Art 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-12/)
- Art 12(4): manufacturers may participate in AI regulatory sandboxes under Art 57 AI Act — [Springlex, CRA Art 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-12/)
- Recital 51 confirms products that are high-risk AI systems and within CRA scope must meet the CRA essential requirements — [EUR-Lex OJ L 2024/2847 recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- The 27 July 2026 Commission guidance does not address the AI Act interplay; it says the Commission "may consider issuing further guidance" including on the interplay between the CRA and Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and DORA — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pt 9](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- The Commission FAQ's "Interplay with other legislation" section covers aviation, marine equipment, Product Liability Directive, Machinery Regulation, GPSR, RED, EHDS, GDPR and Data Act but has no AI Act entry — [Commission FAQ s.2](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)

### Inferences
- Decision logic: (i) is the product a high-risk AI system (AI Act Art 6)? (ii) if default-category under the CRA → AI Act Art 43 route covers CRA cybersecurity, and one EU declaration can cover both acts (Art 28(3) single declaration dossier); (iii) if important/critical and the AI Act route would be Annex VI internal control → run the CRA Art 32(2)/(3)/(4) route (likely with a CRA notified body) for the cybersecurity requirements.
- Teams should expect an AI Act notified body to be usable for CRA Annex I only if its notification file shows a CRA Art 39 assessment; this needs checking in NANDO/SMCS once CRA scopes appear.

### Gaps
- No Commission or ENISA guidance yet exists on practical sequencing of AI Act Art 43 and CRA Art 32 assessments; the Commission has only signalled possible future guidance.

## KQ4. Art 32 conformity assessment procedures and Annex VIII modules (A, B, C, H), FOSS rule

### Takeaway
Default products may always use Module A; Class I may use Module A only if harmonised standards/common specifications/EUCC-type schemes ("substantial"+) are fully applied, otherwise Module B+C or H; Class II needs B+C, H or a qualifying EU certificate; critical products need certification if an Art 8(1) act requires it, otherwise the Class II routes; Art 32(5) lets manufacturers of FOSS products in Annex III categories use any Art 32(1) procedure (incl. Module A) if the technical documentation is made public at placing on the market.

### Cited Findings

**Art 32 text** — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 32](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_32.html); [cyberresilienceact.eu full text](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html)
- 32(1): the manufacturer assesses the product and its processes against Annex I using (a) internal control (Module A, Annex VIII Part I); (b) EU-type examination (Module B, Part II) followed by conformity to type based on internal production control (Module C, Part III); (c) full quality assurance (Module H, Part IV); or (d) "where available and applicable", a European cybersecurity certification scheme per Art 27(9).
- 32(2): Class I — where harmonised standards, common specifications or European cybersecurity certification schemes at assurance level at least "substantial" are not applied, applied only in part, or do not exist, the product and processes must use (a) Module B + Module C or (b) Module H.
- 32(3): Class II — (a) B+C; (b) H; or (c) "where available and applicable", a certification scheme under Art 27(9) at assurance level at least "substantial".
- 32(4): critical — (a) a scheme under Art 8(1); or (b) if Art 8(1) conditions are not met, any paragraph 3 procedure.
- 32(5): manufacturers of products qualifying as free and open-source software that fall under Annex III categories may use any paragraph 1 procedure provided the Art 31 technical documentation is made available to the public at the time of placing on the market.
- 32(6): fees must account for the interests and needs of microenterprises, SMEs and start-ups and be reduced proportionately.
- Art 32 does not have a separate "default products" rule; default products are those with no Annex III/IV core functionality and fall under 32(1) (Commission guidance footnote 22 says "default" is not a CRA-defined term) — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pt 137 & fn 22-23](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance pt 137: default-category products "can always rely on the internal control procedure"; manufacturer "can nonetheless always choose to apply a more stringent" procedure (fn 23); FOSS Class I/II products may follow default-category procedures per Art 32(5) — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission FAQ s.6.1: Module A is permitted for (i) all default products; (ii) Class I "if a harmonised standard has been applied in accordance with Article 32(2)"; (iii) Class I or II FOSS with public technical documentation (Art 32(5)); fn 11 confirms FOSS Class II may also self-assess — [Commission FAQ s.6.1 and fn 11](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- FAQ s.6.6: technical documentation need not be made public "with the exception of" FOSS manufacturers in Annex III categories self-attesting under Art 32(5) — [Commission FAQ s.6.6](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)

**Class I eligibility for Module A — what "fully applied" means (Commission guidance s.6.2)** — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pts 147-152](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Pt 148: third-party assessment (B+C, H or certification scheme) is mandatory for Class II (except FOSS per 32(5)) and for critical products; Class I needs third-party assessment "only if" the manufacturer has not applied, or applied only in part, OJ-cited harmonised standards, common specifications or certification schemes at least "substantial".
- Pt 149: for Class I to use internal control, "(i) all the applicable requirements of a relevant harmonised standard need to be applied; and (ii) the standard's scope needs to cover at least all the cybersecurity risks associated to that product's core functionality".
- Pt 150-151: the product may be broader than the standard; the manufacturer must still do the Art 13(2) risk assessment and treat residual risks "via other means", documenting additional measures; Module A remains available if the standard covers the core-functionality risks (Example 62: antivirus with disk-cleaning and anti-tracking extras).
- Pt 152 / Example 63: a Class I router that integrates a Class II firewall component is assessed as Class I as a whole; applying the router harmonised standard (plus e.g. the firewall standard for the firewall function) allows Module A for the whole product.
- Fn 24: using a European cybersecurity certification scheme counts as third-party involvement because a certification body is involved.

**Presumption-of-conformity consequences (guidance s.6.3)**: a cited harmonised standard gives presumption only for the risks it covers; ancillary functions outside the standard get no presumption even where Module A is allowed (Examples 64-65) — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pts 153-155](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

**Annex VIII in practice** — [Streamlex, CRA Annex VIII](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-viii/); cross-checked [Springlex, CRA Annex VIII](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/annex-8/)
- Part I, Module A (internal control): pt 1 manufacturer fulfils pts 2-4 and declares on its sole responsibility that products meet Annex I Part I and that it meets Part II; pt 2 draw up Annex VII technical documentation; pt 3 take measures so that design, development, production and vulnerability handling (and their monitoring) ensure compliance with Annex I Parts I and II; pt 4.1 affix CE marking to each compliant product; pt 4.2 draw up a written EU declaration of conformity per product (Art 28) and keep it with the technical documentation for 10 years after placing on the market or for the support period, whichever is longer, copy to authorities on request; pt 5 authorised representative may do pt 4 if mandated. No notified body; no NB identification number.
- Part II, Module B (EU-type examination): pt 1 a notified body examines technical design/development and the vulnerability-handling processes and attests compliance with Annex I Parts I and II; pt 2 assessment = technical documentation and supporting evidence plus examination of specimens of one or more critical parts (combination of production type and design type); pt 3 application to a single notified body of choice containing 3.1 name/address (and of authorised representative if it applies), 3.2 written declaration the same application has not been lodged with another notified body, 3.3 technical documentation enabling assessment incl. "an adequate risk analysis" and Annex VII elements where applicable, 3.4 supporting evidence for design and vulnerability-handling solutions incl. test results where needed and documents used where harmonised standards/specifications were not fully applied; pt 4 notified body shall 4.1 examine documentation and evidence, 4.2 verify specimens match documentation and identify standard vs non-standard elements, 4.3 test correct application of harmonised standards chosen, 4.4 test non-standard solutions, 4.5 agree test location with manufacturer; pt 5 evaluation report, releasable only with manufacturer's agreement (without prejudice to notifying-authority duties); pt 6 issue EU-type examination certificate (manufacturer name/address, conclusions, validity conditions, data identifying approved type and processes, annexes), or refuse with detailed reasons; pt 7 notified body monitors changes in the state of the art and may require further investigation; manufacturer informs the notified body of modifications affecting conformity or certificate validity, which need additional approval as an addition to the certificate; pt 8 notified body performs periodic audits to ensure vulnerability-handling processes are properly implemented; pt 9 notified body informs its notifying authority of certificates/additions issued or withdrawn and periodically/on request lists refused, suspended or restricted ones; informs other notified bodies of refused/withdrawn/suspended/restricted certificates and, on request, issued ones; Commission, Member States and other notified bodies may obtain copies; Commission and Member States may obtain technical documentation and examination results; notified body keeps a copy of certificate, annexes, additions and technical file until certificate expiry; pt 10 manufacturer keeps certificate, annexes and additions with the technical documentation for 10 years after placing on the market or the support period, whichever is longer; pt 11 authorised representative may lodge the application and fulfil pts 7 and 10 if mandated. The annex sets no fixed certificate validity period (the certificate states its own validity conditions). No NB identification-number affixing rule in Module B/C.
- Part III, Module C (conformity to type based on internal production control): pt 1 manufacturer fulfils pts 2-3 and declares products conform to the type in the EU-type examination certificate and meet Annex I Part I, and that it meets Part II; pt 2 take all measures so production and monitoring ensure conformity with the approved type and Part I and that the manufacturer meets Part II; pt 3.1 affix CE marking to each conforming product; pt 3.2 written EU declaration of conformity per product model, kept 10 years or support period (whichever longer), copy on request; pt 4 authorised representative may fulfil pt 3 if mandated. Production phase is not assessed by the notified body (FAQ s.6.2).
- Part IV, Module H (full quality assurance): pt 1 manufacturer fulfils pts 2 and 5 and declares on sole responsibility that products meet Part I and its vulnerability processes meet Part II; pt 2 operate an approved quality system covering design, development, final inspection and testing, and vulnerability handling, maintained throughout the support period, subject to surveillance (pt 4); pt 3.1 application to one notified body of choice with (a) name/address, (b) technical documentation for one model per product category incl. Annex VII elements, (c) quality-system documentation, (d) declaration no other notified body has the application; pt 3.2 quality system must ensure compliance and be documented in written policies, procedures and instructions covering (a) quality objectives, organisation, management responsibilities for design, development, quality and vulnerability handling, (b) design specifications and standards / means used where standards not fully applied, (c) procedural specifications for Part II, (d) design control and verification techniques, (e) production, quality control and assurance techniques, (f) examinations and tests before, during and after production with frequencies, (g) quality records (inspection reports, test and calibration data, personnel qualifications), (h) monitoring of quality-system effectiveness; elements matching the national standard implementing a harmonised standard are presumed to conform; pt 3.3 notified body assesses the quality system; audit team needs quality-management experience plus at least one assessor experienced in the product field and technology with knowledge of the Regulation; audit includes a site visit where premises exist; reasoned decision notified; pt 3.4 manufacturer maintains the approved system; pt 3.5 manufacturer informs the notified body of intended changes; notified body decides whether reassessment is needed and notifies with reasons; pt 4 surveillance: 4.1 purpose to verify the manufacturer fulfils quality-system obligations; 4.2 manufacturer grants access to design, development, production, inspection, testing and storage sites and supplies quality-system documentation, design records (analyses, calculations, tests) and manufacturing records (inspection reports, test and calibration data, personnel qualifications); 4.3 periodic audits with an audit report to the manufacturer (the two mirrors read differ on whether an "unexpected visits" clause is present — see Gaps); pt 5.1 affix the CE marking and, "under the responsibility of the notified body", the notified body's identification number to each conforming product; pt 5.2 EU declaration of conformity per product model kept 10 years or support period (whichever longer); pt 6 keep available to national authorities for at least 10 years or the support period (whichever longer): (a) technical documentation under 3.1, (b) quality-system documentation, (c) approved changes (3.5), (d) notified-body decisions and reports (3.5, 4.3); pt 7 notified body informs its notifying authority of quality-system approvals issued/withdrawn and lists refused/suspended/restricted ones; informs other notified bodies; pt 8 authorised representative may fulfil 3.1, 3.5, 5 and 6 if mandated.
- Commission FAQ s.6 practical reading: the three modules are "in increasing order of complexity, cost but also external support"; in B+C "only one notified body participates" and it "does not only carry out a documentation-based assessment" but performs necessary tests itself or via an external lab; the EU-type certificate "is valid for a certain period of time, as defined by the notified body"; substantial modifications need a new assessment "by the same or a different notified body" possibly revising the certificate; non-substantial modifications are not reassessed; under Module H the CE marking is affixed "together with the NANDO number of the notified body"; Module H suits manufacturers with many product types or frequent updates, and scope extensions to new/substantially modified products need a new assessment by the same notified body but are "a more streamlined process"; ISO 9000 certification alone does not satisfy Module H; the CRA mandates no specific evaluation methodology and sets no requirements on test laboratories; manufacturers may test in own or external labs — [Commission FAQ s.6.1-6.5](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Art 47 (operational obligations of notified bodies): assessments proportionate, avoiding "unnecessary burdens for economic operators", considering size (esp. SMEs), sector/structure/complexity, product risk level, and mass/serial production, while keeping "the degree of rigour and the level of protection required"; non-compliance before certification → require corrective measures and no certificate; post-certification non-compliance → corrective measures, suspend/withdraw where necessary; failing corrective measures → restrict, suspend or withdraw — [Springlex, CRA Art 47](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-47/)

**Vendor estimates (commentary, unverified)**: CRA Decoded (19 July 2026) cites consultancy quotes of 3-6 months for a Module B+C engagement once contracted, 2-4 months to find/contract a body, €20k-€60k per Module B examination, €80k-€150k for a first Module H engagement with surveillance "typically every one to two years", break-even around four products for Module H — [CRA Decoded (commentary)](https://cra-decoded.com/blog/posts/cra-notified-body-capacity-crisis/)

### Inferences
- Decision tree for route selection: default → Module A (or any stricter route voluntarily). Class I → Module A only if an OJ-cited harmonised standard (or common specification / Art 27(9)-listed scheme) is applied in full AND covers all core-functionality risks; since no CRA harmonised standard is OJ-cited as of Oct 2026 (see KQ5), Class I products assessed today must plan for B+C or H unless citations arrive before placing on the market. Class II → B+C, H or qualifying certificate. Critical → B+C/H (or certificate) until an Art 8(1) act mandates certification. FOSS in Annex III → any route incl. Module A if technical documentation is public.
- Module B+C fits few, stable product types; Module H fits portfolios with frequent substantial modifications because new variants extend the approved quality system rather than requiring a new type examination.

### Gaps
- The two mirrors read disagree on whether Module H pt 4 includes an "unexpected visits/tests" clause (Streamlex says none present; Springlex says "unannounced visits" not mentioned) — treat Module H surveillance as "periodic audits plus site access" unless the OJ text is checked directly; the EUR-Lex HTML could not be loaded beyond the recitals.
- No official data on notified-body fees or lead times; only vendor estimates.

## KQ5. Art 27 harmonised standards and presumption; Art 27(8)-(9) certification schemes; Art 28 common specifications; Art 29 EU declaration (Annex V/VI); Arts 30-31 CE marking incl. software

### Takeaway
No CRA harmonised standard had been cited in the OJ as of Sept/Oct 2026 and no common specification or Art 27(9) delegated act has been adopted (the EUCC presumption delegated act is planned for Q4 2026), so no presumption of conformity is yet available; the EU declaration must follow Annex V (or Annex VI simplified with a URL) and the CE marking for software goes on the declaration or the accompanying website, with the notified body number added only under Module H.

### Cited Findings

**Article numbering note**: in the OJ text, presumption of conformity is Art 27, the EU declaration of conformity is Art 28, general CE-marking principles Art 29, CE-marking rules Art 30, technical documentation Art 31, conformity assessment Art 32, SME support Art 33; "common specifications" sit inside Art 27(2)-(7), not a separate Art 28 — [cyberresilienceact.eu full text](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html); [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 27](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_27.html)

**Art 27 presumption of conformity** — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 27](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_27.html)
- 27(1): products and processes conforming to harmonised standards (or parts) whose references are published in the OJ are presumed to conform to the Annex I requirements covered; the Commission requests standards under Art 10(1) of Reg. 1025/2012 and should take account of existing European and international cybersecurity standards.
- 27(2): common specifications by implementing act (examination procedure Art 62(2)) only where (a) a standardisation request was not accepted, not delivered by deadline, or does not match the request, and (b) no harmonised standard reference is published or expected within a reasonable period; 27(3) Commission informs the Reg. 1025/2012 committee; 27(4) consults stakeholders; 27(5) common specifications give presumption for requirements covered; 27(6) when a harmonised standard is later cited, the Commission repeals the overlapping implementing act; 27(7) Member State objection procedure.
- 27(8): products and processes covered by an EU statement of conformity or certificate under a Reg. 2019/881 scheme are presumed to conform to the Annex I requirements covered.
- 27(9): the Commission may adopt delegated acts (Art 61) specifying which Reg. 2019/881 schemes can demonstrate conformity with Annex I; a certificate at least "substantial" under such a scheme removes the need for third-party assessment for the corresponding requirements under Art 32(2)(a)-(b) and 32(3)(a)-(b).
- Commission guidance pt 153 confirms Art 27(8) presumption applies "if this has been specified via delegated acts in accordance with Article 27(9)" — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pt 153](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

**Status of harmonised standards (as of Sept/Oct 2026)**
- Standardisation request: Commission Implementing Decision C(2025) 618 of 3 Feb 2025 (mandate M/606) to CEN, CENELEC and ETSI; 15 horizontal standards clustered into 3 deliverables (secure design/development/production — due 30 Aug 2026; Annex I Part I product properties — due 30 Oct 2027; vulnerability handling — due 30 Aug 2026) and 26 vertical standards (31 deliverables) covering Annex III/IV categories due 30 Oct 2026 — [Commission FAQ s.6.10](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission factpage timeline: "Q3 2026: first standardisation deliverables (horizontal and product-specific)"; "30 October 2027: more standardisation deliverables" — [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation)
- In early July 2026 the Commission proposed a draft amendment to M/606 moving the horizontal/vulnerability-handling drafting deadlines from 30 Aug 2026 to 31 Oct 2026 and the vertical (Type C) deadline from 30 Oct 2026 to 31 Dec 2026; as of 8 Sept 2026 the amendment was not adopted; no CRA harmonised standard was cited in the OJ as of 30 July, 8 Sept or 11 Sept 2026; prEN 40000-1-3 (vulnerability handling, CEN/CLC/JTC 13 WG9, building on EN 18031) is expected to be cited first; prEN 50770 series drafts cover OT verticals (firewalls/IDS/IPS, network management, VPN, routers/switches); ETSI opened a public enquiry on 17 draft product standards on 13 Aug 2026 — [cyberresilienceact.eu, standardisation deadlines note (independent guide, not official)](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-standardisation-deadlines-pushed-back-two-months.html)
- Hogan Lovells (20 Jan 2026, law-firm commentary): drafts for browsers, password managers, antivirus, VPN, network management, SIEM and boot managers at "mature draft" stage; first finalised deliverables expected Q3 2026 — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-cyber-resilience-act-getting-ready-for-cra-compliance-in-2026)
- Cenitia tracker (commentary, updated 5 Oct 2026): a work programme, draft or published EN title is "different from an OJ citation"; EN 18031 citations (under Decision 2025/138 for RED) "carry limitations" and neither EN 18031 nor ETSI EN 303 645 "automatically gives CRA presumption of conformity" — [Cenitia (commentary)](https://cenitia.com/library/cra-harmonised-standards-tracker)
- The Commission guidance records that no standard was cited (indirectly: it discusses future cited standards hypothetically, e.g. "it is expected that the harmonised standard for routers will address...") — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pt 154](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

**Common specifications**: none adopted; the Commission factpage lists no common-specification implementing act — [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation)

**Art 27(9) EUCC delegated act**: Commission factpage lists "Q4 2026 — Delegated act (planned) — Presumption of conformity for the EUCC certification scheme with the CRA"; not adopted as of the page's 27 July 2026 update — [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation); BSI (6 Aug 2026) confirms it is "not yet in place" — [BSI, Notification CRA](https://www.bsi.bund.de/EN/Themen/Unternehmen-und-Organisationen/Informationen-und-Empfehlungen/Cyber_Resilience_Act/Notifizierung-CRA/notifizierung-cra.html). Searches found no "Have your say" page for this draft (see Gaps).

**Art 28 EU declaration of conformity** — [cyberresilienceact.eu full text, Art 28](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html)
- 28(1): drawn up under Art 13(12); states that fulfilment of the applicable Annex I essential requirements has been demonstrated.
- 28(2): follows the Annex V model structure and contains the elements specified in the relevant Annex VIII module; "updated as appropriate"; made available in the language(s) required by the Member State where the product is placed/made available; the simplified declaration (Art 13(20)) follows Annex VI with the same language rule.
- 28(3): where several Union acts require a declaration, a single declaration covers all, identifying each act and its publication reference.
- 28(4): by drawing it up the manufacturer assumes responsibility for compliance.
- 28(5): the Commission may amend Annex V minimum content by delegated act.
- Annex V elements (8) — [Streamlex, CRA Annex V](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-v/): 1. name and type and any additional information enabling unique identification of the product; 2. name and address of the manufacturer or authorised representative; 3. statement that the declaration is "issued under the sole responsibility of the provider"; 4. object of the declaration (identification allowing traceability, may include a photograph); 5. statement that the object conforms to the relevant Union harmonisation legislation; 6. references to relevant harmonised standards, common specifications or cybersecurity certifications used; 7. where applicable, notified body name and number, description of the procedure performed and identification of the certificate; 8. additional information; signed for and on behalf of, place and date of issue, name, function, signature.
- Annex VI simplified declaration model — [Streamlex, CRA Annex VI](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-vi/): "Hereby, … [name of manufacturer] declares that the product with digital elements type …" is in compliance with Regulation (EU) 2024/2847; plus "The full text of the EU declaration of conformity is available at the following internet address: …".
- Art 13(20): manufacturers supply either the full EU declaration or a simplified one; "Where a simplified EU declaration of conformity is provided" it must contain "the exact internet address" of the full declaration — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 13](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_13.html)
- Commission FAQ s.6.8: the declaration "must accompany the product"; two formats (Annex V full; Annex VI one-sentence simplified with URL); it is linked to the individual product, not only the type, but need not include the unique identifier; "a new version of the product might need a new declaration", especially after substantial modification; cannot be signed without a positive conformity assessment; single-declaration dossier per Art 28(3) and Blue Guide s.4.4 — [Commission FAQ s.6.8](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission guidance pt 175: product families sharing architecture, security-relevant design, intended purpose and risks may use a single risk assessment, single technical documentation, single conformity assessment and "a single EU declaration of conformity" that "clearly identifies the product variants" — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pts 174-177](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

**Art 29-30 CE marking** — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 30](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_30.html); [cyberresilienceact.eu full text, Art 29](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html)
- Art 29: CE marking subject to the general principles in Art 30 of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008.
- Art 30(1): affixed "visibly, legibly and indelibly" to the product; where not possible or warranted by the product's nature, on the packaging and on the EU declaration of conformity; for software, "either to the EU declaration of conformity" or on the website accompanying the software; the website section must be easily and directly accessible to consumers.
- Art 30(2): may be less than 5 mm high because of the product's nature, provided visible and legible (FAQ s.6.7: not for aesthetic reasons).
- Art 30(3): affixed before placing on the market; may be followed by a pictogram or other mark indicating a cybersecurity risk or use per Art 30(6) implementing acts.
- Art 30(4): where a notified body is involved under Module H (full quality assurance), its identification number follows the CE marking; affixed by the notified body or, under its instructions, by the manufacturer/authorised representative.
- Art 30(5): Member States enforce; where other CE-marking legislation also applies, the marking indicates compliance with that too.
- Art 30(6): the Commission may adopt implementing acts for labels, pictograms and marks incl. support-period indications (none adopted per factpage).
- Commission FAQ s.6.7: CE marking is a visual self-declaration addressed to consumers and authorities; "Software products also need to bear the CE marking"; cannot be affixed without a positive conformity assessment; cannot be placed where not easily visible in intended use — [Commission FAQ s.6.7](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Annex VIII Module H pt 5.1 is the only module requiring the notified body's identification number on the product; Modules A, B and C have no NB-number rule — [Streamlex, CRA Annex VIII](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-viii/)
- Art 44: the Commission assigns each notified body a single identification number (even if notified under several Union acts) and publishes an up-to-date list of bodies, numbers and activities — [Springlex, CRA Art 44](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-44/)

### Inferences
- A software-only product needs: the CE mark on the Annex V declaration and/or an easily reachable website section; an Annex V declaration (or Annex VI sentence with exact URL) accompanying the product; under Module B+C no NB number is added to the CE mark (the certificate is referenced in Annex V item 7 instead); under Module H the NB number follows the CE mark.
- Until an OJ citation exists, "applying" a draft EN 40000/50770/ETSI standard gives neither presumption nor Module A eligibility for Class I; it does, however, serve as evidence in Annex VII item 5 ("other relevant technical specifications") and reduces re-work when the standard is cited.

### Gaps
- No primary source for a draft/consultation text of the Art 27(9) EUCC delegated act; "Q4 2026 planned" comes only from the Commission factpage dated 27 July 2026. Status on 30 Oct 2026 could not be confirmed (the factpage may not have been updated since).
- Could not access the OJ or the Commission standardisation page to independently confirm "no CRA harmonised standard cited" at 30 Oct 2026; the latest explicit statements found are dated 8-11 Sept 2026 (independent guide) and 5 Oct 2026 (commentary).

## KQ6. Annex VII technical documentation: elements, retention, language, authority requests

### Takeaway
Annex VII requires eight element groups (general description; design/development/production and vulnerability-handling processes incl. SBOM, CVD policy, contact address and secure-update mechanism; risk assessment; support-period rationale; standards applied; test reports; copy of the declaration; SBOM on reasoned request); the dossier must exist before placing on the market, be kept current at least through the support period, and be retained for 10 years or the support period, whichever is longer; it may be in any language but must be supplied to an authority in a language it easily understands.

### Cited Findings
- Art 31(1): technical documentation contains all relevant data/details of the means used to meet Annex I, "at least" the Annex VII elements; 31(2): drawn up before placing on the market and continuously updated, where appropriate, at least during the support period; 31(3): where other Union acts also require technical documentation, a single set may cover all — [cyberresilienceact.eu full text, Art 31](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html)
- Annex VII elements (as applicable to the product) — [Streamlex, CRA Annex VII](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-vii/):
  1. General description: (a) intended purpose; (b) software versions affecting compliance with the essential requirements; (c) for hardware, photographs or illustrations of external features, markings and internal layout; (d) user information and instructions per Annex II.
  2. Design, development, production and vulnerability handling: (a) design/development information incl. drawings/schemes where applicable and a description of the system architecture showing how software components build on or feed into each other; (b) vulnerability-handling process information incl. the software bill of materials, the coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy, evidence of a contact address for reporting vulnerabilities, and a description of the technical solutions for secure distribution of updates; (c) production and monitoring process information and evidence of validation.
  3. Cybersecurity risk assessment (Art 13), including how the Annex I Part I requirements apply.
  4. Information taken into account to determine the support period (Art 13(8)).
  5. List of harmonised standards (fully/partly applied, OJ-cited), common specifications (Art 27) or Reg. 2019/881 schemes (Art 27(8)) applied; where not applied, descriptions of solutions adopted to meet Annex I Parts I and II and other technical specifications used; where partly applied, which parts.
  6. Test reports on conformity of the product and of the vulnerability-handling processes with Annex I Parts I and II.
  7. Copy of the EU declaration of conformity.
  8. Where applicable, the SBOM, upon a reasoned request from a market surveillance authority, if necessary to check compliance with Annex I.
- Retention — Art 13(13): manufacturers keep the technical documentation and EU declaration at the disposal of market surveillance authorities "for at least 10 years" after placing on the market "or for the support period, whichever is longer" — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 13](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_13.html); the same 10-years-or-support-period rule appears in Annex VIII Module A pt 4.2, Module B pt 10, Module C pt 3.2, Module H pts 5.2 and 6 — [Streamlex, CRA Annex VIII](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-viii/)
- Art 13(12): before placing on the market, draw up the Art 31 technical documentation, carry out or have carried out the chosen Art 32 procedure, then draw up the Art 28 declaration and affix the Art 30 CE marking — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 13](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_13.html)
- Art 13(8): support period "shall be at least five years" unless the product is expected to be in use for less — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 13](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_13.html)
- Art 13(18): user information/instructions in paper or electronic form in "a language which can be easily understood by users and market surveillance authorities", kept available for at least 10 years or the support period, whichever longer; 13(19): support-period end date (at least month and year) clearly stated at purchase; 13(22): on "a reasoned request from a market surveillance authority", manufacturers provide the information and documentation necessary to demonstrate conformity "in a language which can be easily understood by that authority" (paper or electronic form) — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 13](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_13.html)
- Commission FAQ s.6.6: technical documentation "can be written in any language" but if required by an authority must be provided in a language it easily understands; it is not only an internal deliverable; no obligation to share it with customers or the public except FOSS Art 32(5) self-attesters — [Commission FAQ s.6.6](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission guidance pt 113: regardless of whether updates are substantial modifications, manufacturers must keep the risk assessment and technical documentation "accurate, complete and continuously up to date" (Arts 13(7), 31(2)) — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pt 113](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance pts 37-39 (products designed before 11 Dec 2027 but placed on the market after): evidence in Annex VII item 6 need not include test results covering the original design phases where the risk assessment shows existing measures are adequate; tests may be grouped across product families; vulnerability-handling (Part II) evidence is still required — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252, pts 33-39](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance pts 174-177: single risk assessment, single technical documentation and single conformity assessment allowed for product families with the same architecture, security-relevant design, intended purpose and risks; cybersecurity-relevant differences (interfaces, software stacks, update mechanisms, remote connectivity) must be reflected — [Commission guidance annex, C(2026) 5252](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Simplified technical documentation form for micro/small enterprises (Art 33(5)): see KQ8 — no implementing act adopted.

### Inferences
- Minimum artefact set per product (Module A): Annex VII dossier (items 1-7, with SBOM held ready for item 8), signed Annex V declaration (or Annex VI + URL), CE mark on product/packaging/declaration/website, user information with support-period end date, all retained ≥10 years or the support period. Module B+C adds: application file (incl. no-other-NB declaration), EU-type examination certificate + annexes/additions, notified-body evaluation report, change-notification records. Module H adds: quality-system documentation, approved-change records, notified-body decisions and audit reports, NB number on the CE mark.
- Because the SBOM is in Annex VII twice (item 2(b) as part of the process description and item 8 on reasoned request), teams should maintain a machine-readable SBOM per software version listed under item 1(b).

### Gaps
- The CRA text read does not specify SBOM format or depth (the Commission factpage and FAQ were silent on an SBOM implementing act; Art 13(24) empowers the Commission to specify format/elements by implementing act — not verified in text read here).

## KQ7. Chapter IV notified bodies (applies since 11 June 2026): procedure, who is notified, accreditation, capacity, lead times

### Takeaway
As of the latest verifiable checks (late June, 19 July, 30 Aug, 7-8 Sept 2026) no CRA notified body appeared in NANDO/SMCS; Member States are only required to "strive" for sufficient bodies by 11 Dec 2026; accreditation is against EN ISO/IEC 17065 (Modules B+C) and EN ISO/IEC 17021-1 (Module H), notification runs through NANDO with a 2-week (accredited) or 2-month (non-accredited) objection window, and commentators estimate 12-18 months for a body to complete accreditation plus notification.

### Cited Findings

**Legal framework**
- Art 35(1): Member States notify the Commission and other Member States of bodies authorised to perform CRA conformity assessments; 35(2): Member States shall strive to ensure, by 11 December 2026, "a sufficient number of notified bodies" in the Union to avoid bottlenecks and market-entry barriers — [Springlex, CRA Art 35](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-35/)
- Art 36: each Member State designates a notifying authority responsible for assessing, designating, notifying and monitoring conformity assessment bodies; it may entrust assessment to its national accreditation body (Reg. 765/2008) — [Commission FAQ s.7.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307); [Commission, CRA conformity assessment page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-conformity-assessment)
- Art 39 requirements (paragraphs 2-12): national-law legal personality; third-party independence from the assessed organisation/product; no involvement in design, development, manufacture, supply, import, distribution, installation, purchase, ownership, use or maintenance of assessed products, nor consultancy that conflicts with independence; integrity and competence, "free from all pressures and inducements, particularly financial"; capacity for every Annex VIII task notified, with personnel, documented reproducible procedures separating NB tasks from other activities, procedures scaled to undertaking size/sector/complexity/production; personnel training, knowledge of Annex I, harmonised standards, common specifications and Union legislation, ability to draw up certificates/records/reports; impartiality and remuneration independent of number/results of assessments; liability insurance (unless state liability); professional secrecy (except towards market surveillance authorities); participation in standardisation and the Art 51 coordination group; consistent, fair, proportionate terms with particular consideration of SMEs on fees — [Springlex, CRA Art 39](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-39/)

## KQ9. Timing: assessment before placing on the market; re-assessment on substantial modification; security updates

### Takeaway
Conformity assessment, declaration and CE marking must be completed before each placing on the market; a substantial modification makes the modified product a new placing on the market requiring (re)assessment focused on the changed parts, while security updates that reduce risk without changing intended purpose or adding new risks are generally not substantial modifications (Commission guidance pts 104-113).

### Cited Findings
- Art 13(12): technical documentation, conformity assessment, declaration and CE marking all "before placing a product with digital elements on the market" — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 13](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_13.html); Art 30(3): CE marking affixed before placing on the market — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com, Art 30](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_30.html)
- Art 69(2): products placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027 are subject to the CRA "only if, from that date, they are subject to a substantial modification"; 69(3): Art 14 reporting applies to all in-scope products regardless; 69(1): EU-type examination certificates/approval decisions on cybersecurity requirements under other Union harmonisation legislation (e.g. RED Delegated Regulation 2022/30) "shall remain valid until 11 June 2028" unless they expire earlier — [Springlex, CRA Art 69](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-69/); [Commission FAQ s.1.4, 7.1](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission FAQ s.7.2: the CRA applies to individual units, not types; units of a non-compliant legacy type cannot be placed on the market on/after 11 Dec 2027 even if the type was first placed earlier (10,000-router example) — [Commission FAQ s.7.2](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission FAQ s.6.2: substantial modifications "require a new assessment by the same or a different notified body" (Module B+C) with possible revision of the EU-type certificate; "Other modifications that do not affect the compliance ... are not subject to reassessment"; Module H scope extensions for new/substantially modified products need a new assessment by the same notified body but are streamlined; s.6.8: a new product version "might need a new declaration of conformity, especially when it implements a substantial modification" — [Commission FAQ s.6.2, 6.3, 6.8](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission guidance C(2026) 5252 (non-binding; Commission interpretation; formally adopted later once all language versions exist, and "It is only from that moment that it will apply") — [Commission Communication C(2026) 5252](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131455):
  - Pt 104: per recital 39 a product is substantially modified where a change alters the level of cybersecurity risk and that altered/additional risk was not considered in the risk assessment; assess case-by-case whether an update introduces new or increased risks.
  - Pt 105: new functionalities changing the product's intended purpose as a whole generally are substantial modifications (Examples 40-41: monitoring dashboard gaining machine control; personal-data organiser gaining automated profiling/decision-making).
  - Pt 106: functionalities already foreseen and assessed in the risk assessment, or that do not alter the risk profile, are not substantial modifications (Examples 42-43: group chat foreseen in design; pre-assessed automated control loops activated later).
  - Pt 107: the test is "not ... the scale or complexity of the change" but its potential adverse impact on the risk profile (Examples 44-45: a "remember me" persistent-login token feature, or an unencrypted diagnostics-log export, are substantial modifications).
  - Pt 108: "security updates are generally not to be regarded as substantial modifications"; a security update that does not modify intended purpose and introduces no new risks is not substantial "even where that update may introduce significant technical changes", including constraining functionality to mitigate vulnerabilities (Examples 46-48: buffer-overflow/auth-bypass fixes; tightening firewall rules, disabling ports, mandatory MFA where foreseen; disabling a deprecated algorithm for a supported stronger one).
  - Pt 109: a security update may still be a substantial modification if it changes intended purpose or "materially changes that product's boundaries or dependency structure" (Examples 49-50: moving local encryption to a remote service; replacing internal key management with a third-party external key service).
  - Pt 110: non-exhaustive four-question test — does the update (a) introduce new threat vectors (interfaces, channels, execution environments, external dependencies); (b) enable new attack scenarios; (c) change the likelihood of known attack scenarios; (d) change the potential impact of known attack scenarios.
  - Pt 111-112: all-negative answers (with risk-assessment assumptions still valid) indicate no substantial modification; otherwise reassess risks and whether essential requirements are still met.
  - Pt 113: regardless of qualification, keep the risk assessment and technical documentation up to date (Arts 13(7), 31(2)) and meet Annex I Part II during the support period.
  - Pt 116: a substantially modified product made available on the market is treated as a new product; making it available "constitutes a new placing on the market", whoever carries out the modification.
  - Pt 122-123: when the original manufacturer substantially modifies (incl. iterative software development) it remains the manufacturer; it "may reuse existing documentation and tests" for unaffected aspects; the conformity assessment "should focus on the substantially modified parts"; a third-party body should likewise focus on the modified parts and may reuse existing documentation and test results.
  - Pt 124: for products placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027, a later substantial modification by the original manufacturer does not by itself require bringing the whole product into full CRA compliance unless the modification negatively affects the product's cybersecurity as a whole; otherwise obligations are limited to the modified parts.
  - Pts 33-39: units of a pre-CRA design placed on the market after 11 Dec 2027 need not be redesigned if the risk assessment shows adequate existing measures, but still need the conformity assessment, declaration and CE marking; test evidence for original design phases is not required where the risk assessment shows adequacy; Part II vulnerability-handling evidence is required.
- Commission FAQ s.1.4 example: a 2028 bug-fix update to a mid-2027 smart TV is not a substantial modification; a 2029 update enabling smart-home control is — [Commission FAQ s.1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Art 13(10): where subsequent substantially modified software versions are placed on the market, the manufacturer may meet Annex I Part II point (2) (remediation incl. security updates) only for the last version, if users of earlier versions can access the latest version free of charge and without additional hardware/software adjustment costs — [Commission FAQ s.4.3.2 quoting Art 13(10)](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission guidance s.5.1 (per table of contents) addresses "Substantial modifications and the support period"; a vendor summary states substantial modifications do not automatically reset the support period — [turingpoint (vendor commentary, 11 Sept 2026)](https://turingpoint.de/en/blog/cra-commission-guidance-2026/) (section not extracted here — see Gaps)
- Annex VIII Module B pt 7: manufacturer must inform the notified body of modifications that may affect conformity or certificate validity; such modifications require additional approval as an addition to the certificate; pt 8: notified body performs periodic audits of vulnerability-handling processes — [Streamlex, CRA Annex VIII](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-viii/)

### Inferences
- Operational rule set for release engineering: (1) classify each release against the pt 110 four-question test and record the answers in the risk-assessment change log; (2) pure security fixes and foreseen feature activations → no new conformity assessment, but update Annex VII and (if versioned) the declaration's software-version field; (3) new interfaces, dependencies, data flows or intended-purpose changes → treat as a new placing on the market: re-run the applicable Art 32 route (notified-body delta assessment under Module B, or quality-system scope extension under Module H), issue a new declaration, re-affix CE marking.
- Under Module B+C the notified body must be told of any change that "may affect" conformity even if the manufacturer concludes it is not substantial, since pt 7 is an information duty with the body deciding on additions.

### Gaps
- Guidance section 5.1 (support period consequences of substantial modification) was not extracted verbatim; the "does not reset the support period" reading comes from a vendor summary only.
- The Commission guidance is formally a draft approved for translation (C(2026) 5252 "will be formally adopted ... at a later date"); whether the final adoption occurred before 30 Oct 2026 could not be verified.

## Decision-tree summary (classification → route → artefacts)

**Step 1 — Identify the single core functionality** (Art 7(1)/8(1); guidance pts 139-145): main features without which the intended purpose cannot be met; ignore ancillary functions and integrated components; separately-sold modules are separate products. Record the chosen category (or "default") and rationale in Annex VII item 1(a).

**Step 2 — Classify** using IR 2025/2392 technical descriptions:
- Annex IV (critical): security-box hardware (HSMs, payment terminals, tachographs); smart meter gateways and advanced cryptoprocessing devices; smartcards/secure elements at AVA_VAN.4+ (incl. TPMs, eUICC).
- Annex III Class II: hypervisors/container runtimes; firewalls/IDS/IPS (incl. WAF, anti-spam gateways); tamper-resistant microprocessors/microcontrollers at AVA_VAN 2-3.
- Annex III Class I: the 19 categories listed in KQ1.
- Otherwise default.
- FOSS placed on the market in a commercial activity: classified the same way but Art 32(5) applies to the route.

**Step 3 — Route** (Art 32; guidance pts 147-152):
- Default → Module A (any stricter route optional).
- Class I → Module A only if an OJ-cited harmonised standard / common specification / Art 27(9)-listed scheme ("substantial"+) is applied in full and covers all core-functionality risks (none cited as of Sept/Oct 2026) → otherwise Module B+C or Module H.
- Class II → Module B+C, Module H, or EUCC-type certificate ("substantial"+) once an Art 27(9) delegated act names the scheme (planned Q4 2026).
- Critical → certificate if an Art 8(1) delegated act requires it (none adopted) → otherwise as Class II.
- FOSS in Annex III → any Art 32(1) route incl. Module A, provided Annex VII documentation is public at placing on the market.
- High-risk AI system → AI Act Art 43 route covers CRA cybersecurity (Art 12(2)) unless important/critical under AI Act Annex VI internal control → CRA route applies (Art 12(3)).

**Step 4 — Artefacts** (Arts 13(12)-(13), 28, 30, 31; Annexes V-VIII):
- All routes: Annex VII dossier (8 element groups incl. SBOM, CVD policy, contact address, secure-update mechanism, risk assessment, support-period rationale, standards list, test reports); Annex V declaration (or Annex VI + exact URL) accompanying the product; CE marking before placing on the market (software: on declaration or accompanying website); user information incl. support-period end date; retention ≥10 years or support period, whichever longer; documents supplied to authorities on reasoned request in a language they easily understand.
- Module B+C adds: single-NB application with no-other-NB declaration; EU-type examination certificate + annexes/additions; evaluation report; change notifications; periodic NB audits of vulnerability handling; Annex V item 7 (NB name/number, procedure, certificate ID).
- Module H adds: documented quality system (design, development, final inspection/testing, vulnerability handling) maintained through the support period; NB approval decision; periodic surveillance audit reports; approved-change records; NB identification number after the CE marking.
- Re-assessment trigger: substantial modification (pt 110 test) → new placing on the market → delta assessment on modified parts, new declaration, re-marking; security updates generally exempt.
