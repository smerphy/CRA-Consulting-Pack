# EU CRA Standards and Official-Guidance Landscape (status as of 30 October 2026)

Scope note: this file covers (1) the CRA harmonised-standards programme under standardisation request M/606, (2) common specifications (Art 27(2)/Art 28 context), (3) EU cybersecurity certification schemes (EUCC, EUCS, EU5G) and their CRA role, (4) the inventory of official guidance relevant to engineers, and (5) a mapping of existing international standards to CRA Annex I and Art 13 duties. Status statements are as of 30 October 2026; where a status could not be verified from a primary or reliable secondary source, this is flagged rather than guessed. Web-search budget was exhausted near the end of the research run; remaining gaps are listed per section.

Headline status (all sourced below):
- No CRA harmonised standard has been cited in the Official Journal; the Commission's harmonised-standards index has no entry for Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. Article 27(1) presumption of conformity is therefore not yet available for any product category.
- The original request C(2025) 618 (3 Feb 2025) set deadlines of 30 Aug 2026 (horizontal items 1 and 15), 30 Oct 2026 (vertical items 16-41) and 30 Oct 2027 (horizontal items 2-14). A draft amending decision notified 7 July 2026 would move these to 30 Oct 2026, 31 Dec 2026 and 30 Oct 2027 respectively; as of the latest trackers (17-26 Oct 2026) it had not been adopted/published.
- EN 40000-1-1 (vocabulary) and EN 40000-1-2 (principles) have passed CEN-CENELEC formal vote with ratification scheduled 2 Nov 2026 but are not intended for OJ citation; prEN 40000-1-3 (vulnerability handling) is the only horizontal standard expected to be cited and is post-enquiry/pre-formal-vote; prEN 40000-1-4 (generic security requirements) is still in drafting with a 30 Oct 2027 deadline.
- ETSI opened Public Enquiry on 17 vertical final drafts (EN 304 617-627 and 631-636) on 13 Aug 2026, closing mid-Sept to mid-Nov 2026.
- No Article 27(2) common specifications have been adopted or formally signalled; the Commission's public position remains that the ESO standards are "progressing well".
- EUCC (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482) is the only adopted EU scheme; a delegated act under Art 27(9) recognising EUCC for CRA presumption of conformity is planned for Q4 2026 per the Commission factpage but not adopted. EUCS and EU5G are not adopted.
- Commission Art 26 guidance C(2026) 5252 was published 27 July 2026; the Commission FAQ is at v1.4 (4 Sept 2026); ENISA's Single Reporting Platform went live 11 Sept 2026; BSI TR-03183-1 v1.0.0 was published 5 Aug 2026.

## Key Question 1: Exact content and deadlines of the standardisation request M/606 (C(2025) 618), and the 2026 amendment

### Takeaway
C(2025) 618 of 3 Feb 2025 requested 41 European standards (15 horizontal, 26 vertical) with deadlines of 30 Aug 2026 (items 1 and 15), 30 Oct 2026 (items 16-41) and 30 Oct 2027 (items 2-14); the ESOs accepted it on 3 April 2025. A draft amending implementing decision notified on 7 July 2026 (feedback to 3 Aug 2026) would push items 1 and 15 to 30 Oct 2026 and items 16-41 to 31 Dec 2026, but as of the most recent checks (17-26 Oct 2026) it had not been adopted and the Commission's standardisation page still cites the original C(2025) 618.

### Cited Findings
- The request is Commission Implementing Decision C(2025) 618 of 3 February 2025, signed by EVP Henna Virkkunen, addressed to CEN, Cenelec and ETSI; it expires on 30 November 2027 — [C(2025) 618 text (IBF mirror PDF)](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/Dateidownloads/standardisation-request-cra.pdf)
- Article 1(2): entries 1-15 and 39-41 are to be developed by CEN and/or Cenelec; entries 16-38 jointly by CEN, Cenelec and ETSI. Article 2(3): entries 39, 40 and 41 (security boxes, smart meter gateways, smartcards/secure elements) must be developed "in a restricted setting" with EU-established participants — [C(2025) 618](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/Dateidownloads/standardisation-request-cra.pdf)
- Article 4: ESOs report every six months until all drafts reach Enquiry, then annually; first joint report due 10 months after notification; final joint report by 30 October 2027; ESOs must promptly report deadline/scope concerns — [C(2025) 618](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/Dateidownloads/standardisation-request-cra.pdf)
- Recital 18 records that the JRC and ENISA mapping of existing standards was shared with the ESOs to "carry out a detailed gap analysis" — [C(2025) 618](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/Dateidownloads/standardisation-request-cra.pdf)
- CEN, CENELEC and ETSI officially accepted the request on 3 April 2025, committing to deliver harmonised standards "at least one year before" CRA application; responsible bodies named: CEN-CLC/JTC 13 WG 9 and WG 6, CEN/TC 224 WG 17, CLC/TC 65X WG 3, CLC/TC 47X, and a new ETSI group "EUSR" within TC CYBER — [CEN-CENELEC news, 1 May 2025](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2025/newsletter/ots-62-cra/)
- The Commission FAQ (6.10) summarises: 15 horizontal standards clustered by ESOs into 3 deliverables — (a) designing/developing/producing with appropriate cybersecurity based on risk, by 30 Aug 2026; (b) Annex I Part I properties, by 30 Oct 2027; (c) vulnerability handling, by 30 Aug 2026 — and 26 vertical standards (31 ESO deliverables) by 30 Oct 2026 covering Annex III and IV categories — [Commission FAQ v1.4 PDF](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- The CEN-CENELEC-ETSI Work Programme V1 (2025-04-02) assigns deadlines 2026/08/30 to JT013089 (principles) and JT013090 (vulnerability handling), 2027/10/30 to JT013091 (generic security requirements, lines 2-14), and 2026/10/30 to all vertical lines 16-41; it also lists supporting deliverables EN IEC 62443-4-1:2018/prAA, 62443-4-2:2019/prAA, 62443-3-3:2019/prAA, a TR on threats and security objectives, and TS/TR on applying EN 18037 — [M/606 Work Programme PDF](https://www.cencenelec.eu/media/CEN-CENELEC/News/Newsletters/2025/m_606_work_programme_final.pdf)
- JTC 13 WG 9 convenor's September 2025 slide deck records the request as issued 2025-02-03 and accepted 2025-03-02 (note: conflicts with CEN-CENELEC's 3 April 2025 acceptance date), and that the PT2 generic-requirements DAV target (Jan 2027) already fell after the then-applicable deadline — [JTC13 WG9 Update, Ben Kokx, 17 Sept 2025](https://www.semi.org/sites/semi.org/files/2025-09/JTC13_WG9_Update_Ben_Kokx_SEMI_20250916a.pdf)
- Draft amending decision: Commission notification dated 7 July 2026 under Art 12 of Reg 1025/2012, feedback deadline 3 Aug 2026, contact CNECT-CRA@ec.europa.eu; marked "(2026) XXX draft", not adopted; Article 1 replaces Annex I in full; Article 2 says the amendment lapses if CEN does not accept within one month — [Draft amending decision PDF (IBF mirror)](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/cra-standardization-request-amendment.pdf)
- Recital 3 of the draft: the ESOs' "joint semestrial report of 3 December 2025" informed the Commission of timing impacts; the Commission considers a "limited adjustment of the timeline" appropriate "due to procedural and technical complexities" — [Draft amending decision](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/cra-standardization-request-amendment.pdf)
- New Annex I deadlines in the draft: item 1 (risk-based design/development/production) 30/10/2026; items 2-14 (13 Annex I Part I property standards) 30/10/2027; item 15 (vulnerability handling) 30/10/2026; items 16-41 (all verticals, from identity management through smartcards/secure elements) 31/12/2026 — [Draft amending decision](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/cra-standardization-request-amendment.pdf)
- The amendment keeps the 41-item list unchanged, including item 36 "firewalls, intrusion detection and/or prevention systems, including specifically those intended for industrial use" and item 40 smart meter gateways "and other devices for advanced security purposes, including for secure cryptoprocessing" — [Draft amending decision](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/cra-standardization-request-amendment.pdf)
- As of 17 Oct 2026 the draft amendment "has not been adopted by the Commission"; no amending decision number exists — [craevidence tracker, updated 17 Oct 2026](https://craevidence.com/cra-compliance/harmonised-standards-status)
- As of 26 Oct 2026, "several observers reported it had not been adopted"; the Commission's standardisation page still cites C(2025) 618 (last updated 31 July 2026) — [Blue Panther status, 26 Oct 2026](https://panther.blue/en/insights/cra-harmonised-standards-status/)
- Independent analysis frames the amendment as "a formal acknowledgement that the original 2026 pace was too fast" — [cyberresilienceact.eu, 30 July 2026 (upd. 11 Sept)](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-standardisation-deadlines-pushed-back-two-months.html)
- The Commission's own implementation factpage (last updated 27 July 2026) still shows "first standardisation deliverables (horizontal and product-specific)" for Q3 2026 and "more standardisation deliverables" for 30 Oct 2027 — [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation)
- A second CRA standardisation request is in preparation: the Commission told the Expert Group on 11 June 2026 it is "working on a second standardisation request" per the 2026 Annual Union Work Programme, to address products outside Annex III/IV so more products can benefit from presumption of conformity; the Expert Group will be consulted — [CRA Expert Group 5th meeting minutes, 10 June 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/expert-groups-register/core/api/front/document/135392/download)
- Industry classifies the deliverables as Type A (principles, Annex I lead-in), Type B (horizontal: generic requirements and vulnerability handling) and Type C (vertical product standards); Type C must align with A and B — [SGS SafeGuardS 131/25, 16 Sept 2025](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2025/09/safeguards-13125-update-on-developments-relating-to-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act)

### Inferences
- Because the amendment is unadopted, the legally operative deadlines on 30 Oct 2026 remain those of C(2025) 618: items 1 and 15 were due 30 Aug 2026 and items 16-41 are due today (30 Oct 2026). On the evidence, neither horizontal deadline was met with a ratified EN intended for citation (EN 40000-1-2 ratifies 2 Nov 2026 and is not slated for citation; prEN 40000-1-3 has not reached formal vote), and the verticals cannot complete ETSI/CEN approval by 30 Oct 2026 given enquiry windows running to mid-Nov 2026.
- The draft amendment's own Article 2 (lapse if CEN does not accept within a month) and the Art 22 committee opinion requirement explain part of the delay in adoption; the practical effect of non-adoption is nil for manufacturers since neither old nor new delivery dates change CRA application dates.
- A discrepancy exists in acceptance dates (2 March 2025 per the WG 9 convenor vs 3 April 2025 per CEN-CENELEC); the CEN-CENELEC institutional date should be preferred.

### Gaps
- The full Annex I/Annex II text of the original C(2025) 618 was not retrieved (the IBF mirror omits annexes); the original deadlines are reconstructed from the FAQ, the work programme and the amendment (which shows only new dates).
- The ESOs' joint semestrial report of 3 Dec 2025 was not located; its content on delays is known only through the amendment's recital 3.
- Whether the amending decision was adopted between 26 and 30 Oct 2026 could not be verified (search budget exhausted; the Commission's standardisation page could not be fetched directly).
- No EUR-Lex/OJ publication of C(2025) 618 itself was located (implementing decisions addressed to ESOs are typically not OJ-published).

### Inferences
- The CRA December 2027 timeline is at material risk for presumption-of-conformity purposes: with the earliest OJ citations realistically in 2027, manufacturers of important Class I products cannot yet plan on Module A self-assessment under Art 32(2) and may need to book notified-body capacity (Module B+C or H) that itself does not yet exist (NANDO empty as of Sept 2026, see KQ4).
- The CENELEC OT track is the laggard: prEN 50770 parts are still at draft/PWI stage and depend on finishing EN IEC 62443-4-2/A11:2026 first, making the 31 Dec 2026 vertical deadline implausible for items 20b/21b/22b/25b/27b/36b.
- The renumbering of TC 47X deliverables (prEN 5076x enquiry numbers to EN 4000x-3) means trackers using either numbering refer to the same work items; the report-writer should present both.

### Gaps
- No formal-vote dates or FprEN numbers were found for prEN 40000-1-3, prEN 40000-10/-11, prEN 18330 or the TC 47X standards; HAS assessment outcomes (positive/negative) are not public.
- No official CEN-CENELEC press release in 2026 stating a delay was located (the CEN-CENELEC CRA topic page returned 404); the delay acknowledgement rests on the Commission's draft amendment recital and ESO webinar materials.

## Key Question 3: ETSI deliverables (EN 304 6xx series, EN 303 645 updates) and their CRA role

### Takeaway
ETSI TC CYBER's EUSR group opened Public Enquiry (first phase of ETSI's EN approval procedure) on 17 final-draft verticals (EN 304 617-627 and 631-636) on 13 Aug 2026, with windows closing between mid-Sept and mid-Nov 2026 (e.g. EN 304 627 routers 16 Jul-20 Sep 2026; EN 304 626 operating systems 26 Aug-25 Oct 2026); an 18th deliverable EN 304 642 (telecom network functions) is also tracked. The drafts share a common clause spine mapping to Annex I Part I, include an Annex A CRA mapping, and (per Applus) the EN 304 627 enquiry draft did not yet normatively reference EN 40000-1-3/-1-2. ## Key Question 4: The HAS consultant assessment and OJ citation process, and why it matters (presumption of conformity; Module A for important Class I)

### Takeaway
Under CEN-CENELEC rules a homegrown harmonised EN undergoes mandatory HAS-consultant assessments at Mature Draft (pre-enquiry) and pre-Formal Vote stages (max 4 per work item), must use the Common Checklist and an Annex ZZ mapping clauses to legal requirements, and is then assessed by the Commission under Reg 1025/2012 before OJ citation (Art 27(6) CRA). Citation matters because Art 27(1) presumption of conformity only attaches to cited standards, and under Art 32(2) an important Class I product may use Module A self-assessment only if it fully applies a cited harmonised standard (or common specification or substantial-level certification scheme) covering its core-functionality risks; otherwise Module B+C or H with a notified body is mandatory — and no CRA notified body was listed in NANDO as of Sept 2026.

### Cited Findings
- HAS process (CENELEC BOSS): the Mature Draft HAS assessment is mandatory for homegrown hENs; consultant comments must be addressed before Enquiry; TBs must use the Common Checklist (CCMC rejects drafts without it); Annex ZZ is an informative annex mapping clauses to EU legal requirements in the order of the legal text; a standard covering several acts needs one Annex ZZ each; typical schedule is 50 weeks NWI-to-Enquiry and 18 weeks Enquiry-to-Formal Vote — [CENELEC BOSS: Drafting hENs for OJEU citation](https://boss.cenelec.eu/homegrowndeliverables/en/pages/enforojeu/)
- CEN training (Dec 2024): Mature Draft assessment mandatory, Pre-Formal Vote assessment mandatory, Last Confirmatory Assessment "should become exceptional"; maximum 4 assessments per work item; published standards cannot be assessed; quality check covers normative references and Annex ZA/ZZ — [CEN Experts training: hEN development for OJEU citation](https://experts.cen.eu/media/Experts/Trainings/2024/3_harmonized-standards-development-for-ojeu-citation.pdf)
- Art 27 CRA structure: (1) harmonised standards whose references are published confer presumption; (2) Commission may adopt implementing acts establishing common specifications only if a request was not accepted, standards not delivered by deadline or not matching the request, and no reference is published nor expected "within a reasonable period"; (3) Commission must first inform the Art 22 committee; (5) common specifications confer presumption; (6) overlapping common specifications are repealed once a hEN is cited; (8) certificates under a Reg 2019/881 scheme confer presumption to the extent covered; (9) Commission delegated acts specify which schemes may be used, and a certificate at least "substantial" removes third-party assessment for the corresponding requirements — [Springlex CRA Art 27](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-27/)
- Commission FAQ 6.1: Module A (Annex VIII Part I) is available for default-category products; for important Class I "if a harmonised standard has been applied in accordance with Article 32(2)"; and for FOSS important Class I/II with public technical documentation (Art 32(5)) — [Commission FAQ v1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- Commission FAQ 6.2: Module B+C or H is mandatory for important Class I "if a harmonised standard has not been applied", for all important Class II, and for critical products "unless the use of a European cybersecurity certification scheme is made mandatory" under Art 8(1) — [Commission FAQ v1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- Commission FAQ 6.5: the CRA "does not mandate the use of any specific evaluation methodology"; market surveillance authorities "might consider applying the same methodology" as the manufacturer "especially if that methodology is part of harmonised standard"; cybersecurity testing "is not deterministic" — [Commission FAQ v1.4](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/122331)
- Commission guidance C(2026) 5252, paras 148-151: Class I important products need third-party assessment "only if the manufacturer has not applied or has applied only in part" relevant harmonised standards (references published in the OJEU), common specifications or certification at least "substantial"; to be eligible for internal control "(i) all the applicable requirements of a relevant harmonised standard need to be applied; and (ii) the standard's scope needs to cover at least all the cybersecurity risks associated to that product's core functionality"; footnote 25 confirms the same applies to Art 27(2) common specifications and Art 27(9) schemes — [Commission guidance annex C(2026) 5252](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Chapter IV (Arts 35-51, notified bodies) applied from 11 June 2026; NANDO "is effectively empty for the CRA" as of the September 2026 check; Art 35(2) target of sufficient notified bodies is 11 Dec 2026 — [cyberresilienceact.eu, 26 June 2026 (upd. 11 Sept)](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-notified-bodies-rules-apply-11-june-2026.html)
- Commission (11 June 2026): all Member States intend to rely on accreditation; some 300 CAB representatives attended an April 2026 event; ENISA published that week a document on technical competence requirements for CABs; a gap analysis comparing CABs notified under EUCC and the RED delegated act was to follow; a body "comparable to RED CA" would be established once notified bodies operate; Module B "is where most requests are expected" — [CRA EG 5th minutes](https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/expert-groups-register/core/api/front/document/135392/download)
- Precedent for citation with restrictions: Implementing Decision (EU) 2025/138 (28 Jan 2025) cited EN 18031-1/-2/-3:2024 under the RED with restrictions — no presumption where the user may choose not to set a password (clauses 6.2.5.1/6.2.5.2), for toys/childcare without parental controls (EN 18031-2 cl. 6.1.3-6.1.6), and for EN 18031-3 cl. 6.3.2.4 — [Ezurio analysis, 7 Feb 2025](https://www.ezurio.com/resources/blog/understanding-eu-commission-implementation-of-decision-eu-2025-138-new-harmonized-standards-and-restrictions)
- Without a cited standard "there is no presumption of conformity under Article 27, and the Article 32 self-assessment route for Class I products is constrained" — [Interlynk, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.interlynk.io/resources/cra-harmonised-standards-delay)

### Inferences
- The EN 18031/RED precedent shows the Commission will cite with restrictions where HAS consultants find gaps; CRA verticals built on EN 18031-style decision trees (or dropping them, as EN 40000-1-4 does) should expect similar scrutiny of "applicability" logic.
- For an engineer today, the operative consequence is that every Annex III product is on a third-party path until the first OJ citations appear (realistically 2027), and the notified-body market will not exist before late 2026 at best; this is the practical "why it matters".

### Gaps
- The HAS assessment reports for CRA drafts (e.g., the 3 Aug 2026 pre-FV submission of prEN 40000-1-3) are not public; outcomes unknown.
- No source quantified the typical interval from ESO ratification (DAV) to OJ citation under Reg 1025/2012 for recent NLF acts.

## Key Question 6: EUCC status, certified categories, mapping of CC/EUCC certificates to CRA (Arts 27(8)-(9), 32), and Commission plans for delegated acts

### Takeaway
EUCC (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482 of 31 Jan 2024, amended by (EU) 2024/3144, applicable 27 Feb 2025) is the only adopted EU scheme; ENISA's register showed 103 valid EUCC certificates (as filtered) issued by eight CBs, mostly smartcards/secure elements and cryptographic modules, with the first "substantial"-level certificate (DEKRA, GMV GNSS Cryptographic Module) on 23 July 2025. The Art 27(9) delegated act recognising EUCC is planned for Q4 2026 (Commission factpage); a content paper was promised to the Expert Group in June 2026; the Commission said in Feb 2025 it was not working on Art 8 delegated acts to make certification mandatory for critical products.

## Key Question 7: EUCS (cloud) and EU5G scheme status, and the Cybersecurity Act revision

### Takeaway
EUCS remains an unadopted draft (first draft Dec 2020; sovereignty criteria removed in the March 2024 V1.0.413; ECCG vote postponed April 2024 and never held), and the Commission's Cloud and AI Development Act proposal COM(2026) 502 of 3 June 2026 confirms it "has not yet been adopted" with work to resume alongside the Cybersecurity Act revision (CSA2, proposed 20 Jan 2026); EU5G is likewise not adopted (ENISA consulted on eUICC specifications under EUCC in June-Sept 2024). ## Key Question 9: Standards-to-CRA mapping (coverage of Annex I Part I, Part II and Art 13 duties; residual gaps)

### Takeaway
Official mapping work (JRC-ENISA 2024; ETSI TR 103 990 2024; ENISA EUCC study 2025) concludes that every Annex I requirement is at least partially covered by some existing standard but no single standard covers all, with the thinnest coverage in Part II process duties (SBOM, free/timely updates with user notification, information sharing on third-party components, testing cadence), in hardware-level and generic (non-IoT, non-IACS) products, and in practical implementation detail; sector standards (IEC 62443 for IACS, EN 303 645 for consumer IoT, EN 18031 for RED radio equipment) carry scope limits and EN 18031 is cited only under the RED with restrictions. Art 13 process duties (risk assessment and documentation, due diligence on components, support period determination, reporting to the SRP, user information, technical documentation, CE marking) are largely outside product-security standards and are addressed by the CRA text, Commission guidance/FAQ and BSI TR-03183.

Official gap findings (cross-cutting):
- JRC-ENISA overall: "there is not a single standard capable of covering all the requirements"; all requirements at least partially covered; horizontal standards most relevant, with IEC 62443 (IACS) and IoT standards as sector exceptions; weakest areas are hardware-level measures, practical implementation detail, and generic (non-IoT, non-industrial) products — [JRC-ENISA mapping](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-11/Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20Requirements%20Standards%20Mapping%20-%20final_with_identifiers_0.pdf); [JRC presentation](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/all_files/NaiFovino.pdf)
- JRC-ENISA Part I specifics: 2(a) standards cover detection not fixing; 2(b) high-level only; 2(c) no detail on secure update mechanisms or user notification; 2(g) impact on other networks only minimal IoT provisions; 2(i) attack-surface controls theoretical; 2(k) defence-in-depth/sandboxing gaps; 2(l) event logging not explicit in EN 303 645 — [JRC-ENISA mapping](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-11/Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20Requirements%20Standards%20Mapping%20-%20final_with_identifiers_0.pdf)
- JRC-ENISA Part II specifics: SBOM — ISO/IEC 27036 does not address SBOM creation/exchange (SPDX, CycloneDX, ISO/IEC 5962 complementary); remediation and testing — "no single standard"; disclosure — timelines/formats lacking; information sharing on third-party components — not comprehensively covered by 29147/30111; secure distribution — no explicit hashes/code-signing; free updates and notification methods — undefined in 30111/27002/62443-4-1 — [JRC-ENISA mapping](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-11/Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20Requirements%20Standards%20Mapping%20-%20final_with_identifiers_0.pdf)
- ETSI TR 103 990: no ETSI SBOM standard; weak test coverage; side-channel mitigations largely unstandardised; risk assessment guidance incomplete (proposes TVRA plus AVA_VAN); need to distinguish "known" vs "exploitable" vulnerabilities — [ETSI TR 103 990](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_tr/103900_103999/103990/01.01.01_60/tr_103990v010101p.pdf)
- Commission guidance 9.2.2 and 9.2.3 now address "known exploitable vulnerabilities" and "effective and regular tests and reviews", and 9.2.1 upstream reporting/sharing of fixes — partially closing TR 103 990's definitional gap through guidance rather than standards — [Guidance annex contents](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- The 2026 Commission guidance adds a RED-era precedent: a cited standard covering only core functionality gives presumption only for that scope; ancillary functions need separate coverage or other means (paras 149-154, Figures 6-8) — [Guidance annex](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

### Inferences
- For an engineering team today, the best-covered path is: IEC 62443-4-1 (or SSDF) for process plus ISO/IEC 29147/30111 for disclosure/handling plus BSI TR-03183-2 for SBOM plus EN 303 645/EN 18031/62443-4-2 for product controls by sector — which is essentially the architecture the ESOs have adopted (EN 40000-1-2 principles, 40000-1-3 normatively on 29147/30111, 40000-1-4 on EN 18031 plus controls, verticals on 62443/303 645 heritage). Aligning now to those base documents minimises rework when the CRA standards are cited.
- Art 13 duties that no listed standard covers and that must be met from the CRA text and guidance: determining and publishing the support period (Art 13(8)-(9)), SRP reporting (Art 14), due diligence on third-party components with CRA-specific conclusions (Art 13(5)), Annex VII technical documentation, Annex II user information, EU declaration of conformity and CE marking, and the "substantial modification" assessment for software updates.

### Gaps
- No official (EU) mapping of NIST SSDF, NIST IR 8259, UL 2900, IEC 81001-5-1, ISO/IEC 18974 or ISO/IEC 5230 to the CRA was found; the table's entries for these rely on the standards' own scope descriptions plus the CRA text, and should be labelled as researcher assessment rather than official findings.
- The JRC-ENISA report's final sections were read only in part (the report is 122k characters; Part II items 6-8 and conclusions were covered, but the full standards list in the reference section was truncated).
- EN 303 645 clause 6 provision counts and any ETSI plan to update EN 303 645 for CRA alignment were not verified.
