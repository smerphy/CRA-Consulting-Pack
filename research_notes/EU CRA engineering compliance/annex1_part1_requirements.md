# EU CRA Annex I Part I — Essential "product properties" requirements and their translation into engineering controls and evidence (as of 30 October 2026)

Scope note: Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (the Cyber Resilience Act, CRA). Annex I Part I = "Cybersecurity requirements relating to the properties of products with digital elements". The official numbering is **point (1)** (the general risk-based requirement) and **point (2)** with lettered sub-points **(a) to (m)** — 13 specific requirements. The requirement often described as "no known exploitable vulnerabilities" is **point (2)(a)**, not a separate point. Some secondary sources (notably the 2024 JRC-ENISA mapping study, written against the pre-adoption text) use an older numbering ("1, 2, 3(a)-(k)"); this is flagged where relevant.

Sourcing note: EUR-Lex itself could not be fetched to the Annex (the HTML/PDF pages are too long for the fetch tool and direct download was blocked), so the verbatim Annex I wording below is taken from two authoritative reproductions: (i) Annex B of the **ENISA Secure by Design and Default Playbook v1.0 (July 2026)**, which reproduces the Annex I text in full with IDs "ANNEX-1.PT1.2.a" etc.; and (ii) Table A-1 of the **draft ETSI EN 304 621 v1.0.4 (20 July 2026)**, which quotes each Annex I Part I item. Both agree word for word. Article 13(3) is quoted via a compliance-library mirror (cyberday.ai) and Article 13(1)-(8) via streamlex/springlex paraphrases; recitals via the EUR-Lex HTML (recitals 1-122 were readable in two fetches).

## Key Question 1 — Full structure of Annex I Part I; which items are absolute vs qualified; what the recitals say about "known exploitable vulnerabilities"

### Takeaway
Annex I Part I has one absolute, overarching obligation (point (1): an "appropriate level of cybersecurity based on the risks") and thirteen specific obligations in point (2)(a)-(m) which are **all** introduced by a single chapeau — "On the basis of the cybersecurity risk assessment referred to in Article 13(2) and where applicable" — so every lettered item is formally risk-qualified; in addition (c) and (m) contain a second, internal "where applicable"/"where such data can be transferred" qualifier, and (b) carries a tailor-made-product carve-out. The recitals (54, 55, 38) treat (2)(a) as the central objective of the risk assessment: avoid placing products on the market with known exploitable vulnerabilities, with all available security patches applied at the moment of placing on the market.

### Cited Findings

**Verbatim structure (ENISA Playbook Annex B, reproducing Annex I; cross-checked against ETSI EN 304 621 Table A-1):**
- Point (1): products "shall be designed, developed and produced in such a way that they ensure an appropriate level of cybersecurity based on the risks." — [ENISA Secure by Design and Default Playbook v1.0, Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf); [draft ETSI EN 304 621 Table A-1](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-621/-/raw/main/EN-304-621.md)
- Point (2) chapeau: "On the basis of the cybersecurity risk assessment referred to in Article 13(2) and where applicable, products with digital elements shall:" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(a): be made available on the market "without known exploitable vulnerabilities" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(b): be made available with a "secure by default configuration", unless otherwise agreed between manufacturer and business user for a tailor-made product, "including the possibility to reset the product to its original state" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(c): ensure vulnerabilities can be addressed through security updates, "including, where applicable, through automatic security updates" installed within an appropriate timeframe, enabled as a default setting, with "a clear and easy-to-use opt-out mechanism", through notification of available updates to users, and the option to temporarily postpone them — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(d): protection from unauthorised access by appropriate control mechanisms, "including but not limited to authentication, identity or access management systems", and "report on possible unauthorised access" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(e): protect confidentiality of stored, transmitted or otherwise processed data, personal or other, "such as by encrypting relevant data at rest or in transit" by state of the art mechanisms, and by other technical means — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(f): protect integrity of stored/transmitted/processed data, "commands, programs and configuration" against unauthorised manipulation or modification, "and report on corruptions" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(g): process only data "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary" for the intended purpose "(data minimisation)" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(h): protect availability of "essential and basic functions, also after an incident", including resilience and mitigation against denial-of-service attacks — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(i): minimise the negative impact by the products themselves or connected devices on availability of services provided by other devices or networks — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(j): be designed, developed and produced "to limit attack surfaces, including external interfaces" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(k): be designed, developed and produced to reduce the impact of an incident "using appropriate exploitation mitigation mechanisms and techniques" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(l): provide security related information "by recording and monitoring relevant internal activity", including access to or modification of data, services or functions, "with an opt-out mechanism for the user" — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- (2)(m): possibility for users to "securely and easily remove on a permanent basis" all data and settings and, "where such data can be transferred" to other products or systems, ensure this is done securely — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)

**Qualifiers — which items are "absolute" and which are risk-qualified:**
- Only point (1) stands outside the "on the basis of the cybersecurity risk assessment ... and where applicable" chapeau; every lettered item (a)-(m) sits under it. — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- Internal, second-level qualifiers: (c) "including, where applicable, through automatic security updates"; (m) "where such data can be transferred"; (b) "unless otherwise agreed between manufacturer and business user in relation to a tailor-made product". No other lettered item has an internal "where applicable". — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf); the Secure Controls Framework mapping confirms the same text for the chapeau and (c) — [SCF STRM EU CRA Annex I 2024](https://content.securecontrolsframework.com/strm/scf-strm-emea-eu-cyber-resilience-act-annex-i-2024.pdf)
- Article 13(3) requires the risk assessment to "indicate whether and, if so in what manner, the security requirements set out in Part I, point (2), of Annex I are applicable" and how they are implemented; it must also indicate how the manufacturer applies Part I point (1) and Part II. — [cyberday.ai, quoting Art 13(3)](https://www.cyberday.ai/requirement/cra-article-13-3-documenting-risk-assessment)
- Article 13(4): where a requirement is judged not applicable, the technical documentation must justify this; Recital 55 adds that residual risks relating to a non-applicable requirement must be addressed "by other means", e.g. limiting intended use or informing users. — [springlex Art 13 page](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/); [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- The draft ETSI EN 304 621 (network management systems) states its clause-5 requirements "are unconditional unless marked with conditional phrasing" and clause 5.1 maps use cases to low/medium/high levels per requirement set — i.e. the vertical standard itself does the "where applicable" selection. — [draft ETSI EN 304 621 clause 5.1](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-621/-/raw/main/EN-304-621.md)
- CEN-CENELEC PT2 (EN 40000-1-4) slides: applicability of control requirements "is handled by the regulatory risk assessment" and the Annex I→security-objective mapping "has no specific assessment criteria". — [CEN-CENELEC webinar slides, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.cencenelec.eu/media/CEN-CENELEC/Events/Webinars/2026/2026-03-05_cra_unlocked_pt2_stan4cr_slides.pdf)

**Recitals on "known exploitable vulnerabilities" and the risk assessment:**
- Recital 54: manufacturers should carry out the risk assessment to identify relevant requirements, with the goal of avoiding placing products on the market with known exploitable vulnerabilities; vulnerability-handling requirements apply throughout the support period; manufacturers must assess which Part I requirements apply. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Recital 38: essential requirements apply to each individual product at the moment of placing on the market; a product should have "all available security patches" at that point. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Recital 55: non-applicable requirements must be justified in the risk assessment within the technical documentation; residual risks addressed by other means. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Recital 37: unfinished software (betas) may be released for testing subject to a risk assessment. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Recital 64: products should be secure by default and security updates free of charge. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)

**How the draft harmonised standards operationalise (2)(a) in practice:**
- Draft ETSI EN 304 621 KEV_EXPLOIT-1: a product passes if it (i) has no known exploitable vulnerabilities, or (ii) none reported within the previous 14 days or within the disclosure period in its vulnerability-handling procedure, or (iii) remaining ones have public documentation of risk and mitigation. A vulnerability becomes "known" when published in the EU Vulnerability Database, an industry vulnerability database, or reported directly to the manufacturer. The draft does not define "exploitable". — [draft ETSI EN 304 621 clause 5.3](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-621/-/raw/main/EN-304-621.md)
- EN 304 621 Annex G.5.3: the product should be free of known exploitable vulnerabilities both when first made available **and** when the user first starts it; the post-release gap is addressed by secure updates. — [draft ETSI EN 304 621 Annex G](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-621/-/raw/main/EN-304-621.md)
- Draft ETSI EN 304 626 (operating systems) v0.0.11 (11 Dec 2025) frames TR-NKEV as "No known exploitable vulnerabilities at first use" and offers five mitigations: MI-KEVD (documented secure update before/at first use), MI-KEVA (automatic secure update by default before/at first use), MI-KEVM (documented list of KEVs and fixes/mitigations, compliant with prEN 40000-1-3), MI-KEVT (test every KEV to show mitigation), MI-SCAN (no findings from the three most comprehensive freely available scanners, or age-consistent/documented findings). Each mitigation has Preparation/Activities/Verdict/Evidence fields. — [draft ETSI EN 304 626 clause 5.2.2](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-626/-/raw/main/EN-304-626.md)
- The 2024 JRC-ENISA mapping found no standard requires fixing before release: ISO/IEC 18045 cites only penetration testing; ETSI EN 303 645 "has no explicit assessment requirement and no requirement to fix flaws before release". — [JRC-ENISA CRA Requirements Standards Mapping](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-11/Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20Requirements%20Standards%20Mapping%20-%20final_with_identifiers_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- Legally, every lettered item is risk-qualified, but in practice the drafting bodies treat (2)(a) (KEV), (2)(b) (secure defaults/reset), (2)(d) (access control) and (2)(l) (logging) as near-universal: EN 304 621 makes authentication/access control apply "regardless of use case or risk tier" and makes logging "essential for all products in all use cases and all risk levels"; the ETSI drafts hard-wire (2)(a) as a release gate. A manufacturer that marks any of these as "not applicable" should expect to carry a heavy justification burden under Art 13(4)/Recital 55.
- "Known exploitable vulnerabilities" is converging (in the ETSI drafts) on an operational definition tied to publication in the EUVD or an industry database (CVE/NVD), with a short grace window (14 days in EN 304 621) tied to the manufacturer's own CVD/vulnerability handling SLA, and with "documented mitigation" accepted in lieu of a fix. There is no official Commission or ENISA definition of "exploitable" yet (gap below).

### Gaps
- No official (Commission or ENISA) interpretive definition of "exploitable" (e.g., whether a KEV-catalogue listing, EPSS score, or public PoC is the test) was found; the Commission's July 2026 guidance C(2026) 5252 reportedly covers risk assessment but the annex PDF (ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456) returned as unreadable binary and the Commission press page failed to load, so its text on Annex I could not be verified directly.
- EUR-Lex itself could not be fetched to the Annex; verbatim wording is from ENISA's and ETSI's reproductions (which agree).

## Key Question 2 — Art 13(2)-(3) cybersecurity risk assessment: content, update, how it determines applicability, documentation (Annex VII)

### Takeaway
Article 13(2)-(4) make the cybersecurity risk assessment the hinge of Part I: it must be based on intended purpose, reasonably foreseeable use and conditions of use (operational environment, assets to protect), take account of expected time in use, be documented and "updated as appropriate" during the support period, state for each Part I point (2) item whether and how it applies and how it is implemented, and be included in the technical documentation (Annex VII item 3) with justifications for any non-applicable item. The Commission's July 2026 guidance (paras 156-163 per a security-firm summary) adds that cost or commercial strategy alone cannot justify leaving a risk unaddressed and that a vague disclaimer shifting an unresolved design problem to the customer does not suffice.

### Cited Findings
- Art 13(1): manufacturers must ensure products are "designed, developed and produced in accordance with the essential cybersecurity requirements" in Annex I Part I. — [springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(2): manufacturers must assess cybersecurity risks and use the outcome across planning, design, development, production, delivery and maintenance, "with a view to minimising cybersecurity risks". — [streamlex Art 13](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-13)
- Art 13(3) (quoted): the assessment "shall be documented and updated as appropriate during a support period"; it "shall comprise at least an analysis of cybersecurity risks based on the intended purpose and reasonably foreseeable use, as well as the conditions of use, such as the operational environment or the assets to be protected, taking into account the length of time the product is expected to be in use"; it "shall indicate whether and, if so in what manner, the security requirements set out in Part I, point (2), of Annex I are applicable" and "how those requirements are implemented"; and "how the manufacturer is to apply Part I, point (1)" and Part II. — [cyberday.ai Art 13.3](https://www.cyberday.ai/requirement/cra-article-13-3-documenting-risk-assessment)
- Art 13(4): the risk assessment is included in the technical documentation (Art 31, Annex VII); it may be integrated into a risk assessment required by other Union law; non-applicable requirements need a justification. — [springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(5)-(7): due diligence on third-party components including FOSS; report and remediate component vulnerabilities; document cybersecurity aspects "including known vulnerabilities" and update the risk assessment where applicable. — [springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(8): support period at least five years or the expected time in use if shorter; Art 13(9): updates available at least 10 years or the support period, whichever longer. — [streamlex Art 13](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-13)
- Annex VII item (3): "an assessment of the cybersecurity risks against which the product with digital elements is designed", developed, produced, delivered and maintained under Art 13, "including how the Part I essential requirements of Annex I apply". Annex VII also requires (1) product description incl. software versions affecting compliance and (for hardware) photos/illustrations of external features, marking and internal layout; (2) design/development/production and vulnerability-handling info (architecture, SBOM, CVD policy, contact, secure update distribution); (4) support-period information; (5) harmonised standards/common specs/certification applied, or descriptions of the solutions adopted where not applied; (6) test reports; (7) EU DoC; (8) SBOM on reasoned request. — [streamlex Annex VII](https://streamlex.eu/annexes/cra-en-annex-vii/)
- Recital 93: the Commission is to establish a simplified technical documentation form for microenterprises and small enterprises. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Recital 58: dependencies on high-risk suppliers, including non-technical risk factors, should be considered in the cybersecurity risk assessment. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Commission guidance C(2026) 5252 (27 July 2026, under Art 26, non-binding, 84-page annex, 67 examples): per SCHUTZWERK's summary of paras 156-163, residual risk must be measured against an appropriate level of cybersecurity considering intended purpose, foreseeable use, conditions of use, affected users and assets, and expected time of use; "cost, commercial strategy, or internal risk tolerance alone" do not show a risk is adequately addressed; a documented real deployment environment can be part of intended purpose but a vague disclaimer shifting an unresolved design problem to the customer does not suffice. — [SCHUTZWERK blog (security firm)](https://www.schutzwerk.com/en/blog/cra-guidance-2026/)
- Commission draft guidance (3 March 2026, consultation to 31 March 2026), per Linklaters: the risk assessment should identify intended use and reasonably foreseeable misuse, map threat scenarios and attack paths, document risk treatment including security-by-design and secure default configurations, and assess third-party/open-source components, which "cannot be treated as 'black boxes'". — [Linklaters Tech Insights](https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102mmlo/eu-cyber-resilience-act-commission-issues-first-draft-guidance-10-key-points-y)
- Final guidance details: Communication C(2026) 5252 with annex; press release 27 July 2026; official document links ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131455 (Communication) and /131456 (annex). — [cyberresilienceact.eu (independent site run by i46 s.r.o.)](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/commission-cra-application-guidance-published-27-july-2026.html); [streamlex guidance page (adopted 27 Jul 2026, last updated 19 Aug 2026)](https://streamlex.eu/law-resources/cra-implementation-guidance-c2026-5252/); [Hunton blog 29 Jul 2026](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/european-commission-issues-guidance-on-the-cyber-resilience-act)
- BSI TR-03183-1 v1.0.0 "General Requirements" (31 July 2026; non-binding "living document"; gives no presumption of conformity) proposes an ISO 31000-based process: RH_RA.1.1.1 asset identification, RH_RA.1.1.2 threat modelling, RH_RA.1.2 analysis (impact 1-5 × likelihood from interface restriction/access restriction/user capability), RH_RA.1.3 evaluation vs acceptance criteria, RH_RT.1 treatment (incl. RH_RT.1.2.1 risk sharing with suppliers), RH_DOC.1 documentation, RH_UPD.1 updates. Part I (1) is handled by RH_RA.1 and RH_RT.1.1.1; each (2)(a)-(m) item is applicable "if a selected control addresses it", otherwise the manufacturer documents and justifies non-applicability (RH_RT.1.1.2). Verdicts per control are PASS/FAIL/N/A; controls typed Activity/Mechanism/Documentation. — [BSI TR-03183-1 v1.0.0 (mirror)](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/bsi-tr-03183-1-v-1-0-0.pdf); [IBF article 27 Aug 2026](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/news-and-knowledge/technical-papers-and-news-on-ce-marking/the-new-technical-guideline-bsi-tr-03183-1)
- BSI TR-03183-1 assessment report content (s.4.8): date, product identification (name/model/versions/SBOM), risk assessment (assets, threats, evaluated and accepted risks), design documentation (architecture, selected controls, non-applicable requirements with justification), verification processes, vulnerability handling, user documentation — and the report "can therefore form part of the technical documentation". — [BSI TR-03183-1 v1.0.0](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/bsi-tr-03183-1-v-1-0-0.pdf)
- Belgian CCB "CRA 101" (SECURE project, v. 31 Mar 2026): risk analysis must cover intended purpose, foreseeable use and conditions of use (operational environment, assets); the justification must explain how cybersecurity-by-design is applied, which Part I requirements apply or not, and how Part II is implemented; update "regularly throughout the support period". — [CCB SECURE CRA101 Update 2026](https://ccb.belgium.be/sites/default/files/2026-04/SECURE_CRA101_Update2026.pdf)
- Draft ETSI EN 304 621 clause 6.1: where a product function does not fulfil a requirement, "the related risk assessment needs to prove that non-product means, such as obligations for the operational environment and user information" mitigate the residual risk; the assessment covers functional completeness and functional sufficiency, using the Annex VII architecture description where quantitative testing would be disproportionate. — [draft ETSI EN 304 621 clause 6.1](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-621/-/raw/main/EN-304-621.md)
- EN 40000-1-2 "Principles for cyber resilience" (CEN-CLC/JTC 13 WG 9 PT1) is the horizontal process standard for Part I requirement (1): it sets generic principles for all lifecycle stages, requirements for assessing and treating risk, and lifecycle activities; implementation is shown "through documented process outputs". — [CEN-CENELEC webinar slides 5 Mar 2026](https://www.cencenelec.eu/media/CEN-CENELEC/Events/Webinars/2026/2026-03-05_cra_unlocked_pt2_stan4cr_slides.pdf); [EVS prEN 40000-1-2 scope](https://www.evs.ee/en/pren-40000-1-2)

### Inferences
- A defensible Art 13 risk assessment therefore needs, at minimum: a product/boundary definition (including any remote data processing), intended purpose and foreseeable (mis)use, operational environment and user profile, an asset list, threat model/attack paths, risk rating, a per-item applicability statement for (2)(a)-(m) with implementation reference, residual-risk statements with "other means" (user information, intended-use limits) for anything not fully met by the product, and a change log showing updates during the support period. The BSI TR-03183-1 report template and the ETSI "Preparation/Activities/Verdict/Evidence" fields are the two most concrete public templates for this as of October 2026.

### Gaps
- The Commission guidance annex itself could not be read (binary/unfetchable); paragraph references (156-163 on risk assessment; 178-202 on RDPS; 90, 103-113 on substantial modification) come from SCHUTZWERK's secondary summary and should be verified against the official PDF.
- No source gave a specific minimum update cadence for the risk assessment ("as appropriate" / "regularly" only).

## Key Question 3 — Secure by default/reset, automatic updates with opt-out, separation of security and functionality updates, user notification: where each sits (Part I vs Part II vs Art 13)

### Takeaway
Secure-by-default and reset-to-original-state sit in Part I (2)(b); automatic security updates enabled by default with an easy opt-out, user notification and temporary postponement sit in Part I (2)(c) (product capability); separation of security updates from functionality updates "where technically feasible" sits in Part II point (2) (manufacturer process), with Part II points (7) and (8) covering secure, timely, automatic-where-applicable distribution and free dissemination with advisory messages; Article 13(8)-(9) govern the support period and 10-year availability of updates, and Art 13(10) relaxes secure-by-default for substantially modified software to the latest version under conditions.

### Cited Findings
- (2)(b) = secure-by-default configuration + reset to original state, with the tailor-made-product carve-out; (2)(c) = updateability including, where applicable, automatic updates by default, opt-out, notification, postponement. — [ENISA Playbook Annex B](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- Part II point (2): remediate without delay, "including by providing security updates; where technically feasible, new security updates shall be provided separately from functionality updates". Part II point (7): mechanisms to securely distribute updates so vulnerabilities are fixed or mitigated in a timely manner "and, where applicable for security updates, in an automatic manner". Part II point (8): security updates disseminated without delay and, unless agreed otherwise for tailor-made products, free of charge, "accompanied by advisory messages". — [ENISA Playbook Annex B (Part II)](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ENISA_Secure_By_Design_and_Default_Playbook_v1.pdf)
- Recital 56: products, especially consumer products, should support automatic security updates; users can approve installation as a final step and can deactivate automatic updates via a clear mechanism; products not designed for automatic updates still require timely notification and updates. Recital 57: where technically feasible, security updates delivered separately from functionality updates so users need not accept new features to get fixes. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Art 13(10): for substantially modified software versions the secure-by-default requirement need only be met for the latest version if earlier users can obtain it free of charge without extra hardware/software costs; Art 13(11): archives of old versions allowed with clear risk warnings; Art 13(19): support-period end date (month/year) stated at purchase, with end-of-support notification where feasible. — [streamlex Art 13](https://streamlex.eu/articles/cra-en-art-13)
- Recital 40: security updates may be limited to the latest substantially modified version under conditions. — [springlex Art 13 linked recitals](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Draft ETSI EN 304 621 clause 5.4 (secure by default): SBD_TECH-1 only Annex K-compliant cryptography enabled by default on any interface reachable beyond localhost; SBD_TECH-2 legacy crypto only for backward compatibility with user notification and upgrade path; SBD_TECH-3 a method to reset to secure-by-default settings, and a NOTE that "the tailor-made product exception does not remove this reset requirement". — [draft ETSI EN 304 621 clause 5.4](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-621/-/raw/main/EN-304-621.md)

### Inferences
- Engineering reading: (2)(b) is a *state* requirement (what the product looks like out of the box and after reset), (2)(c) a *capability* requirement (the product can receive, verify, install, notify, postpone and — where applicable — auto-install updates), Part II (2)/(7)/(8) are *process* requirements on the manufacturer (produce and distribute fixes, separately from features, free, with advisories). The technical file should therefore carry both product evidence (default-config export, reset test, update-flow test, signature-verification test, anti-rollback test) and process evidence (release policy, update channel design, advisory template).
- The ETSI drafts' treatment that opt-out is implemented via postponement/scheduling in managed environments, while consumer-style products enable automatic installation by default, is consistent with Recital 56 and is likely how notified bodies will read "where applicable".

### Gaps
- No source defines "appropriate timeframe" for automatic installation numerically; EN 304 621 references availability targets instead.

### Takeaway

## Key Question 6 — Special cases: hardware/microcontrollers, software-only, indirect connection, remote data processing (do Annex I requirements extend to the backend?)

### Takeaway
Yes — the CRA's product definition (Art 3(1)) includes "remote data processing solutions" (Art 3(2): processing at a distance, software designed/developed by or for the manufacturer, whose absence would prevent a product function), so Annex I applies to the manufacturer's own backend to the extent it supports a product function; Recitals 11-12 explain the boundary (manufacturer-designed software needed for a function is in; general cloud services, SaaS and websites not supporting product functions are out; the manufacturer's whole IT estate is not covered). Recital 9 confirms products with only an indirect connection are in scope. Hardware components such as MCUs sold separately are products with digital elements where the intended use includes a data connection (and MCUs/microprocessors with security functions are Annex III important products with dedicated harmonised standards EN 50765/50766/50767), but vendors stress the integrator remains responsible for product-level risk assessment, defaults and the technical file.

### Cited Findings
- Recital 9: products in scope whether connected physically or logically ("network sockets or APIs"); manufacturers must also secure products that are only indirectly connected. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Recital 11: remote data processing solutions are in scope when the manufacturer designed the software and it is needed for the product to perform a function; the rules do not require measures covering the manufacturer's whole network and information systems. Recital 12: cloud services count only if they meet the definition; cloud-enabled features letting users control a device remotely are in scope; websites not supporting product functions and cloud services built outside the manufacturer's responsibility are not. — [EUR-Lex recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847)
- Art 3(2) (as quoted by DIGITALEUROPE): remote data processing = "data processing at a distance for which the software is designed and developed by the manufacturer" (or under its responsibility) and whose absence would prevent the product performing one of its functions. — [DIGITALEUROPE position paper, 25 Jul 2025](https://cdn.digitaleurope.org/uploads/2025/07/Towards-clear-guidance-for-remote-data-processing-under-the-CRA_DIGITALEUROPE.pdf)
- DIGITALEUROPE: for in-scope remote processing, the product's essential requirements apply — citing Annex I Part I 2(e) and stating data flows to/from the remote service must be encrypted; argues automatic updates (2(c)) and internal activity monitoring (2(l)) should not themselves count as RDPS; worked examples (e-reader sync service in; third-party generic storage out; mobile-banking backend may fall under DORA). — [DIGITALEUROPE](https://cdn.digitaleurope.org/uploads/2025/07/Towards-clear-guidance-for-remote-data-processing-under-the-CRA_DIGITALEUROPE.pdf)
- Commission guidance C(2026) 5252 (per SCHUTZWERK, paras 178-202): RDPS is part of the product when three conditions hold together — processing at a distance; product cannot perform one of its functions without it; manufacturer designed/developed the software or had it done under its responsibility. Manufacturer's own onboarding/configuration cloud and manufacturer-operated OTA update service "will likely qualify"; a manufacturer application on third-party IaaS/PaaS can qualify; generic third-party storage normally not manufacturer-developed but still a dependency to assess with due diligence. Cellular/Wi-Fi connectivity is a channel, not automatically RDPS; a manual alternative does not automatically remove a remote function from the analysis. — [SCHUTZWERK](https://www.schutzwerk.com/en/blog/cra-guidance-2026/); same three-part test in [Linklaters](https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102mmlo/eu-cyber-resilience-act-commission-issues-first-draft-guidance-10-key-points-y)
- BSI TR-03183-1: for its purposes "all manufacturer-developed software that provides a function used by placed components counts as RDPS", including non-essential functions such as telemetry or advertising; configuring third-party software is not development; integrated third-party components remain the manufacturer's responsibility under Art 13(5). — [BSI TR-03183-1 v1.0.0](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/bsi-tr-03183-1-v-1-0-0.pdf)
- Draft ETSI EN 304 621 Annex R (normative, RDPS): the core clause-5 requirements "continue to apply to the product as a whole" whether a function is local, RDPS-supported or split; Annex R adds boundary requirements for both sides — REQ-RDPS-L-AUTH/INTEG/CONF/AVAIL (local side) and REQ-RDPS-R-AUTH/INTEG/CONF/AVAIL (RDPS side): verify origin/authenticity and integrity (incl. replay) before acting, protect confidentiality where disclosure is an unacceptable risk, detect unavailability/delay and apply defined behaviour; it "does not extend to the full remote service environment or underlying third-party infrastructure" beyond assumptions and dependencies; assessment evidence = design/config evidence, acceptance of valid interactions, rejection/recovery behaviour for invalid ones. MAS_TECH-2: product must not connect to undocumented RDPS services. — [draft ETSI EN 304 621 Annex R](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-621/-/raw/main/EN-304-621_AnnexR.md); [EN 304 621 main text](https://labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-621/-/raw/main/EN-304-621.md)
- DIN generic-security-requirements project scope: covers products "including their remote data processing solutions". — [DIN project page](https://www.din.de/en/getting-involved/standards-committees/nia/projects/wdc-proj:din21:389316789)
- ETSI TR 104 071 (mobile devices) flags the manufacturer-operated app distribution platform and update server as RDPS within CRA scope. — [ETSI TR 104 071](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_tr/104000_104099/104071/01.01.01_60/tr_104071v010101p.pdf)
- Hardware/MCU: TI (STDA050, Oct 2026, vendor) states its MCUs/MPUs sold as components are products with digital elements; chips with built-in security features Class I, with hardware tamper resistance Class II (TI's interpretation); TI covers silicon/SDK-level items while the OEM/SoM vendor must do product-level risk assessment, SBOM, DoC and CE; names EN 50765 as the (under-development) harmonised standard; maps features: (2)(a) SDK scanned pre-release, (2)(b) HW root of trust/secure boot/ROM enforcement, (2)(c) firmware patches via CI/CD or secure folder, (2)(d) HW access control and debug-port control, (2)(f) signed firmware, (2)(j) hardware isolation; reset-to-original-state and logging for an MCU not addressed. — [TI STDA050 (vendor)](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/STDA050)

### Inferences
- For backends: the practical test is "manufacturer-developed (or commissioned) + required for a product function + at a distance"; if met, the Art 13 risk assessment must cover the RDPS, the Annex I (2)(d)/(e)/(f)/(h)/(l) controls apply at the product↔cloud boundary (and, per EN 304 621 Annex R, on both endpoints), and the technical file should include a "remote-function map" (which functions depend on which services, who develops/operates them, and what the local fallback behaviour is). Hosting infrastructure (IaaS/PaaS) is a supply-chain dependency under Art 13(5), not part of the product.
- For bare MCUs: (2)(b)/(2)(m) "reset to original state"/"remove all data" and (2)(l) logging are the hardest items to satisfy at chip level; expect the chip vendor's technical file to document integration assumptions (debug lock states, fuse settings, key provisioning) and to rely on the integrator (Annex K NOTE 2 / MI-PDDI-1 pattern), with EN 50765/50766 eventually defining the split.

### Gaps
- The Commission guidance text on RDPS and hardware could only be read through secondary summaries; the official annex PDF was unreadable in this session.
- No official source was found on how (2)(l) logging opt-out or (2)(m) deletion should be met by a component with no user interface; the ETSI drafts leave 5.15 (data transfer) non-mandatory in EN 304 621 ("support for data transfer is not required" due to protocol diversity).
