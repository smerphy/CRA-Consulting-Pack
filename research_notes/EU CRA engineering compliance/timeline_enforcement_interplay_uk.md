## Key Question 1 — Application timeline (Art 71) and transitional provisions (Art 69); treatment of legacy products

### Takeaway
The CRA (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, OJ L 2024/2847 of 20 November 2024) entered into force on 10 December 2024 and applies in full from 11 December 2027, with Chapter IV (notified bodies, Arts 35–51) applying from 11 June 2026 and Art 14 (manufacturer reporting) from 11 September 2026. Products placed on the market before 11 December 2027 are caught only if substantially modified after that date — except that Art 14 reporting applies to all in-scope legacy products regardless; existing EU type-examination certificates/approval decisions for cybersecurity requirements under other Union harmonisation law remain valid until 11 June 2028.

### Cited Findings
- Art 71(1): the Regulation enters into force "on the twentieth day following that of its publication"; Art 71(2): "shall apply from 11 December 2027"; "Article 14 shall apply from 11 September 2026"; Chapter IV (Arts 35–51) "shall apply from 11 June 2026". OJ reference OJ L, 2024/2847, 20.11.2024 — [springlex Art 71](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-71/); corroborated by [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 71](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_71.html). EUR-Lex metadata gives "First date of entry into force: 2024-12-10" — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng).
- Art 69(1): "EU type-examination certificates and approval decisions issued regarding cybersecurity requirements" for products with digital elements subject to other Union harmonisation legislation "shall remain valid until 11 June 2028", unless they expire before that date or unless otherwise specified in that other legislation — [springlex Art 69](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-69/); [lxgesetze Art 69](https://lxgesetze.de/crv/69); [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 69](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_69.html).
- Art 69(2): products placed on the market before 11 December 2027 are "subject to the requirements set out in this Regulation only if" they are "subject to a substantial modification" from/after that date — [springlex Art 69](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-69/); [lxgesetze Art 69](https://lxgesetze.de/crv/69).
- Art 69(3): by way of derogation from para 2, "the obligations laid down in Article 14 shall apply to all products" with digital elements within scope placed on the market before 11 December 2027 — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 69](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_69.html); [springlex Art 69](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-69/).
- Art 3(30) "substantial modification": a change after placing on the market affecting compliance with the essential requirements or altering the intended purpose — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 3](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_3.html).
- Commission guidance (27 Jul 2026): substantial modification is case-by-case based on intended purpose and cybersecurity risk; "security updates do not, as a general rule, constitute a substantial modification"; where it occurs the product is "re-placed on the market"; a substantial modification triggers reassessment of the support period but does not reset it — [Hogan Lovells summary](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-cyber-resilience-act-european-commission-publishes-final-cra-guidance); [Osborne Clarke, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/european-commission-publishes-new-guidelines-cyber-resilience-act).
- Commission guidance on legacy reporting: Art 14 applies "to all in-scope products, including products placed on the market before September 2026"; no retroactive reporting of vulnerabilities known before 11 Sep 2026 unless active exploitation occurs/becomes known afterwards; reporting continues after the support period ends — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-cyber-resilience-act-european-commission-publishes-final-cra-guidance); same point in [Maples, 21 Sep 2026](https://maples.com/knowledge/ireland-update-the-cyber-resilience-act).
- Commission guidance on placing software on the market: software is placed on the market once first supplied in the course of commercial activity; identical copies of the same version count as placed when first offered in the EU; "different variants that differ in components, configuration or enabled functionality" may be distinct products with different dates — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-cyber-resilience-act-european-commission-publishes-final-cra-guidance); [CENTR summer 2026 update](https://www.centr.org/policy/policy-updates/item/eu-policy-update---summer-2026.html).
- Art 13(8): support period "shall be at least five years" unless expected use time is shorter; Art 13(9): each security update "remains available after it has been issued for a minimum of 10 years" or the rest of the support period — [springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/).
- Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/881 (CSIRT dissemination delay) adopted 11 Dec 2025, entry into force 10 May 2026 — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/19136-commission-delegated-regulation-eu-2026881-supplementing-the-cyber-resilience-act-on-notification-dissemination-delay-conditions). Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 (technical description of important/critical products) dated 28 Nov 2025, in force 21 Dec 2025 — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/2392/oj/eng).
- Harmonised standards: as of Sep–Oct 2026 "No CRA harmonised standard has yet been cited in the Official Journal"; standardisation request M/606 (accepted Apr 2025, ~41 standards); July 2026 draft amendment moved first horizontal standards deadline from 30 Aug 2026 to 31 Oct 2026 and verticals from 30 Oct to 31 Dec 2026; ETSI opened approval on 17 vertical final drafts on 13 Aug 2026; remaining horizontals due 30 Oct 2027 — [cra-facts.com, 8 Sep 2026](https://cra-facts.com/blog/eu-cyber-resilience-act-news-september-2026); [cyberresilienceact.eu state of play](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/state-of-play.html).
- Notified bodies: as of 8 Sep 2026 "no notified body has been designated for the CRA" in NANDO; Art 43 designation takes months per body — [cyberresilienceact.eu](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-notified-bodies-rules-apply-11-june-2026.html).

### Inferences
- Because Art 69(2) keys off "placed on the market" (first making available, Art 3(21)) and the Commission treats each new version/variant as potentially a new placing, a software vendor that ships a new major version after 11 Dec 2027 is placing a new product and cannot rely on legacy status; routine security patches to a pre-2027 version do not trigger the CRA under the guidance.
- Art 69(1) matters mainly for RED-delegated-act type-examination certificates (the only existing EU cybersecurity type-examination regime for in-scope products): such certificates stay valid to 11 Jun 2028 even though the underlying delegated act is repealed on 11 Dec 2027 — but the certificate evidences RED compliance, not CRA conformity, so a CRA conformity assessment is still needed for products placed on the market from 11 Dec 2027.
- With no harmonised standards cited and no notified bodies designated as of October 2026, the practical window for third-party assessment of important Class II / critical products before 11 Dec 2027 is very tight; the Art 35(2) 11 Dec 2026 target is best-efforts only.

## Key Question 2 — Enforcement: Chapter V market surveillance (Arts 52–60), Art 64 penalties, and Member State enforcement laws as of October 2026

### Takeaway
Enforcement is national: Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 applies (Art 52(1)); each Member State designates one or more market surveillance authorities (Art 52(2)) with powers to demand data and documentation on reasoned request (Art 53), require corrective action/withdrawal/recall for products presenting a significant cybersecurity risk (Art 54), act on compliant-but-risky products (Art 57) and formal non-compliance (Art 58), run joint activities (Art 59) and coordinated "sweeps" including covert purchases (Art 60), with a Union safeguard procedure (Art 55), Commission-level intervention via implementing acts (Art 56) and an ADCO (Art 52(15)). Art 64 sets three fine tiers (€15m/2.5%, €10m/2%, €5m/1%), exempts open-source stewards entirely and micro/small manufacturers from early-warning-deadline fines, and leaves penalty rules to national law — as of October 2026 only a handful of Member States (Finland, Hungary, Malta, Germany) have adopted CRA enforcement acts; several others are at draft stage.

### Cited Findings — market surveillance powers (Arts 52–63)
- Art 52(1): Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 applies to products with digital elements; Art 52(2): each Member State designates "one or more market surveillance authorities"; Art 52(3): the same authorities supervise open-source stewards' Art 24 obligations and may require "all appropriate corrective actions"; Art 52(4)–(5): cooperation with national cybersecurity certification authorities (Art 58 of Reg 2019/881), coordinator CSIRTs and ENISA, who may be asked for technical advice/analysis; Art 52(7): cooperation with data protection authorities, which may request and access CRA documentation; Art 52(10): authorities may advise economic operators; Art 52(11): consumer complaint route under Art 11 of Reg 2019/1020; Art 52(13): annual reporting to the Commission and reporting of competition-relevant findings; Art 52(14): for high-risk AI systems, the AI Act market surveillance authorities perform CRA market surveillance; Art 52(15): ADCO established under Art 30(2) of Reg 2019/1020; Art 52(16): ADCO publishes statistics and indicative guidance on support periods and may recommend enforcement focus — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 52](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_52.html).
- Art 53 (Access to data and documentation): where necessary to assess conformity with Annex I, authorities "upon a reasoned request" get access to data "required to assess the design, development, production and vulnerability handling", including related internal documentation, "in a language easily understood by them" — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 53](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_53.html). Note: the mirror shows only one paragraph and does not mention source code; see Gaps.
- SBOM access: Art 13(25) lets ADCO run a Union-wide dependency assessment and market surveillance authorities may request SBOMs from manufacturers; Art 13(24) empowers the Commission to set SBOM format/elements by implementing act — [springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/).
- Art 54 (national procedure, significant cybersecurity risk): trigger is "sufficient reason to consider" a product presents a significant cybersecurity risk; evaluation possibly with the CSIRT; if non-compliant, corrective action, withdrawal or recall within a "reasonable period, commensurate with the nature of the cybersecurity risk"; para 2 brings in non-technical risk factors from NIS2 Art 22 coordinated supply-chain risk assessments and cooperation with NIS2 Art 8 authorities; para 5 provisional measures (prohibit/restrict/withdraw/recall) notified to Commission and Member States; para 8: if no objection within three months the measure "shall be deemed to be justified"; para 9: all Member States then take restrictive measures — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 54](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_54.html).
- Art 55 (Union safeguard procedure): triggered by a Member State objection within three months or the Commission considering the national measure contrary to Union law; the Commission consults and decides whether the measure is justified within nine months of the Art 54(5) notification; if justified, all Member States withdraw the product; if not, the Member State withdraws its measure; where the cause is a shortcoming in harmonised standards, Art 11 of Reg 1025/2012 applies; where a certification scheme or common specification is at fault, the Commission considers amending/repealing the Art 27(9) delegated act or Art 27(2) implementing act — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 55](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_55.html).
- Art 56 (Union-level procedure): the Commission may act on "sufficient reason to consider" (including from ENISA information) that a product presenting significant risk is non-compliant; for non-technical risks it informs NIS2 Art 8 authorities and consults the NIS2 Cooperation Group and ENISA; where urgent action is needed and national measures are absent/ineffective, the Commission evaluates (with ENISA analysis if requested) and may adopt implementing acts requiring corrective or restrictive measures including withdrawal or recall "within a reasonable period, commensurate with the nature of the risk" (examination procedure, Art 62(2)); applies only while the exceptional situation lasts — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 56](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_56.html).
- Art 57 (compliant products presenting significant risk): where a compliant product still presents a significant cybersecurity risk to health/safety, fundamental rights, essential entities' services or other public interests, the authority may require "all appropriate measures" including withdrawal/recall, "commensurate with the nature of those risks"; Commission evaluates and may adopt Union-level implementing acts in urgent cases — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 57](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_57.html).
- Art 58 (formal non-compliance): CE marking affixed in breach of Arts 29–30 or missing; EU declaration of conformity not drawn up or incorrect; notified body identification number missing; technical documentation unavailable or incomplete → manufacturer must "put an end to the non-compliance concerned"; if it persists, restrict/prohibit, recall or withdraw — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 58](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_58.html).
- Art 59 (joint activities): authorities may agree joint activities "in particular products with digital elements that are often found to present cybersecurity risks"; the Commission or ENISA may propose joint compliance checks; agreements must be made public — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 59](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_59.html).
- Art 60 (sweeps): "simultaneous coordinated control actions" on particular products/categories; may include inspections of products acquired under a "cover identity"; coordinated by the Commission unless authorities "otherwise agree"; ENISA may propose sweeps based on Art 14(1)/(3) notifications; authorities may use Arts 52–58 powers plus national-law powers; Commission officials may be invited — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 60](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_60.html).
- Art 16 (single reporting platform): "a single reporting platform shall be established by ENISA"; the first-receiving CSIRT must "without delay, disseminate the notification via the single reporting platform"; para 3: CSIRTs pass actively-exploited-vulnerability/severe-incident information to "the market surveillance authorities of their respective Member States" — this is the enforcement feed-through from reporting to market surveillance — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 16](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_16.html).
- Art 66 amends Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 (list of Union harmonisation legislation) by adding "Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 of the European Parliament and of the Council" (point 72 per one mirror; the springlex mirror shows the inserted point numbered differently — see Gaps) — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Art 66](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_66.html); [springlex Art 66](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-66/). Recitals 106–108: Reg 2019/1020 market surveillance rules apply; Member States may designate existing authorities, including national cybersecurity certification authorities and RED (2014/53/EU) authorities; ADCO may liaise with the RED ADCO — [EUR-Lex OJ text (recitals)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847).
- Art 65 provides for representative actions and Art 67 amends Directive (EU) 2020/1828 (representative actions) — [european-cyber-resilience-act.com Articles list](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Articles.html).

## Key Question 3 — Interplay with other EU law (NIS2, RED delegated act, AI Act, GDPR, MDR/IVDR, Machinery Regulation, Data Act, PLD, DORA, Cybersecurity Act/EUCC/EUCS, eIDAS)

### Takeaway
The CRA is a product-market-access law that sits alongside entity-focused regimes (NIS2, DORA) and sector product laws. It fully excludes MDR/IVDR, type-approved vehicles, certified aviation products and marine equipment (Art 2(2)–(4)), gives a "deemed to comply" rule for AI Act Art 15 cybersecurity (CRA Art 12; mirrored in AI Act Art 42(3) as inserted by Reg 2026/1744), facilitates but does not presume Machinery Regulation compliance (recital 53), replaces the RED cybersecurity delegated act from 11 Dec 2027 (Delegated Regulation 2026/339), and treats European cybersecurity certification (EUCC) as a presumption-of-conformity route (Art 27(8)–(9)) pending a delegated act planned for Q4 2026. Cloud/SaaS is in scope only as "remote data processing" under the manufacturer's responsibility; other cloud services fall to NIS2 (recital 12). The new PLD makes software a product and cybersecurity vulnerabilities a potential defect from 9 Dec 2026, with non-compliance with mandatory safety requirements (which will include CRA Annex I) triggering a presumption of defectiveness.

## Key Question 5 — UK-based manufacturers and Northern Ireland (deliverable d)

### Takeaway
The CRA applies to any product with digital elements made available on the EU market whatever the manufacturer's location; a UK (GB) manufacturer is a third-country manufacturer who must meet Arts 13–14 itself, and whose EU importer (Art 19) or optional authorised representative (Art 18) carries the EU-side duties. The CRA does not yet apply in Northern Ireland: it is not in Annex 2 of the Windsor Framework; only Arts 66 and 68 (amending Annex 2 acts) apply via Art 13(3); the EU proposed adding the CRA to Annex 2 point 47 (Council Decision (EU) 2025/799, 14 Apr 2025) and an Article 13(4) exchange of views ran from June 2025, was reported concluded by the Specialised Committee co-chairs on 7 May 2026, and awaits a Joint Committee decision which requires UK agreement subject to Schedule 6B NI Act 1998 democratic safeguards (applicability motion). The UK's own regime is PSTI (in force 29 Apr 2024; three requirements; OPSS enforcement; fines up to £10m/4%); the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill (Lords Report Stage from 26 Oct 2026) does not regulate product security; and the 28 Oct 2026 Budget committed to a 2027 consultation on tackling "insecure technology at source" and software liability. UK Approved Bodies cannot issue CE certificates, so a CRA third-party assessment needs an EU notified body.

### Cited Findings — extraterritorial reach and economic-operator roles
- Scope keys off products "made available on the market" (Art 2(1)); "placing on the market" is the "first making available of a product" on the Union market (Art 3(21)); "making available" is supply "in the course of a commercial activity", paid or free (Art 3(22)); "manufacturer" is one who "develops or manufactures products with digital elements" under its own name/trademark (Art 3(13)); "importer" is an EU-established person placing on the market a product bearing "the name or trademark of" a non-EU entity (Art 3(16)); "authorised representative" acts under a "written mandate from a manufacturer" (Art 3(15)) — [Art 2 mirror](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_2.html); [Art 3 mirror](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_3.html).
- Art 18: a manufacturer "may, by a written mandate, appoint an authorised representative" (optional, not mandatory); the mandate cannot cover Art 13(1)–(11), 13(12) first subpara or 13(14); minimum delegable tasks: keep the EU DoC and technical documentation for 10 years or the support period "whichever is longer", provide conformity information on "reasoned request", cooperate with authorities — [Art 18 mirror](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_18.html).
- Art 19 (importers): only place compliant products on the market; verify conformity assessment (Art 32), technical documentation, CE marking, EU DoC and Annex II information, and the manufacturer's Art 13(15),(16),(19) compliance; put its name/address/e-contact/website on the product/packaging/document; must not place non-conforming products on the market and must inform the manufacturer and authorities of significant cybersecurity risks; corrective action/withdrawal/recall; inform the manufacturer of vulnerabilities; keep the EU DoC 10 years or the support period "whichever is longer"; inform authorities and users if the manufacturer ceases operations — [Art 19 mirror](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_19.html). Art 20 imposes parallel due-care duties on distributors ("shall act with due care in relation to the requirements") — [Art 20 mirror](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_20.html). Art 21: an importer/distributor that "places a product with digital elements on the market under its name or trademark" or "carries out a substantial modification" becomes "subject to Articles 13 and 14" — [Art 21 mirror](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_21.html).
- Art 14(7) for non-EU manufacturers: report to the CSIRT of the Member State of the main establishment ("where cybersecurity decisions for its products are predominantly made"); where there is no EU main establishment, use the CSIRT of the first applicable Member State in this order: authorised representative, importer, distributor, then where most users are located; ENISA has simultaneous access — [Art 14 mirror](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_14.html).
- Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 Art 4: certain products "may be placed on the market only if" there is "an economic operator established in the Union" responsible for Art 4(3) tasks; eligible operators are an EU-established manufacturer, "an importer, where the manufacturer is not established in the Union", an authorised representative with written mandate, or an EU fulfilment service provider "with respect to the products it handles" where none of the others exists; Art 4(5) confines this to listed legislation — Regulations 305/2011, 2016/425, 2016/426 and Directives 2000/14, 2006/42, 2009/48, 2009/125, 2011/65, 2013/29, 2013/53, 2014/29, 2014/30, 2014/31, 2014/32, 2014/34, 2014/35, 2014/53, 2014/68 — the CRA is not listed; Art 6: an online offer "targeted at end users in the Union" is deemed made available on the market — [EUR-Lex 2019/1020](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R1020); [legislation.gov.uk retained Art 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2019/1020/chapter/II/data.htm). CRA Art 66 adds the CRA to Annex I of 2019/1020 (the general list), not to Art 4(5) — [Art 66 mirror](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_66.html).
- Commission Art 4 Guidelines (2021/C 100/01): for direct shipments from a third-country manufacturer to an EU end-user, the authorised representative is the Art 4 operator "if an authorised representative has been mandated in writing"; "there may also be an importer" (e.g., one offering the product online); an EU fulfilment service provider is the fallback "if there is no importer"; otherwise "the product may not be offered for sale to EU end-users" — [Commission Guidelines (ibf-solutions mirror)](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/amtsblaetter/guidelines-on-the-market-surveillance-regulation-2019-1020.pdf). Reg 2019/1020 Art 3(11): a fulfilment service provider offers at least two of warehousing, packaging, addressing, dispatching "without having ownership of the products involved" — [EUR-Lex 2019/1020](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R1020); CRA recital 78 refers to fulfilment service providers under "Article 3, point (11), of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020" — [EUR-Lex CRA recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ:L_202402847).
- GPSR Art 16 (applies 13 Dec 2024): a consumer product "shall not be placed on the market unless" an EU-established economic operator is responsible for the Reg 2019/1020 Art 4(3) tasks; CRA Art 11 makes GPSR Chapter III Section 1 (which contains Art 16) applicable to PDEs for safety risks not covered by the CRA where no other harmonisation legislation applies — [EUR-Lex GPSR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R0988); [springlex CRA Art 11](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-11/).
- PLD Art 8(1)(c)(iii): where there is "no importer established within the Union or authorised representative", the EU fulfilment service provider is liable; Art 8(4) extends to online platforms allowing consumer distance contracts — [EUR-Lex PLD](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024L2853).

### Inferences
- Because the CRA was added to Annex I of Reg 2019/1020 but not to Art 4(5), the CRA itself does not require a non-EU manufacturer to appoint an EU "responsible economic operator" for market access; the CRA's own mechanism is that whoever places the product on the EU market from a third country is the importer (Art 19) with verification and record duties. However, for a GB manufacturer selling direct-to-consumer online into the EU: (i) the online offer is "made available" under Reg 2019/1020 Art 6; (ii) a private consumer cannot be an importer (placing on the market requires commercial activity), so in a pure direct-ship model there is no CRA importer and the manufacturer alone carries Arts 13–14, with the Art 14(7) CSIRT determined by the authorised representative (if any) or the location of most users; (iii) if the product is also radio equipment (RED, listed in Art 4(5)) or an unharmonised consumer product (GPSR Art 16 via CRA Art 11), an EU responsible economic operator (authorised representative or fulfilment service provider) is mandatory in any event; (iv) from 9 Dec 2026 the PLD makes the EU fulfilment service provider or online platform liable where no EU importer/authorised representative exists. The practical conclusion is that a GB vendor should appoint an EU authorised representative under CRA Art 18 even though Art 18 is optional.
- Northern Ireland: as of 30 Oct 2026 the CRA's substantive provisions do not apply in NI; NI manufacturers are third-country manufacturers for CRA purposes, like GB ones, but NI goods have the Windsor Framework route for RED/other Annex 2 harmonised legislation. If the Joint Committee adopts the Annex 2 addition (the exchange of views concluded 7 May 2026 and a decision is pending), the CRA would apply in NI, PSTI would cease to apply to in-scope NI supplies (SI 2023/1007 Sch 3 para 1), and GB–NI divergence would arise — which is why the UK EM focuses on "the avoidance of a hard border" and proportionality. An applicability motion in the NI Assembly (or a ministerial finding of no new regulatory border/exceptional circumstances) would be needed first.
- UK regulatory direction: no UK CRA equivalent exists or is formally proposed; PSTI remains narrower (consumer connectable products; three outcome requirements; no vulnerability-handling or SBOM duties). The 2027 consultation signals movement towards vendor obligations/liability, and the Law Commission review may mirror the EU PLD's treatment of software as a product.
