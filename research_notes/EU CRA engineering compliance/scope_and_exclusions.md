## KQ1. Exact definitions (Art 3) and the recitals that explain them

### Takeaway
Art 3 contains 51 definitions (not 50). The scope-critical ones are "product with digital elements" (Art 3(1)), "remote data processing" (Art 3(2)), "software"/"hardware"/"component" (Art 3(4)-(6)), the connection definitions (Art 3(8)-(10)), "manufacturer" (Art 3(13)), "placing"/"making available" (Art 3(21)-(22)), "intended purpose"/"reasonably foreseeable use"/"misuse" (Art 3(23)-(25)), "substantial modification" (Art 3(30)) and "free and open-source software" (Art 3(48)). Recitals 11-12 (remote data processing/cloud), 15-16 (commercial activity), 17-20 (FOSS), 38-42 (substantial modification) and 92 (software "production") explain them.

### Cited Findings (verbatim operative text, Art 3 — numbering per the OJ)
- Art 3(1) 'product with digital elements': "a software or hardware product and its remote data processing solutions, including software or hardware components being placed on the market separately" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/); quoted identically in [Commission FAQ, 3 Dec 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Art 3(2) 'remote data processing': "data processing at a distance for which the software is designed and developed by the manufacturer, or under the responsibility of the manufacturer, and the absence of which would prevent the product with digital elements from performing one of its functions" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(4) 'software': "the part of an electronic information system which consists of computer code"; Art 3(5) 'hardware': "a physical electronic information system, or parts thereof capable of processing, storing or transmitting digital data"; Art 3(6) 'component': "software or hardware intended for integration into an electronic information system"; Art 3(7) 'electronic information system': a system "capable of processing, storing or transmitting digital data" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(8) 'logical connection': "a virtual representation of a data connection implemented through a software interface"; Art 3(9) 'physical connection': connection "implemented using physical means, including through electrical, optical or mechanical interfaces, wires or radio waves"; Art 3(10) 'indirect connection': a connection "which does not take place directly but rather as part of a larger system that is directly connectable to such device or network"; Art 3(11) 'end-point': any device connected to a network serving as an entry point to it — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(12) 'economic operator': the manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, distributor, "or other natural or legal person who is subject to obligations" in relation to manufacture or making available — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(13) 'manufacturer': "a natural or legal person who develops or manufactures products with digital elements or has products with digital elements designed, developed or manufactured, and markets them under its name or trademark, whether for payment, monetisation or free of charge" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(14) 'open-source software steward': "a legal person, other than a manufacturer, that has the purpose or objective of systematically providing support on a sustained basis for the development of specific products with digital elements, qualifying as free and open-source software and intended for commercial activities, and that ensures the viability of those products" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(15) 'authorised representative': natural or legal person "established within the Union who has received a written mandate from a manufacturer to act on its behalf in relation to specified tasks" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(16) 'importer': person "established in the Union who places on the market a product with digital elements that bears the name or trademark of a natural or legal person established outside the Union" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(17) 'distributor': person in the supply chain, other than manufacturer or importer, "that makes a product with digital elements available on the Union market without affecting its properties" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(20) 'support period': period during which the manufacturer must ensure vulnerabilities "are handled effectively and in accordance with" Annex I Part II — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(21) 'placing on the market': "the first making available of a product with digital elements on the Union market"; Art 3(22) 'making available on the market': "the supply of a product with digital elements for distribution or use on the Union market in the course of a commercial activity, whether in return for payment or free of charge" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(23) 'intended purpose': the use intended by the manufacturer "including the specific context and conditions of use" as specified in instructions, promotional/sales materials and statements, and technical documentation; Art 3(24) 'reasonably foreseeable use': use "not necessarily the intended purpose" but "likely to result from reasonably foreseeable human behaviour or technical operations or interactions"; Art 3(25) 'reasonably foreseeable misuse': use not in accordance with intended purpose "which may result from reasonably foreseeable human behaviour or interaction with other systems" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(30) 'substantial modification': "a change to the product with digital elements following its placing on the market, which affects the compliance of the product with digital elements with the essential cybersecurity requirements set out in Part I of Annex I or which results in a modification to the intended purpose for which the product with digital elements has been assessed" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(48) 'free and open-source software': "software the source code of which is openly shared and which is made available under a free and open-source licence which provides for all rights to make it freely accessible, usable, modifiable and redistributable" — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(27) 'conformity assessment': verifying whether Annex I essential requirements are fulfilled; Art 3(31) 'CE marking': manufacturer's indication that the product "and the processes put in place by the manufacturer" conform; Art 3(39) 'software bill of materials': formal record of components' details and supply-chain relationships; Art 3(40)-(42) vulnerability / exploitable / actively exploited vulnerability — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Full list of the 51 defined terms, in order: product with digital elements; remote data processing; cybersecurity; software; hardware; component; electronic information system; logical connection; physical connection; indirect connection; end-point; economic operator; manufacturer; open-source software steward; authorised representative; importer; distributor; consumer; micro/small/medium enterprises; support period; placing on the market; making available on the market; intended purpose; reasonably foreseeable use; reasonably foreseeable misuse; notifying authority; conformity assessment; conformity assessment body; notified body; substantial modification; CE marking; Union harmonisation legislation; market surveillance authority; international standard; European standard; harmonised standard; cybersecurity risk; significant cybersecurity risk; SBOM; vulnerability; exploitable vulnerability; actively exploited vulnerability; incident; incident having an impact on security of the product; near miss; cyber threat; personal data; FOSS; recall; withdrawal; CSIRT designated as coordinator — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)

### Cited Findings (explanatory recitals)
- Recital 9: scope is wide; obligations relate to products connected physically "via hardware interfaces" and logically "such as via network sockets, pipes, files, application programming interfaces or any other types of software interface"; manufacturers must also secure products "only indirectly connected" — [Springlex Art 2 page, Recital 9](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Recital 11: remote data processing solutions should be covered "irrespective of whether data is processed or stored locally on the user's device or remotely"; but only "in so far as it is necessary for a product with digital elements to perform its functions"; example: a mobile app requiring access to an API or database "provided by means of a service developed by the manufacturer" — that service is in scope as a remote data processing solution; requirements do not extend to securing "a manufacturer's network and information systems as a whole" — [Springlex Art 3 page, Recital 11](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Recital 12: cloud solutions are remote data processing solutions "only if they meet the definition"; cloud-enabled functionalities of smart-home devices letting users control the device remotely are in scope; "websites that do not support the functionality of a product with digital elements, or cloud services designed and developed outside the responsibility of a manufacturer" are out; NIS2 (Directive 2022/2555) applies to SaaS, PaaS, IaaS cloud computing services (for medium-sized and larger providers) — [Springlex Art 3 page, Recital 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Recital 15: CRA applies to economic operators "only in relation to products with digital elements made available on the market"; commercial activity indicators include charging a price, charging for technical support beyond cost recovery, "an intention to monetise, for instance by providing a software platform through which the manufacturer monetises other services", requiring processing of personal data as a condition of use for reasons other than "exclusively for improving the security, compatibility or interoperability", or accepting donations exceeding costs; donations "without the intention of making a profit" are not commercial — [Springlex Art 1 page, Recital 15](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-1/)
- Recital 16: products supplied as part of a service charged "solely to recover the actual costs" (e.g. public administrations) are not commercial on that ground alone; products "developed or modified by a public administration entity exclusively for its own use" are not made available on the market — [Springlex Art 1 page, Recital 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-1/)
- Recital 18: only FOSS "made available on the market" is in scope; development circumstances and financing are irrelevant; FOSS "not monetised by their manufacturers" is not a commercial activity; FOSS components intended for integration by other manufacturers are "making available on the market only if the component is monetised by its original manufacturer"; regular releases alone do not imply commercial activity; not-for-profit development is non-commercial if all earnings after costs serve not-for-profit objectives; the Regulation "does not apply to natural or legal persons who contribute with source code" to FOSS not under their responsibility — [Springlex Art 3 page, Recital 18](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Recital 19: stewards get a "light-touch and tailor-made regulatory regime"; covers FOSS "ultimately intended for commercial activities, such as for integration into commercial services or into monetised products"; intention for integration includes cases where integrating manufacturers "contribute to the development of that component in a regular manner or provide regular financial assistance"; sustained support includes hosting collaboration platforms, hosting source code, governing/managing, steering development; stewards may not affix the CE marking — [Springlex Art 13 page, Recital 19](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Recital 20 (summary via EUR-Lex fetch): hosting software on open repositories or collaboration platforms does not by itself constitute making available on the market; such providers are distributors only if they supply software commercially — [EUR-Lex CELEX:32024R2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R2847)
- Recital 38: essential requirements "apply to each individual product with digital elements when placed on the market, irrespective of whether ... manufactured as an individual unit or in series"; each unit should have received all available security patches when placed on the market; modifications "not foreseen by the manufacturer in the initial risk assessment" that may mean the product no longer meets the requirements are substantial; repairs can be assimilated to maintenance if compliance/intended purpose is unaffected — [Springlex Art 21 page, Recital 38](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-21/)
- Recital 92: for software, creation "almost exclusively focuses on design and development"; compiling, building, packaging, making available for download or copying onto media "should be considered to be activities amounting to production" for conformity-assessment purposes — [Springlex Art 1 page, Recital 92](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-1/)
- Recital 78: an online marketplace that merely intermediates is not an economic operator; if it also distributes products it is a distributor for those sales; if it sells own-branded products it is a manufacturer — [Springlex Art 3 page, Recital 78](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)

### Inferences
- "Manufacturer" turns on marketing a product under one's own name/trademark, not on who wrote the code; outsourced development under your brand still makes you the manufacturer (Art 3(13) "has ... developed"). Gratis supply is caught if it occurs "in the course of a commercial activity" (Art 3(22) read with Recital 15).
- Because Art 3(1) includes components "placed on the market separately", a library, SDK, driver, firmware image or chip sold or otherwise commercially supplied on its own is itself a product with digital elements, with its own manufacturer.

- The authentic OJ text could not be opened directly (EUR-Lex WAF challenge; page too long for the fetch tool). Wording was verified against Commission quotations where available; a final check against the OJ PDF is recommended before publication.

## KQ2. Art 1 subject matter and Art 2(1) scope test: connection test; remote data processing vs SaaS/cloud; web apps, mobile apps, APIs, SDKs, libraries, dev tools, firmware, IoT, industrial; bespoke software; internal-use; FOSS

### Takeaway
A product is in scope when three cumulative conditions are met: (i) it is a product with digital elements, (ii) it is made available on the EU market in the course of a commercial activity, and (iii) its intended purpose or reasonably foreseeable use includes a direct or indirect, logical or physical, data connection to a device or network (Art 2(1); Commission FAQ). Software is a product only when it is supplied to and executes on the user's side; software merely accessed remotely (web apps, SaaS, websites) is not a product and is caught only to the extent it is a "remote data processing solution" of a product (Commission guidance paras 19-21). Internal-use software is not placed on the market; bespoke software supplied commercially to one customer is placed on the market (but may use the "tailor-made" derogations for two requirements).

### Cited Findings — Art 1 and Art 2(1)
- Art 1 lays down: (a) rules for making available products with digital elements to ensure their cybersecurity; (b) essential cybersecurity requirements for design, development and production, and economic-operator obligations; (c) essential requirements for manufacturers' vulnerability-handling processes "during the time the products are expected to be in use"; (d) market surveillance and enforcement rules — [Springlex Art 1](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-1/)
- Art 2(1): "This Regulation applies to products with digital elements made available on the market, the intended purpose or reasonably foreseeable use of which includes a direct or indirect logical or physical data connection to a device or network." — [Springlex Art 2](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Commission FAQ (3 Dec 2025, updated 4 Sept 2026): three cumulative elements — meets the definition; is made available on the market; intended purpose or reasonably foreseeable use includes a connection — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)

### Cited Findings — the "data connection" test
- Commission guidance (27 July 2026) para 26-28: scope is "anchored not in the mere presence of electronics, but in the capacity ... to exchange digital information"; a data connection requires binary states "deliberately encoded as information by a source" and decodable at the destination; signals used "solely to trigger or power a function" are not a data connection and such a product "does not fall within the scope of Article 2(1)" — [Commission guidance annex C(2026) 5252](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission FAQ examples of direct physical connections: USB (printer-laptop), Ethernet, fibre, copper fieldbus (PROFIBUS sensor-PLC), Wi-Fi (POS terminal), Bluetooth (headset), NFC (door lock) — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission FAQ: a direct logical connection exists where the product initiates or manages communication (browser HTTPS session, email client IMAP/SMTP); an indirect logical connection where the product does not itself communicate but runs on a host that does — "an offline text editor or a calculator" indirectly connected via the OS are in scope — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission FAQ out-of-scope examples (no connection capability): dishwasher with cycle firmware; basic calculator; electronic toy with pre-recorded effects; coffee machine with control panel; electric toothbrush with wireless charging station — footnote: such firmware "may, however, fall in scope ... when placed on the market separately" — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)

### Cited Findings — what counts as a product (software, firmware, hardware, components)
- Commission FAQ list of product forms: standalone downloadable software (mobile app, downloadable program); software intended for integration when placed separately, "e.g. firmware or software meant to be embedded into hardware devices"; software placed with hardware whether pre-loaded or not (printer drivers, laptop OS, FPGA design/programming tools); hardware from foundational components (integrated circuits, motherboards, sensors) to consumer devices to industrial IoT and machinery — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission guidance para 18: CRA can cover (i) standalone software "whether digitally or physically distributed"; (ii) hardware with embedded software (IoT, laptops, tablets); (iii) standalone hardware (integrated circuits, motherboards); (iv) any combination of hardware and software supplied separately but intended to operate together — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 20: for software to be in scope it "must be provided to a user, obtained by that user and operated on, or as part of, an electronic information system on the user's side"; includes browser extensions and web-technology apps "supplied for local execution" — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 21 and Examples 3-6: software "that executes remotely and is merely accessed by the user" is not a product; this is "typically the case for web applications, including progressive web apps, where they are accessed exclusively through a web browser"; websites are not products and are in scope only as remote data processing; a mobile app from an app store IS a product (Ex 3); an Electron-style desktop app built with web tech but installed locally IS a product (Ex 4); a browser-only web app is NOT a product unless it supports a product's functionality (Ex 5); an informational website is NOT a product (Ex 6) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 22-24 (computer code): "software" covers both machine code and source code; sharing FOSS code on public repositories is generally not placing on the market; unfinished code shared during design/development (testing/review) is not placed on the market; sample/demo/tutorial code is not placed on the market; but code supplied to customers as a product IS placed on the market "regardless of whether it is machine code or source code" — Example 7: Company A licensing source code to Company B for a customisable internal platform is placing it on the market; A is not responsible for B's subsequent adaptations — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 25 and Examples 8-9 (hardware + software combination): whether software is part of a product depends on whether it is "necessary for the product ... to perform its intended functions", not on delivery channel; software necessary "to operate, configure, control or use" the product is part of it even if obtained via app store/download after the hardware is placed on the market; placing on the market of such software occurs when the hardware units are placed on the market; printer + downloadable drivers = one product (Ex 8); wearable + companion app = one product (Ex 9) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 29-32 (complex systems): a multi-element system placed on the market as a single product is one product with digital elements; long design cycles, legacy components, and interoperability constraints "do not in themselves exclude complex systems from the CRA's scope" but feed the risk-based approach (recital 55); Example 10 allows a legacy insecure protocol only where required for interoperability, with the secure protocol enabled by default where feasible — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

### Cited Findings — remote data processing (RDPS) versus SaaS/cloud
- Commission guidance para 180: RDPS covers only "the software elements ... of data processing at a distance", not the hardware it runs on — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 182: not RDPS — internal systems for HR, payroll, CRM, "continuous integration/continuous delivery (CI/CD) pipelines, the distribution of security updates to edge locations", and auditing/testing systems such as penetration testing, threat hunting, red teaming — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 184-188: three elements — (i) at a distance; (ii) absence would prevent the product performing one of its functions; (iii) designed and developed by or under the responsibility of the manufacturer; (ii) and (iii) are "decisive and cumulative"; "at a distance" includes edge computing and on-premises/private cloud processing — on-prem is "just as likely to qualify as RDPS" as public cloud — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 189-192: "functions" is not limited to core functionality or intended purpose; examples of functions whose remote processing counts: sending commands to a device; synchronising files; onboarding the user; configuration/personalisation; "automated distribution of updates, including feature update and security patching"; identity and access management; a function available both remotely and manually still counts; NOT RDPS: "remote analysis of telemetry data collected purely for statistical purposes or future product development" — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 194: an informational website the product redirects to is not RDPS; "an authentication portal that issues credentials or tokens required for the product ... to operate would be considered RDPS" — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 195-196: "under the responsibility of the manufacturer" means tailor-made for the manufacturer — "built solely by or on behalf of the manufacturer, based on designs and specifications provided by it"; licensing an existing third-party product/service "or slightly modified versions thereof" is not; who operates the solution is "not a decisive factor" — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 197-202 (cloud service models): on third-party IaaS, the manufacturer's own OS/applications may be RDPS; on third-party PaaS, the manufacturer's deployed application may be RDPS; a third-party SaaS integrated into the product "is therefore not designed and developed by the manufacturer" and is not RDPS; such non-RDPS elements (hypervisor, PaaS OS, SaaS app) must be treated "similar to third-party components" with risk assessment, product-level mitigations and due diligence akin to Art 13(5) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 205: RDPS subject to conformity assessment is limited to "those software modules that are responsible for the functionality of the product" and their interfaces; "further back-end systems ... with which the product with digital elements does not directly interact, are not considered RDPS" but remain external dependencies for the risk assessment (para 206) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 207: assurance artefacts reusable for third-party remote services include evidence of NIS2 compliance under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, DORA compliance, EUCC/Cybersecurity Act certificates, and ISO/IEC 27017:2015 or 27001:2022 conformity — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 208: a major change in a third-party remote service "should not qualify as a substantial modification" of the product, because those elements are not under the manufacturer's responsibility; the risk assessment may need revising — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance section 8.3 use cases: mobile banking app — the bank's self-hosted "banking interface" API is RDPS; account-management and ledger systems the app does not directly talk to are NOT RDPS; a third-party SaaS support-chat integrated in the app is NOT RDPS but a component to be risk-assessed; smart thermostat cloud functions on third-party IaaS ARE RDPS; e-reader using a generic third-party SaaS storage service: NOT RDPS (not developed under the manufacturer's responsibility) but treated as a component; industrial robot's manufacturer-developed vision service on IaaS IS RDPS — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission FAQ: "standalone Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) or other cloud solutions designed and developed outside the responsibility of a manufacturer ... are not themselves products with digital elements"; they are in scope only where they meet the RDPS definition — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)

### Cited Findings — internal-use software; bespoke/single-customer software; B2B
- Commission FAQ ("products manufactured only for one's own use"): "Placing on the market is considered not to take place where a product is manufactured for one's own use" (Blue Guide 2022 s.2.3); development and configuration tools a manufacturer builds for its own use are out of scope "unless they are placed on the market as separate products"; footnote 4: "The CRA ... does not cover products manufactured for own use in its scope" — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Recital 16: products developed or modified by a public administration "exclusively for its own use" are not made available on the market — [Springlex Art 1 page, Recital 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-1/)
- Commission guidance Example 7: source code licensed to a single company for its internal platform IS placed on the market by the licensor — i.e. bespoke commercial supply to one customer is in scope — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission FAQ ("tailor-made"): manufacturers may deviate from only two essential requirements — secure-by-default configuration (Annex I Part I point (2)(b)) and free security updates (Annex I Part II point (8)) — for "tailor-made products that are fitted to a particular purpose for a particular business user" where both parties "explicitly agreed to a different set of contractual terms" (Recital 64); examples: custom-developed hardware/software for a specific business user, or products for a customer's "closed networks or air-gapped environments"; NOT tailor-made: a CRM platform sold to many businesses with minor customisations, or plugin/API-customisable platforms that are "fundamentally the same product for every customer"; evidence of tailor-made status goes in the technical documentation (Art 31) — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission guidance para 246 (automotive context, but a general principle on B2B channels): placement on the market is judged on "the objective conditions under which it is made available"; the use of restricted B2B channels is evidence of exclusive intended use, whereas open retail/online channels bring a component into scope "irrespective of any statements concerning its intended use" — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

### Cited Findings — unfinished software, prototypes, trade fairs
- Art 4(2): prototypes and non-compliant products may be shown at trade fairs/exhibitions/demonstrations with a visible non-compliance sign; Art 4(3): Member States shall not prevent making available "unfinished software which does not comply" for "a limited period required for testing purposes" with a visible sign; Art 4(4): para 3 does not apply to safety components under other Union harmonisation legislation — [Springlex Art 4](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-4/)
- Commission FAQ: alpha/beta/release-candidate releases are allowed under Art 4(3) "only for the time necessary to test it and gather feedback"; Recital 37 requires a risk assessment and compliance "to the extent possible" and no forced upgrades to test versions — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)

### Inferences
- A pure browser-accessed SaaS admin console with no installed client is outside the CRA (it is not a product), but as soon as the vendor ships an installed agent, CLI, desktop or mobile client that depends on the console's backend to function, the vendor-built backend modules the client talks to become RDPS and are part of that client product's conformity assessment.
- The guidance treats "components" the vendor did not build (third-party SaaS, PaaS runtime, hypervisor) under Art 13(5)-style due diligence rather than as part of the product — a practical division of labour for engineering teams.
- B2B-only sale is irrelevant to scope per se (the CRA has no consumer-only limitation); it matters only for the "tailor-made" derogations and for classifying "important/critical" products.

### Gaps
- The Commission has not published guidance specifically on APIs, SDKs or ML model files as such; the SDK point is covered only via FOSS Example 33 (an SDK published by a not-for-profit is stewarded, not placed on the market). Treatment of ML model weights as "software" (computer code) is not addressed in the FAQ or guidance; see hard-cases table for a reasoned inference flagged as such.
- The guidance (para 9) says further guidance on interplay with the AI Act and DORA "may" follow; none was located as at 30 Oct 2026.

## KQ3. Exclusions and exceptions: Art 2(2)-(8) and related recitals; delegated acts adopted under Art 2(5)

### Takeaway
Art 2 excludes: products to which the MDR (2017/745), IVDR (2017/746) or motor-vehicle General Safety Regulation (2019/2144) apply (Art 2(2)); products certified under the EASA Basic Regulation 2018/1139 (Art 2(3)); equipment within the Marine Equipment Directive 2014/90/EU (Art 2(4)); identical-specification spare parts (Art 2(6)); products developed or modified exclusively for national security/defence or specifically designed to process classified information (Art 2(7)). Art 2(5) empowers delegated acts to limit/exclude products covered by other Union rules of equal or higher protection; one such act has been adopted — Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535 (L-category vehicles under Regulation 168/2013, except pedal-assist L1e), in force 18 Nov 2025. There is no general "research/prototype" exclusion in Art 2; that is handled by the "placing on the market" concept and Art 4.

### Cited Findings — operative text of Art 2(2)-(8)
- Art 2(2): "does not apply to products with digital elements to which the following Union legal acts apply: (a) Regulation (EU) 2017/745; (b) Regulation (EU) 2017/746; (c) Regulation (EU) 2019/2144" — [Springlex Art 2](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Art 2(3): does not apply to products "that have been certified in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/1139" — [Springlex Art 2](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Art 2(4): does not apply to equipment within the scope of Directive 2014/90/EU (marine equipment) — [Springlex Art 2](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Art 2(5): application to products covered by other Union rules addressing all or some Annex I risks "may be limited or excluded where: (a) such limitation or exclusion is consistent with the overall regulatory framework ...; and (b) the sectoral rules achieve the same or a higher level of protection"; second subparagraph empowers Commission delegated acts under Art 61 "specifying whether such limitation or exclusion is necessary, the products and rules concerned, as well as the scope of the limitation" — [Springlex Art 2](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Art 2(6): does not apply to spare parts "made available on the market to replace identical components ... and that are manufactured according to the same specifications as the components that they are intended to replace" — [Springlex Art 2](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Art 2(7): does not apply to products "developed or modified exclusively for national security or defence purposes or to products specifically designed to process classified information" — [Springlex Art 2](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Art 2(8): obligations "shall not entail the supply of information the disclosure of which would be contrary to the essential interests of Member States' national security, public security or defence" — [Springlex Art 2](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)

### Cited Findings — recitals and Commission interpretation of each exclusion
- Recital 25 (medical devices): MDR/IVDR lay down essential requirements for devices functioning through electronic systems "or that are software themselves", cover certain non-embedded software and a lifecycle approach, with cybersecurity guidance since December 2019; products to which either Regulation applies "should not therefore be subject to this Regulation" — [Springlex Art 2 page, Recital 25](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Recital 26 (national security): products developed/modified exclusively for national security or defence, or designed to process classified information, are out; Member States "are encouraged to ensure the same or a higher level of protection" — [Springlex Art 2 page, Recital 26](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Commission FAQ on dual-use: "dual-use" products with both civilian and defence applications "are therefore subject to the CRA when made available on the market, unless they are modified exclusively for national security or defence purposes"; Member States may add procurement/use requirements under Art 5(1) — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Recital 27 (vehicles and aviation): Regulation 2019/2144 type-approval incorporates UN Regulation No 155 (CSMS, software updates, lifecycle processes); Regulation 2018/1139 certification "ensures the level of assurance aimed for by this Regulation"; products under 2019/2144 and products certified under 2018/1139 are not subject to CRA essential requirements and conformity assessment — [Springlex Art 2 page, Recital 27](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Commission FAQ on aviation: only products actually certified under 2018/1139 are exempt; "drones in the open category" (most leisure and low-risk commercial use) that are not certified "may be covered by the CRA"; uncertified components intended for certified aircraft may be covered — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission FAQ on marine: equipment in scope of Directive 2014/90 is listed in the annex to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1533; components intended for integration into marine equipment but outside the Directive may be covered by the CRA — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission guidance para 241-246 (automotive components): components "exclusively designed and constructed for integration into vehicles" covered by 2019/2144 or 168/2013 are out of scope regardless of whether sold to the OEM or another tier; "generic components ... that can be integrated into different types of products" are in scope; selling through retail/online channels open to the public brings a component in scope irrespective of intended-use statements; restricted B2B automotive channels indicate exclusive use — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Recital 28 (future sectoral carve-outs): sectoral rules may limit/exclude CRA application if consistent with the framework and of at least the same protection; the Commission "should be empowered to adopt delegated acts ... identifying such products and rules" — [Springlex Art 2 page, Recital 28](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Recital 29 (spare parts): exemption covers spare parts repairing "legacy products made available before the date of application" and spare parts that have already undergone CRA conformity assessment — [Springlex Art 2 page, Recital 29](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/)
- Commission guidance para 96-102 and Examples 36-39 (spare parts): the exemption applies "only where the spare part is specifically supplied to repair or extend the durability" of an existing product — the repair purpose must be apparent from context (product identified in order/offer; after-sales/service channel) and evidence kept for authorities; a standalone product that could technically serve as a replacement is not a spare part; "identical" is judged on functional role and cybersecurity-relevant characteristics — different chipset/updated firmware with same protocols and security mechanisms can still be identical (Ex 38), but a different cryptographic implementation or secure-boot mechanism is not identical and the part is a CRA product in its own right (Ex 37); exemption applies whether replacing a component or a whole sub-product (e.g. an entire PLC within an automation system, Ex 39) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Recital 30 (Radio Equipment Directive): CRA essential requirements include all elements of RED Art 3(3)(d), (e), (f) as activated by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30; the Commission intends to repeal/amend 2022/30 so it ceases to apply to products subject to the CRA, with transitional guidance — [Springlex Art 2 page, Recital 30](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-2/); Commission FAQ footnote 5 links the Have-Your-Say initiative "Cybersecurity - repeal of Delegated Regulation supplementing the Radio Equipment Directive" — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Art 69(1): EU type-examination certificates and approval decisions on cybersecurity requirements under other Union harmonisation legislation "shall remain valid until 11 June 2028" unless they expire earlier or that legislation provides otherwise — [Springlex Art 69](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-69/); Commission guidance para 247-257 applies this to RED Delegated Act 2022/30 certificates and Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 certificates: they count as evidence only for the risks they cover, never beyond 11 June 2028, and never exempt from the CRA risk assessment — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

### Cited Findings — delegated acts adopted under Art 2(5) as at 30 Oct 2026
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535 of 29 July 2025, legal basis Art 2(5) second subparagraph CRA: Art 1 excludes from the CRA "products with digital elements that fall within the scope of Regulation (EU) No 168/2013" (L-category two/three-wheel vehicles and quadricycles) but "that exclusion shall not apply to L1e category vehicles designed to pedal" (Art 3 point (94)(b) of Reg 168/2013); Recital 4 relies on UN Regulation No 155 achieving "at least the same level of protection"; Recital 3 notes Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1455 made UN R155 compulsory for L-category vehicles except pedal-assist L1e; entry into force 18 November 2025 (20th day after OJ publication, OJ header date 29.10.2025) — [EUR-Lex CELEX:32025R1535](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R1535); confirmed in [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307) and [Commission guidance para 242](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- No other Art 2(5) delegated act was listed on the Commission's CRA implementation page (last updated 27 July 2026). Other implementing measures listed there: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 of 28 Nov 2025 on technical descriptions of important/critical products (Annex III/IV categories); Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/881 (adopted 11 Dec 2025, "publication pending objection period") on CSIRTs withholding notifications on the Single Reporting Platform; a planned Q4 2026 delegated act on presumption of conformity for the EUCC scheme — [Commission CRA implementation factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation)

### Inferences
- Exclusions are act-based ("to which ... apply"/"certified in accordance with"/"falls within the scope of"), so the test is whether the other act applies to that product, not whether the product is "medical-ish" or "automotive-ish". A medical device accessory that is itself a device under the MDR is out; a generic component sold to a device maker but not itself a device is in scope for its own manufacturer.
- For engineering teams, the automotive-component reasoning (exclusive design/intended use + restricted channel = out; generic or retail = in) is the Commission's template for all "component for an excluded sector" questions.

### Gaps
- No Art 2(5) delegated act has been adopted for any sector other than L-category vehicles as at 30 Oct 2026; no draft for other sectors (e.g. rail, energy) was found.
- The RED Delegated Regulation 2022/30 repeal/amendment: the FAQ links a Have-Your-Say initiative but no adopted repealing act was located in this research.

## KQ4. Economic operators (Chapter II): when manufacturer obligations attach; Art 13(1)-(4) design/development/production and risk-assessment duties; Arts 18-24

### Takeaway
Manufacturer obligations attach "when placing a product with digital elements on the market" and run per unit (Art 13(1); Recital 38; Blue Guide s.2.3), with software deemed placed on the market in all copies at the moment of first commercial supply (guidance para 13-14). Authorised representatives are optional ("may", Art 18(1)) — the CRA does not oblige non-EU manufacturers to appoint one. Importers (Art 19) and distributors (Art 20) carry verification duties and become manufacturers if they rebrand or substantially modify (Art 21); anyone else who substantially modifies and makes available becomes a manufacturer for the affected part, or the whole product if whole-product security is affected (Art 22). Traceability records: 10 years upstream and downstream (Art 23). Stewards have a light regime (Art 24).

### Cited Findings — Art 13 (scope-related obligations)
- Art 13(1): "When placing a product with digital elements on the market, manufacturers shall ensure that it has been designed, developed and produced in accordance with the essential cybersecurity requirements set out in Part I of Annex I" — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(2): manufacturers "shall undertake an assessment of the cybersecurity risks" and take its outcome into account "during the planning, design, development, production, delivery and maintenance phases" — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(3): the risk assessment "shall be documented and updated as appropriate during a support period"; must at least analyse risks "based on the intended purpose and reasonably foreseeable use, as well as the conditions of use" (operational environment, assets to be protected, expected time in use); must indicate whether and how Annex I Part I point (2) requirements apply and how Part I point (1) and Part II vulnerability-handling requirements are applied — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(4): the risk assessment is included in the technical documentation (Art 31, Annex VII); for Art 12 products also subject to other Union acts it may form part of that other risk assessment; where requirements are not applicable the manufacturer "shall include a clear justification" — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(5): due diligence when integrating third-party components "including when integrating components of free and open-source software that have not been made available on the market in the course of a commercial activity"; Art 13(6): report vulnerabilities found in components (including open-source) upstream and share fixes "where appropriate in a machine-readable format" — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(8): support period reflects expected time in use; "shall be at least five years" unless expected use is shorter; Art 13(9): each security update to remain available "for a minimum of 10 years or for the remainder of the support period, whichever is longer"; Art 13(10): where subsequent substantially modified versions of software are placed on the market, Part II point (2) compliance may be ensured only for the latest version if earlier users get it free and without environment-adjustment costs; Art 13(11): public software archives allowed with clear risk warnings — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(12)-(13): before placing on the market, draw up technical documentation, carry out conformity assessment (Art 32), draw up EU declaration of conformity (Art 28), affix CE marking (Art 30); keep documentation and declaration "for at least 10 years after the product ... has been placed on the market or for the support period, whichever is longer" — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(14)-(16): series-production conformity procedures; type/batch/serial number or other identifier; manufacturer name, registered trade name/trademark, postal address, email/digital contact, website on the product, packaging or accompanying document and in Annex II user information — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(17)-(20): single point of contact for users (not limited to automated tools); Annex II information and instructions in paper or electronic form kept available 10 years or support period; end-of-support date (month and year) shown at purchase; end-of-support notification where feasible; copy or simplified EU declaration of conformity — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Art 13(21)-(23): corrective action/withdrawal/recall from placing on the market and through the support period; information to market surveillance authorities on reasoned request; manufacturer ceasing operations must inform authorities and users beforehand — [Springlex Art 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)
- Recital 38: requirements "apply to each individual product with digital elements when placed on the market, irrespective of whether ... manufactured as an individual unit or in series" — [Springlex Art 21 page, Recital 38](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-21/)
- Commission guidance para 11-16 (placing on the market for software): per the Blue Guide s.2.3 the concept refers "to each individual product, not to a type of product"; for standalone software, placing on the market occurs "at the moment of the first offering for distribution or use" once the manufacturing phase is complete, and all copies of that version are deemed placed on the market at the same time regardless of later download dates (Ex 1); non-substantial updates do not reset that date (Ex 2); variants differing in components, configuration or enabled features (OS builds, feature bundles) are distinct products; this applies only to standalone software, not hardware+software combinations — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission FAQ ("product type developed before CRA applies"): Union harmonisation legislation applies to individual products, not types; units of a non-compliant type placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027 need not comply, but further units of that type "cannot be placed on the market on or after 11 December 2027" — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Commission guidance para 33-39 (legacy designs): products designed before 11 Dec 2027 but placed on the market after may be placed "without redesign" if the Art 13(2) risk assessment and technical documentation demonstrate appropriate security; no need to recreate historical design/test documentation; conformity assessment, declaration of conformity and CE marking are still required (Ex 12 microcontroller) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission guidance para 174-177 (product families): variants sharing architecture, security-relevant design, intended purpose and risks may rely on a single risk assessment, single technical documentation, single conformity assessment and a single EU declaration of conformity listing the variants; differences in interfaces, software stacks, update mechanisms or remote connectivity must be reflected — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

### Cited Findings — Art 18 authorised representatives (optional)
- Art 18(1): "A manufacturer may, by a written mandate, appoint an authorised representative" — permissive, no obligation for non-EU manufacturers — [Springlex Art 18](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-18/)

### Cited Findings — Art 19 importers

### Cited Findings — Art 20 distributors
- Art 20(1): distributors "shall act with due care"; Art 20(2): before making available verify the CE marking and that the manufacturer and importer complied with Art 13(15), (16), (18), (19), (20) and Art 19(4) and provided all necessary documents — [Springlex Art 20](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-20/)
- Art 20(3)-(6): do not make available products believed non-conforming "on the basis of information in its possession"; inform manufacturer and authorities where significant risk; ensure corrective measures; inform manufacturer of vulnerabilities without undue delay; cooperate with authorities; inform authorities/users if manufacturer ceased operations — [Springlex Art 20](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-20/)
- Commission FAQ: distributors are not required to bring pre-11 Dec 2027 products into compliance "unless they carry out a substantial modification" — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)

### Cited Findings — Arts 21-22: becoming the manufacturer of someone else's product
- Art 21: "An importer or distributor shall be considered to be a manufacturer ... and shall be subject to Articles 13 and 14, where that importer or distributor places a product with digital elements on the market under its name or trademark or carries out a substantial modification of a product with digital elements already placed on the market" — [Springlex Art 21](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-21/)
- Art 22(1): any natural or legal person other than the manufacturer, importer or distributor "that carries out a substantial modification ... and makes that product available on the market, shall be considered to be a manufacturer"; Art 22(2): subject to Arts 13 and 14 "for the part of the product with digital elements that is affected by the substantial modification or, if the substantial modification has an impact on the cybersecurity of the product with digital elements as a whole, for the entire product" — [Springlex Art 22](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-22/)
- Art 69(2)-(3): products placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027 are subject to the CRA "only if, from that date, those products are subject to a substantial modification"; but Art 14 reporting applies to all in-scope products regardless of placing date — [Springlex Art 69](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-69/)

- Commission guidance para 59 and Example 19: a service provider installing FOSS on a customer's server without substantially modifying it is not placing it on the market; one who substantially modifies FOSS as part of the service falls under Art 22 — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Recital 78: a marketplace selling "its own branded products with digital elements ... would qualify as a manufacturer" — [Springlex Art 3 page, Recital 78](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)

### Cited Findings — Art 23 identification/traceability
- Art 23(1): economic operators shall on request give authorities the name and address of "any economic operator who has supplied them" and, where available, "any economic operator to whom they have supplied" a product; Art 23(2): able to present this "for 10 years after they have been supplied with the product ... and for 10 years after they have supplied" it — [Springlex Art 23](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-23/)

### Cited Findings — Art 24 open-source software stewards (headline only)
- Art 24(1): stewards shall "put in place and document in a verifiable manner a cybersecurity policy" fostering secure development, effective vulnerability handling, and voluntary reporting under Art 15; Art 24(2): cooperate with market surveillance authorities on request; Art 24(3): provide the policy documentation on reasoned request; Art 24(4): Art 14(1) reporting applies "to the extent that they are involved in the development"; Art 14(3) and (8) apply to severe incidents affecting network and information systems the steward provides for development — [Springlex Art 24](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-24/)
- Commission guidance para 71-74, 84: a legal person can be steward for one FOSS and manufacturer for another (e.g. steward of the free "community" edition and manufacturer of the paid edition); a steward that starts monetising becomes manufacturer from that date (not for earlier versions) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

### Inferences
- A non-EU vendor selling directly into the EU without an importer or authorised representative has no EU-established operator for authorities to address under the CRA itself, but Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 Art 4 (into whose Annex I the CRA is inserted by CRA Art 66) requires an EU "economic operator" (manufacturer, importer, authorised representative or fulfilment service provider) for products covered by that Annex — so in practice a non-EU manufacturer of a product subject to Art 4 of 2019/1020 will need one. This cross-reference should be checked by legal counsel; the CRA Art 66 insertion is cited at [Springlex Art 66](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-66/).
- White-labelling (selling a third-party product under your own brand) makes you the manufacturer under Art 3(13) read with Art 21, with full Art 13/14 obligations — the original developer's compliance helps as evidence but does not transfer responsibility.

### Gaps
- Whether Regulation 2019/1020 Art 4 (responsible EU economic operator) actually bites on all CRA products, or only on those also covered by other Annex I legislation listed in Art 4(5) of that Regulation, was not verified in this research; it is flagged above as an inference for legal review.

## KQ5. Substantial modification: definition, recital examples, Commission tests, consequence

### Takeaway
A substantial modification is any post-placing change that affects compliance with Annex I Part I or alters the assessed intended purpose (Art 3(30)). Recital 39 and the Commission's 27 July 2026 guidance make the test risk-based, not size-based: security updates that reduce risk and keep the intended purpose are not substantial (even if technically large); feature updates that change intended purpose or add unassessed risk are; features already foreseen and assessed in the risk assessment are not. The consequence is a new placing on the market and, where applicable, a new (focused) conformity assessment, with the modified version becoming the version to which the support period attaches.

### Cited Findings — law and recitals
- Art 3(30) definition (see KQ1) — [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Recital 39: a software change is substantial where it "modifies the intended purpose ... and those changes were not foreseen by the manufacturer in the initial risk assessment", or where "the nature of the hazard has changed or the level of cybersecurity risk has increased", and the updated version is made available on the market; a security update designed to decrease risk that does not change intended purpose "is not considered to be a substantial modification" — this usually covers minor source-code adjustments and fixes to known vulnerabilities even where functions/performance are modified solely to reduce risk; minor functionality updates ("a visual enhancement or the addition of new pictograms or languages") are generally not substantial; a feature update that modifies original functions or type/performance and meets the criteria is substantial because "the addition of new features typically leads to a broader attack surface" — e.g. "a new input element is added to an application, requiring ... adequate input validation"; bundling a feature with a security update does not change the analysis; the Commission "should issue guidance" — [Springlex Art 3 page, Recital 39](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Recital 41: where a substantial modification occurs the product's compliance "is verified and ... where applicable, it undergoes a new conformity assessment"; notify the third-party body where one is involved — [Springlex Art 3 page, Recital 41](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Recital 42: refurbishment, maintenance and repair (as defined in Ecodesign Regulation 2024/1781 Art 2(18)-(20)) do not necessarily cause substantial modification if intended purpose/functionalities and risk level are unchanged; an "upgrade" might — [Springlex Art 3 page, Recital 42](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-3/)
- Recital 40 and Art 13(10): after substantially modified versions, security updates may be limited to the latest version if earlier users get it free without environment costs — [Springlex Art 13 page, Recital 40](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)

### Cited Findings — Commission guidance (27 July 2026), section 4
- Para 91: substantial modification matters for (a) Art 21 importers/distributors, (b) Art 22 other persons, (c) Art 69(2) legacy products modified after 11 Dec 2027, and (d) determining when iterative software changes require a new conformity assessment — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 95 and Example 35: replacing worn/defective parts with better-performing parts is not substantial unless compliance or assessed intended purpose changes (server RAM upgrade: not substantial) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 104-105, Examples 40-41: new functionality changing the product's intended purpose is "generally" substantial — a read-only machine dashboard gaining control capabilities (Ex 40); a personal-data organiser gaining automated behavioural profiling and content decisions (Ex 41) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 106, Examples 42-43: features "already included in its risk assessment" or that do not alter the risk profile are NOT substantial — group chat with admin/moderation tools foreseen in the original design (Ex 42); enabling pre-built, pre-assessed automated-control loops with foreseen safeguards (Ex 43) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 107, Examples 44-45: assessment is "not ... based on the scale or complexity of the change" but on its impact on the risk profile — a "remember me" persistent-login feature storing tokens locally IS substantial (unassessed token theft/session hijacking risk, Ex 44); a diagnostics/log-export feature storing sensitive data unencrypted IS substantial (Ex 45) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 108, Examples 46-48: security updates are generally not substantial "even where that update may introduce significant technical changes" — fixing buffer overflow/auth-bypass (Ex 46); tightening firewall rules, disabling ports, changing password policy, making MFA mandatory where already available/foreseen (Ex 47); disabling a deprecated algorithm and activating an already-supported one under a crypto-agility design (Ex 48) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 109, Examples 49-50: a security-motivated update IS substantial if it changes intended purpose or adds unassessed risk — replacing local encryption with a mandatory remote encryption service (Ex 49); replacing an internal key lifecycle with a third-party external key-management service, adding new external interfaces/dependencies (Ex 50) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 110 (non-exhaustive checklist): does the update (a) introduce new threat vectors (interfaces, channels, execution environments, external dependencies); (b) enable new attack scenarios; (c) change the likelihood of identified attack scenarios (lower effort, more exposure, weaker safeguards); (d) change the potential impact (scope of data/functions, severity, detectability/recoverability)? — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 113: regardless of qualification, manufacturers must secure updates during the support period and keep risk assessment and technical documentation up to date (Arts 13(7), 31(2)) — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 116, 122-124 (consequence): a substantially modified product made available "is to be treated as a new product" and this "constitutes a new placing on the market", whether by the original manufacturer or another person; the original manufacturer "remains the manufacturer" but the modified product is "newly placed on the market"; it may reuse documentation/tests for unaffected aspects and the conformity assessment (including by a notified body) "should focus on the substantially modified parts"; for pre-11 Dec 2027 products, a substantial modification by the original manufacturer does not of itself require bringing the whole legacy product into compliance "unless the modification negatively affects the cybersecurity of the product ... as a whole" — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Para 15 and Example 2: iterations that are not substantial "do not modify that software's date of placement on the market"; substantial ones are "newly placed on the market" — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)
- Commission FAQ smart-TV example: a 2028 bug-fix update to a mid-2027 TV does not trigger the CRA; a 2029 update enabling the TV "to control smart home systems" is substantial and requires bringing the TV into compliance — [Commission FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307)
- Industry position: the German machine-tool association VDW published a position paper on CRA substantial modifications dated 7 July 2026 (not reviewed in detail; corroborates industry concern) — [VDW position paper](https://vdw.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/VDW_PosPap_CRA_SubstantialModifications_EN_2026-07-07.pdf)

### Inferences
- The practical engineering control is the risk assessment's forward-looking scope: features and configuration changes described and assessed up-front (roadmap items, disabled-but-present capabilities, crypto-agility) can be shipped later without a new placing on the market. Teams should treat the Art 13(3) risk assessment as a living document that explicitly enumerates planned functionality.
- "Substantial modification" and "support period" interact: a substantially modified version restarts the clock for that version (guidance section 5.1 — not reproduced in these notes; covered by the support-period researcher).

### Gaps
- Section 5.1 of the guidance (substantial modification and support period) was not extracted here; it belongs to the lifecycle/support-period workstream.

## KQ7. Amendments to CRA scope proposed or adopted since entry into force (Digital Omnibus, Nov 2025; other 2025-2026 acts)

### Takeaway
No adopted amendment to the CRA's scope, definitions, exclusions or economic-operator roles was found as at 30 Oct 2026. The only adopted change is the corrigendum of 17 Oct 2025 (wording). The Digital Omnibus (COM proposal of 19 Nov 2025, procedure 2025/0360(COD)) creates a single entry point for incident reporting (NIS2, GDPR, DORA, eIDAS, CER) built on the CRA's reporting platform, but the sources reviewed do not show it amending the CRA's scope; it remains a proposal in Council (Irish presidency mandate, Council doc 13886/26, Coreper 11 Oct 2026) with no adoption. The Cybersecurity Act revision (proposed January 2026) is also still a proposal and no CRA scope change was identified in it.

### Cited Findings
- Digital Omnibus: Commission unveiled the package on 19 November 2025 — [Hunton, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/eu-digital-omnibus-introduces-a-single-reporting-point-for-cybersecurity-incidents); European Parliament Legislative Observatory procedure 2025/0360(COD), "Simplification of the digital legislative framework – Digital Omnibus (Omnibus VII)", dated 19/11/2025, ordinary legislative procedure; accompanied by 2025/0359(COD) amending the AI Act — [OEIL](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1866785)

- The Commission's own guidance (27 July 2026) footnote 2 notes the 2026 work programme envisages a "European Product Act" updating the New Legislative Framework, market surveillance and standardisation rules (COM(2025) 870) — a potential future horizontal change, not a CRA amendment — [Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131456)

| 28 | Product placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027 | OUT of Chapter II/III requirements unless substantially modified after that date; Art 14 reporting applies regardless | Art 69(2)-(3); FAQ | [Springlex Art 69](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-69/); [FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/123307) |

Note on row 27: guidance para 182 lists "the distribution of security updates to edge locations" as not RDPS (part of the manufacturer's infrastructure), while para 190 lists "automated distribution of updates, including feature update and security patching" among product functions whose remote processing can be RDPS. The reconciling reading is that the user-facing update service the product calls is RDPS, while internal delivery pipelines behind it are not; this is an inference.
