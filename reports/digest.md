# Cross-cutting digest of the nine research notes

Coordinator's compilation from the researchers' hand-back summaries, 30 October 2026. Where this digest and a notes file disagree, the notes file wins. Where two notes files disagree, prefer the one citing primary text and flag the discrepancy.

## 1. Scope and exclusions (scope_and_exclusions.md)

- Three cumulative scope conditions (Art 2(1); Commission FAQ): product with digital elements + made available on the EU market in the course of a commercial activity + intended or reasonably foreseeable direct or indirect, logical or physical data connection. Offline desktop software is in (indirect connection via the OS); appliances with no connectivity are out.
- Software is a product only if supplied to and executed on the user's side (guidance paras 20-21). Browser-only web apps, SaaS and websites are not products; they are caught only as "remote data processing solutions" (RDPS) of an in-scope product where built by or under the responsibility of the manufacturer and necessary for one of the product's functions. Third-party SaaS integrated into a product is a component subject to Art 13(5) due diligence, not RDPS. Back-end systems the product does not talk to directly are not RDPS.
- Internal-use software is not placed on the market (Blue Guide s.2.3; FAQ). Bespoke software supplied to one customer IS placed on the market (guidance Ex 7) but may use the "tailor-made" derogations for only two requirements (secure-by-default, free updates). B2B-only sale is irrelevant to scope.
- Exclusions: MDR, IVDR, Reg 2019/2144 (Art 2(2)); EASA-certified products only (Art 2(3); open-category drones are in); Marine Equipment Directive (Art 2(4)); identical-spec spare parts (Art 2(6)); exclusively national security/defence/classified (Art 2(7); dual-use is in). Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535 (Art 2(5)): L-category vehicles except pedal-assist L1e, in force 18 Nov 2025. No general R&D/prototype exclusion: handled via "placing on the market" and Art 4(2)-(3).
- Roles: manufacturer = markets under own name or trademark, paid or free (Art 3(13)); obligations attach per unit at placing; for standalone software all copies of a version are deemed placed at first offering (guidance paras 13-14). Authorised representative is optional ("may", Art 18(1)); Art 13(1)-(11), 13(12) first subparagraph and 13(14) cannot be delegated. Importer or distributor becomes manufacturer on own-brand sale or substantial modification (Art 21); any other substantial modifier who makes available becomes manufacturer for the affected part or the whole (Art 22). Integration into a new product is not substantial modification: the integrator is manufacturer of the whole (guidance Ex 51). Traceability records 10 years up- and downstream (Art 23). Art 3 has 51 definitions.
- Substantial modification is risk-based: security updates that reduce risk and keep the intended purpose are not substantial; features already foreseen in the risk assessment are not substantial; small features adding unassessed risk (e.g. "remember me" token storage) are. Consequence: new placing on the market, focused re-assessment of changed parts. Pre-11 Dec 2027 products are caught only if substantially modified after that date (Art 69(2)); Art 14 applies regardless (Art 69(3)).
- Commission guidance: "FAQs on the Cyber Resilience Act" (first 3 Dec 2025, v1.4 of 4 Sept 2026, ~66 pp, living, non-official); Communication C(2026) 5252 final of 27 July 2026 with an ~84-page guidance annex (67 examples): the Art 26 guidance covering placing on the market for software, software vs SaaS/web apps, source code, bundles, the data-connection test, complex systems, legacy designs, FOSS, substantial modification (four-point checklist, 16 worked examples), spare parts, product families, RDPS (three-element test; IaaS/PaaS/SaaS; banking/thermostat/e-reader/robot use cases), vehicle legislation, Art 69(1). Caveat: content approved 27 July 2026; formal adoption "at a later date when all language versions are available"; no evidence of adoption or OJ C publication by 30 Oct 2026; expressly non-binding. A corrigendum to the CRA was published 17 Oct 2025.
- No adopted change to scope, definitions or roles as at 30 Oct 2026. Digital Omnibus (COM(2025) 837, 19 Nov 2025, 2025/0360(COD)) concerns a single incident-reporting entry point; no CRA scope change. Cybersecurity Act revision (CSA2) proposed 20 Jan 2026, still a proposal.
- Gaps: no Commission guidance on APIs/SDKs/ML model files as such. Whether Reg 2019/1020 Art 4 requires an EU responsible operator for all CRA products: answered in the timeline notes (no: CRA Art 66 adds the CRA to Annex I of Reg 2019/1020 but not to Art 4(5)).

## 2. Classification and conformity (classification_and_conformity.md)

- Annex III = 19 Class I + 4 Class II categories; Annex IV = 3 critical categories. The Art 7(4) implementing act is adopted: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 of 28 Nov 2025 (OJ L 1 Dec 2025, in force 21 Dec 2025, CELEX 32025R2392) with per-category technical descriptions (browsers incl. AI-agent browsers; toys: proximity sensing is not location tracking; wearables for children under 14; Class II tamper-resistant chips = AVA_VAN 2-3; secure elements = AVA_VAN.4+ incl. TPMs/eUICC; security boxes incl. HSMs, payment terminals, tachographs). Recitals set "core functionality" rules (integration doesn't reclassify the host; ancillary functions don't declassify; SOAR is not SIEM; a smartphone is not an OS). Guidance C(2026) 5252: one core functionality per product; separately sold modules are separate products (SIEM module Class I, IDS module Class II, analytics default); objective test, not marketing. No Art 7(3) delegated act amending Annex III and no Art 8(1) delegated act mandating certification adopted or consulted on.
- Routes (Art 32): default → Module A; Class I → Module A only if an OJ-cited harmonised standard, common specification or Art 27(9)-listed scheme is applied in full and covers all core-functionality risks (guidance pt 149), else Module B+C or H; Class II → B+C, H or a "substantial"-level EU certificate; critical → B+C/H until an Art 8(1) act exists; Art 32(5): FOSS in Annex III may use any route incl. Module A if the Annex VII documentation is public. Annex VIII Modules A/B/C/H: notified-body application contents, periodic notified-body audits of vulnerability handling under Module B pt 8, retention 10 years or the support period, notified-body identification number on the CE mark only under Module H. Art 12 AI Act interplay: deemed compliance with AI Act Art 15; AI Act Art 43 route, except important/critical products under AI Act Annex VI internal control → CRA route.
- No CRA harmonised standard cited in the OJ (checks to Oct 2026). Commission proposed (July 2026, not yet adopted) pushing M/606 deadlines to 31 Oct 2026 (horizontal) and 31 Dec 2026 (vertical). No common specifications. Art 27(9) EUCC presumption delegated act "planned Q4 2026", not adopted. Consequence: Class I products assessed now must plan for B+C/H.
- Artefacts: Annex VII (8 element groups incl. SBOM, CVD policy, contact address, secure-update mechanism, risk assessment, support-period rationale, standards list, test reports, copy of the DoC; SBOM supplied to MSAs on reasoned request); Art 13(13) retention of at least 10 years or the support period; any language internally but readable by the authority on reasoned request (Art 13(22)); Annex V (8 elements) and Annex VI simplified DoC wording; Art 30: software CE mark on the DoC or an accompanying website. Guidance allows a single risk assessment, documentation and DoC for product families (pts 174-177).
- Notified bodies: zero CRA notified bodies verifiably listed in NANDO (checks to 8 Sept 2026; Commission page 31 July 2026 says "once available"). Art 35(2): Member States "strive" for sufficient bodies by 11 Dec 2026. Accreditation EN ISO/IEC 17065 (B+C) and 17021-1 (H); Germany BSI (with DAkkS), Finland Traficom (FINAS), Malta MDIA, Hungary SZTFH documented; Art 43 objection windows 2 weeks (accredited) or 2 months. Brightsight (NL) and Applus+ publicly in the accreditation pipeline. Vendor-only estimates: 12-18 months per body to designation; 3-6 months per Module B+C engagement; EUR 20-60k Module B, EUR 80-150k first Module H.
- SMEs (Art 33): no simplified technical documentation implementing act adopted or planned; sandboxes optional, none identified.
- Substantial modification (guidance pts 104-124): four-question test (new threat vectors, new attack scenarios, changed likelihood, changed impact); security updates "generally not" substantial unless they change the intended purpose or add external dependencies/interfaces; a substantial modification is a new placing on the market but assessment focuses on the modified parts.
- Caveat: two mirrors disagree whether Module H pt 4 contains an "unexpected visits" clause.
- Primary sources used: the Commission FAQ v1.4 and text extractions of guidance C(2026) 5252 (main text and annexes), downloaded during research and not kept in this repo.

## 3. Annex I Part I (annex1_part1_requirements.md)

- Point (1) is the only absolute obligation ("appropriate level of cybersecurity based on the risks"). All thirteen items (2)(a)-(m) sit under one chapeau: "On the basis of the cybersecurity risk assessment referred to in Article 13(2) and where applicable". Items (c) automatic updates and (m) data transfer carry a second "where applicable"; (b) carries the tailor-made B2B carve-out. "Known exploitable vulnerabilities" is (2)(a). Art 13(3) requires the risk assessment to state whether and how each point (2) item applies and how it is implemented; Art 13(4) and Recital 55 require justification for non-applicable items and treatment of residual risk. Recitals 54 and 38: no known exploitable vulnerabilities and all available patches applied at placing on the market.
- "Known exploitable vulnerabilities": no official definition. Draft ETSI EN 304 621 (v1.0.4, 20 Jul 2026) KEV_EXPLOIT-1: no KEVs, or none reported within 14 days / the CVD window, or a publicly documented mitigation; "known" = published in EUVD or an industry database or reported to the manufacturer. Draft EN 304 626 (operating systems): "at first use" with five testable mitigations.
- Guidance: C(2026) 5252 does not walk through Annex I items (it covers scope/RDPS, substantial modification, support periods, risk assessment paras 156-163: cost or commercial strategy alone cannot justify unaddressed risk). ENISA: JRC-ENISA standards mapping published 4 April 2024 (no newer edition found); Secure by Design and Default Playbook v1.0 (July 2026) with 22 principles, per-principle "minimum evidence", indicative Annex I mapping (Annex C); Playbook Annex B reproduces Annex I verbatim. BSI TR-03183-1 v1.0.0 (31 Jul 2026): non-binding ISO 31000-style method with PASS/FAIL/N/A verdicts.
- Horizontal standards: EN 40000-1-1/-1-2 completed formal approval, ratification scheduled 2 Nov 2026; prEN 40000-1-3 in draft; EN 40000-1-4 (generic security requirements, due 30 Oct 2027) structured Annex I item → security objective → EN 18031-style control (ACM, AUM, SUM, GEC, RLM, TCM, NMM plus new DTM, LIM, GEC-9..13); TR 40000-1-5 discontinued into -1-4. ETSI released 17 final-draft EN 304 6xx verticals in Aug 2026, each with clause 5.x mapped one-to-one to (2)(a)-(m); drafts readable at labs.etsi.org/rep/stan4cra/en-304-6xx.
- Remote data processing: Annex I extends to manufacturer-developed backends required for a product function (Art 3(2); Recitals 11-12). EN 304 621 Annex R applies core requirements to the product as a whole incl. RDPS endpoints, excluding the underlying third-party infrastructure. Indirect connection: Recital 9. Microcontrollers: separately sold chips are PDEs (TI Oct 2026, Microchip Aug 2026 — vendor), EN 50765/50766/50767 future harmonised standards; the integrator carries product-level defaults, reset, logging and the technical file.
- Mapping assembled from JRC-ENISA (pre-adoption numbering flagged), CEN PT2 slides, EN 18031-1 IDs, ETSI EN 303 645 5.1-5.13, IEC 62443-4-2 CRs, OWASP ISVS Appendix B, NIST SP 800-213A, SCF 2026.3.

## 4. Annex I Part II and lifecycle (annex1_part2_vulnerability_handling.md)

- Annex I Part II points 1-8 and Annex II points 1-9 reproduced. Article 13 has 25 paragraphs: 13(2)-(3) risk assessment maintained during the support period; 13(5) due diligence incl. non-commercial FOSS; 13(6) report vulnerabilities upstream and share fixes (machine-readable where appropriate); 13(7) systematic documentation; 13(8) support period (at least 5 years unless a shorter expected use time; criteria; information in Annex VII); 13(9) each security update available for at least 10 years or the remainder of the support period; 13(10)-(11) latest-version rule and public archives with warnings; 13(13)/(18) 10-year retention of technical documentation and user information; 13(16)-(17) contact details and single point of contact (not automated-only); 13(19) end-of-support month and year at purchase plus end-of-support notification; 13(21) corrective action, withdrawal, recall; 13(22) MSA requests; 13(23) cessation of operations notification; 13(24) SBOM implementing-act power; 13(25) ADCO dependency-assessment SBOM requests.
- SBOM: legal minimum = commonly used machine-readable format, at least top-level dependencies; Recital 77: not obliged to make the SBOM public; it lives in the Annex VII technical documentation (point 2(b)) and is supplied to MSAs on reasoned request (point 8); Annex II point 9 makes user access optional. No Commission implementing act on SBOM format adopted or drafted; no delegated act on minimum support periods.
- Automatic updates: Annex I Part I 2(c) (default-on, opt-out, notify, postpone), supported by Part II(7); Recital 56 carve-outs for components and professional or industrial environments.
- Commission FAQ (v1.4): no duty to patch every vulnerability (risk-based remedies incl. advisories and configuration guidance, worked examples); separation of security and functionality updates with examples; a due-diligence actions list (SCA, sandboxing, reviewing the component SBOM, support period, supplier posture, fuzzing, pentest); support-period criteria; shorter than 5 years only where the lifetime is shorter (subscription software, pandemic apps); a manufacturer is not liable if a user declines updates; recall only in exceptional cases.
- Guidance C(2026) 5252: Section 5 support period (5 years is a floor, not the default; substantial modification requires reassessment but does not reset the period; Art 13(10) "additional costs" interpreted); section 7.3 due diligence vs risk assessment; section 9.2.1 upstream reporting (no duplicate reports, licence-compatible fixes, no obligation that fixes are merged); 9.2.2 when a vulnerability is "known" (EUVD or other databases, researcher disclosure, media); 9.2.3 "regular tests" means threat-driven review, not fixed-interval repetition.
- ENISA: draft Technical Advisory on Secure Update Mechanisms v0.1 (May 2026; signing, anti-rollback, default auto-update, advisory linkage; no numeric timelines); draft Technical Advisory on security.txt, CVD policies and security advisories ("SME Vulnerability Kit", Oct 2026, consultation; RFC 9116 profile, CVD policy template, CSAF / ISO/IEC 20153 advisory template with CVE/EUVD IDs; cites the draft prEN 40000-1-3 7-day acknowledgement cap); ENISA is a CVE Root since 20 Nov 2025 (20 CNAs by 6 Aug 2026); SRP live 11 Sep 2026; ENISA/JRC 2024 mapping: ISO/IEC 30111 covers 5 of 8 Part II items, 29147 covers 4.
- Standards: FprEN 40000-1-3 vulnerability handling: enquiry Mar-Apr 2026, formal-vote draft agreed, publication expected end-2026 to Feb 2027, not yet cited; EN 40000-1-1/1-2 formal vote closed 1 Oct 2026, publication planned 18 Nov 2026 (not expected to be cited).
- BSI TR-03183-2 v2.1.0 (20 Aug 2025): CycloneDX 1.6+ or SPDX 3.0.1+, SHA-512, per-component required fields, recursive depth to the first out-of-scope component, SBOM must not contain vulnerability data (use CSAF/VEX).
- No numeric definition of "without delay" in any official source.

## 5. Reporting (reporting_obligations.md)

- Art 3(42) "actively exploited vulnerability" = reliable evidence a malicious actor exploited it without permission; "severe incident" test = Art 14(5)(a)-(b). Guidance para 213: the clock starts at a "reasonable degree of certainty" after an immediate initial assessment; suspicion starts triage, not the clock. Good-faith research findings and unexploited or unreachable component CVEs are not mandatory reports (FAQ 5.2, 5.4; guidance para 218). No retroactive reporting of exploitation known before 11 Sept 2026 (para 217).
- Art 14(2)(a)-(c) actively exploited vulnerabilities: 24h early warning / 72h notification / final report no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available. Art 14(4)(a)-(c) severe incidents: 24h / 72h / final report within 1 month after the 72h notification. Art 14(6) intermediate updates on CSIRT request. The ENISA SRP glossary defines the field sets (mandatory UTC "date/time became aware" at early warning; "general information" and "initial assessment" mandatory at 72h; severity, impact, actor and fix details mandatory at the final report). ENISA FAQ 26: the 72h counter currently runs 48h from early-warning submission.
- Art 14(7): via the SRP to the CSIRT coordinator of the main-establishment Member State (where product cybersecurity decisions are predominantly taken; else most EU employees), simultaneously accessible to ENISA; non-EU manufacturers: authorised representative → importer → distributor → most users. A wrong CSIRT choice may invalidate the filing (ENISA FAQ 18). Art 16(2) dissemination delay on cybersecurity grounds (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/881, adopted 11 Dec 2025, OJ 20 April 2026, in force 10 May 2026) and "particularly exceptional circumstances" (ENISA receives limited information; the PEC toggle exists only on the AEV 72h tab).
- Platform: ENISA SRP live 11 Sept 2026 at https://portal.cra-srp.enisa.europa.eu/ as "initial operating capability": web form only (no API), English only, Art 14 mandatory reports only (Art 15 voluntary deferred). EU Login + MFA; one Primary and up to 20 Secondary "Assigned Representatives"; the CSIRT validates the association (observed 3 business days in Belgium); up to 20 notifications may be filed while pending. ENISA listed coordinating CSIRTs for all 27 Member States on 4 Sept 2026. Documentation: FAQ (31 items), glossary, AR User Manual, registration/submission/interface/PEC guidance, factsheet in 10 languages, T&Cs, help desk cra-srp-helpdesk@enisa.europa.eu. Fallbacks: Ireland cra_fallback@ncsc.gov.ie (only if ENISA declares an outage); BSI: email CERT-Bund only if the SRP is down, reports in English. No official statistics; one public first-hand account (Aikido, 23 Sept 2026: launch-day glitch fixed within hours).
- Only Delegated Regulation 2026/881 exists; no implementing act under Art 14(10) on format and procedure adopted or drafted; the ENISA glossary is the de facto format. No severity scale prescribed.
- Art 14(8) user notification: without undue delay, impacted users and where appropriate all users, machine-readable where appropriate; guidance paras 219-221: risk-based and proportionate (initial disclosure may be limited to affected customers; broader disclosure once mitigated); CSIRT backstop if the manufacturer is not timely.
- Art 15 voluntary reporting (not yet in the SRP; the CSIRT must inform the manufacturer of third-party reports, Art 15(4)); Art 19(5)/20(4) importer and distributor duties (from 11 Dec 2027); Art 24(3) stewards subject to Art 14(1), (3), (8) from 11 Dec 2027 to the extent involved; stewards exempt from fines (Art 64(10)(b)).
- Art 69(3) wording confirmed; Art 71(2) dates. Reporting continues after the end of support.
- Penalties: Art 64(2) top tier up to EUR 15m or 2.5% of worldwide turnover; micro and small enterprises exempt from fines for missing the 24h deadlines (Art 64(10)(a)); enforcement machinery applies from 11 Dec 2027.
- Digital Omnibus (COM(2025) 837, 19 Nov 2025, 2025/0360(COD)) does not amend the CRA. It inserts NIS2 Art 23a (an ENISA single-entry point that "may" build on the CRA SRP), mandates its use for NIS2/GDPR/DORA/eIDAS/CER, moves GDPR Art 33 to 96 hours, and adds NIS2 Art 23(12) deeming a CRA Art 14(3) severe-incident report to satisfy NIS2 Art 23(4) where it contains the required information. Council mandate agreed by Coreper 21 Oct 2026 (ST 14756/26 of 22 Oct): national entry points (new Art 23b) plus an ENISA "incident reporting information point" that cannot receive notifications; keeps Art 23(12); keeps 96h GDPR; fallback application 30 months. Parliament: joint ITRE/LIBE, rapporteurs Aura Salla (EPP) and Marina Kaljurand (S&D); draft report 22 June 2026, amendments 27 July 2026 (over 1,750), "awaiting committee decision", no vote, no trilogue. Nothing changes CRA Art 14 timelines, content or recipients, and nothing could before 2028.
- Multi-regime: cumulative today; different triggers, recipients and clocks; the CRA awareness test is aligned with NIS2 Implementing Regulation 2024/2690 recital 31 and EDPB Guidelines 9/2022 (guidance para 212). AFME (July 2026) asked that financial entities report only under DORA with onward sharing: a request, not law.

## 6. Open source (open_source.md)

- Art 2 never mentions open source; the carve-out works through Art 3(22) "making available on the market" = supply in the course of a commercial activity, read with recitals 15-20. Guidance C(2026) 5252 Section 3 (points 40-88, Examples 13-34): unmonetised FOSS is outside the CRA unless the publisher is a steward (pt 43); charging for binaries or updates = manufacturer (pt 51); an open-core community edition stays unplaced while the paid edition is a product (pts 52-53); separately sold support or consulting does not trigger placing, the decisive factor being whether access to the software incl. maintenance requires payment (pts 55-57); donations are a problem only when they gate access or updates (pts 60-62); grants, bounties and paid development are irrelevant to placing (pts 63-65); not-for-profits reinvesting earnings are not commercial (pt 66); a company publishing unmonetised FOSS it uses in its own products is a steward for it (pts 67-68, Ex 25/29). Dual licensing and paid hosting are not addressed in the guidance: gaps.
- Stewards (Art 3(14), Art 24): a legal person other than a manufacturer, sustaining FOSS "intended for commercial activities" without placing it on the market; foundations, not-for-profits and ordinary companies all qualify, per project (pts 69-74). Obligations: a documented, verifiable cybersecurity policy (Art 24(1)); cooperation with MSAs (24(2)); Art 14 reporting only to the extent involved in development or running development infrastructure (24(3)); the guidance scales this by support type (pts 79-82: non-technical = no reporting; infrastructure = severe incidents; engineering = actively exploited vulnerabilities). No CE marking (recital 19), no fines (Art 64(10)(b)), no SBOM, technical documentation or Annex I; corrective action under Art 52(3) remains. Applies from 11 Dec 2027 (Art 71). No standalone Commission or ENISA steward guidance beyond guidance Section 3.3 and the FAQ.
- Art 25 attestations: a delegated act (Art 61); recital 21 also allows a CSA certification-scheme route. Status: no delegated act, no Commission, ENISA or national programme found; Commission FOSDEM slides (1 Feb 2026) mention only a study; FSFE survey closed 28 Feb 2026. ORC WG two-tier framework draft (Tier 1 self-declaration aligned to BSI TR-03185-2; Tier 2 undecided).
- Contributors and maintainers: recital 18 and guidance pts 48-49, 85-86: contributing code (even with commit rights) to FOSS "not under their responsibility" is outside the CRA; natural persons who do not monetise have no obligations and cannot be stewards (pt 53); cost recovery incl. "reasonable living expenses" is fine (pt 58).
- Manufacturers consuming FOSS: Art 13(5) due diligence incl. unplaced FOSS; Art 13(6) report vulnerabilities to the maintainer and share fixes, machine-readable where appropriate; Art 13(7) documentation; SBOM in Annex I Part II(1) (recital 77: not required to be public); Art 32(5) lets important (Annex III) FOSS products use internal control if the technical documentation is public. Integrators are not responsible for the component's own compliance (pts 86-88).
- Ecosystem: ORC WG (Eclipse; born from the 2024 Apache/Blender/Eclipse/OpenSSL/PHP/PSF/Rust pact): steward white paper V1.0 shipped Jan 2026; due-diligence paper WIP; D4.1 vulnerability-management spec WIP; D4.2/D4.3/D4.4 (principles, generic requirements, steward security policy) not shipped as of Sept 2026; consultation submissions shipped; ORC Learning Hub launched. LF/OpenSSF: Stewards Playbook, One Pager, maintainer readiness guide, manufacturer checklist, OSPS Baseline (41 requirements, 3 levels). LF Research (Mar 2025): 62% unfamiliar with the CRA; 74% of stewards have policies; top steward gaps funding (50%), legal (47%), technical (44%). No EU funding targeted at stewards found (OCCTET is for SMEs; STAN4CR2 funds ETSI).
- Caveats: operative text via the enobyte.com mirror; entry into force 10 Dec 2024 per Commission/EUR-Lex vs 11 Dec 2024 on the ORC page (use 10 Dec 2024).

## 7. Timeline, enforcement, interplay, UK (timeline_enforcement_interplay_uk.md)

- Art 71: in force 10 Dec 2024; Chapter IV from 11 Jun 2026; Art 14 from 11 Sep 2026; everything else 11 Dec 2027. Art 69(1): existing EU type-examination certificates and approval decisions on cybersecurity requirements under other harmonisation law remain valid until 11 June 2028; Art 69(2): pre-11 Dec 2027 products caught only on substantial modification; Art 69(3): Art 14 "shall apply to all products" placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027.
- Enforcement, Arts 52-60: reasoned-request access to design, development and vulnerability-handling data (Art 53); Art 54 three-month "deemed justified" rule; Art 55 nine-month Commission decision; Art 56 Commission implementing acts; Art 57 compliant-but-risky products; Art 58 formal non-compliance; Art 60 sweeps with "cover identity" purchases; Art 16(3) feed from CSIRTs to MSAs. Art 64 tiers: EUR 15m or 2.5% (Annex I, Arts 13-14); EUR 10m or 2% (Arts 18-23, 28, 30(1)-(4), 31(1)-(4), 32(1)-(3), 33(5), 39, 41, 47, 49, 53); EUR 5m or 1% (misleading information); Art 64(10) exempts OSS stewards entirely and micro/small manufacturers for the 24-hour early-warning deadlines. Member State status: adopted — Finland (Act 439/2026, Traficom), Hungary (Act CXXXV/2025, SZTFH), Malta (LN 238/2026, MDIA, own fine regime); passed by parliament, awaiting promulgation — Germany (CRA Implementation Act passed the Bundestag plenary 15 Oct 2026, BSI; fines from 11 Dec 2027); drafts — Netherlands (RDI), Italy (ACN, 9 Oct 2026), Denmark (5 Oct 2026), Spain (no penalty regime yet); France decree 2026-944 names ANSSI as notifying authority only; Ireland NCSC not yet designated.
- Interplay: only the AI Act has a true "deemed to comply" rule (CRA Art 12(1); AI Act Art 42(3) inserted by Reg 2026/1744). RED Delegated Regulation 2022/30 is repealed by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/339 (adopted 16 Feb 2026, OJ 29 Apr 2026, in force 19 May 2026) with effect from 11 December 2027; equipment placed 1 Aug 2025 to 10 Dec 2027 stays under RED/EN 18031. Machinery Regulation: recital 53 facilitation (Annex III 1.1.9/1.2.1), not presumption; Machinery Art 20(9) presumption is for Reg 2019/881 certificates. MDR/IVDR, Reg 2019/2144 vehicles, certified aviation and marine equipment excluded (Art 2(2)-(4)). GDPR recital 32 and Art 52(7) DPA cooperation. Recital 72 names GDPR, DORA, ePrivacy and NIS2 as complementary reporting regimes. NIS2 boundary: recital 12. PLD 2024/2853 (from 9 Dec 2026): software is a product, a cybersecurity vulnerability can be a defect (recital 32), Art 7(2)(f) "safety-relevant cybersecurity requirements", Art 10(2)(b) presumption on breach of mandatory safety requirements, Art 11(2)(c) no later-defect defence for missing security updates. DORA: no cross-reference either way; Art 28(5) "highest quality information security standards" is the commercial hook. EUCS still not adopted; CSA2 proposed 20 Jan 2026.
- Digital Omnibus: the AI Omnibus is law (Reg (EU) 2026/1744, OJ 24 Jul 2026, in force 27 Jul 2026; Annex III high-risk from 2 Dec 2027, Annex I from 2 Aug 2028). The data/cyber Omnibus COM(2025) 837 (2025/0360(COD)) does not amend the CRA; it proposes an ENISA-run NIS2 single entry point (18 months, extendable to 24) covering NIS2/GDPR/DORA/eIDAS/CER and "building on" the CRA platform. Status 30 Oct 2026: Coreper mandate 21 Oct 2026 (ST 14756/26) replacing the ENISA single entry point with a national entry point (new NIS2 Art 23b); Parliament "awaiting committee decision" (ITRE/LIBE, Salla/Kaljurand; draft report 22 Jun 2026, over 1,750 amendments); trilogues not started. No CRA date, reporting, SBOM or documentation change adopted.
- UK and Northern Ireland: the CRA applies to any product made available in the EU; GB manufacturers are third-country manufacturers; Art 18 authorised representative optional; Art 19 importer duties; Art 21 rebranding or modification = manufacturer; Art 14(7) CSIRT routing for non-EU manufacturers. CRA Art 66 adds the CRA to Annex I of Reg 2019/1020 but not to Art 4(5), so the CRA itself imposes no mandatory EU "responsible economic operator"; but RED (Art 4(5)), GPSR Art 16 (via CRA Art 11) and PLD Art 8 bite on direct-to-consumer sellers; Reg 2019/1020 Art 6 deems targeted online offers "made available". Northern Ireland: the CRA is not in Annex 2 of the Windsor Framework; only Arts 66/68 apply via Art 13(3); Council Decision (EU) 2025/799 (14 Apr 2025) proposes adding it to point 47; the Art 13(4) exchange of views ran Jun 2025 to 7 May 2026 (reported concluded), no Joint Committee decision found as of 30 Oct 2026; UK agreement requires Schedule 6B safeguards (applicability motion); the DSIT explanatory memorandum of 5 May 2026 says the exchange "remains ongoing". PSTI SI 2023/1007 Sch 3 para 1 would switch NI supplies from PSTI to the CRA if it is added. - Gaps: Art 53 source-code paragraph not confirmed; operative articles of COM(2025) 837 not retrieved; Joint Committee outcome on NI not confirmed; CSR Bill stage dates from vendor trackers; EUCS post-2025 status and CSA2 trilogue status unverified.

## 9. Standards and guidance landscape (standards_and_guidance_landscape.md)

- Standardisation request M/606 = Commission Implementing Decision C(2025) 618 of 3 Feb 2025 (accepted by CEN/CENELEC/ETSI 3 Apr 2025): 41 standards — 15 horizontal (item 1 risk-based design, items 2-14 Annex I Part I properties, item 15 vulnerability handling) and 26 verticals (items 16-41 covering all Annex III/IV categories). Original deadlines: 30 Aug 2026 (items 1 and 15), 30 Oct 2026 (items 16-41), 30 Oct 2027 (items 2-14). A draft amending decision notified 7 July 2026 (feedback to 3 Aug 2026) would move these to 30 Oct 2026 / 31 Dec 2026 / 30 Oct 2027, citing the ESOs' 3 Dec 2025 semestrial report and "procedural and technical complexities"; as of trackers dated 17-26 Oct 2026 it had not been adopted, so the operative deadlines remain the original ones. A second CRA standardisation request (products outside Annex III/IV) is being drafted per the 2026 AUWP.
- CEN/CENELEC: EN 40000-1-1 (vocabulary) and EN 40000-1-2 (principles) passed formal vote, ratification scheduled 2 Nov 2026, but CEN-CENELEC records "no citation expected". prEN 40000-1-3 (vulnerability handling; enquiry 19 Dec 2025 to 15 Feb 2026; normatively built on ISO/IEC 29147/30111; SBOM of top-level dependencies) went to the Commission for pre-formal-vote HAS assessment on 3 Aug 2026 and is the only horizontal standard expected to be cited; prEN 40000-1-4 (generic requirements, clauses 6.1-6.13 mirror Annex I 2(a)-(m), built on EN 18031 minus its applicability trees) is still drafting against Oct 2027. Verticals: TC 47X chip standards (prEN 50764/50765/50766, enquiry 24 Jan to 25 Mar 2026; being renumbered EN 40001-3/40002-3/40003-3/40004-3) and TC 224 (prEN 18330, EN 40000-10, EN 40000-11) have closed enquiry; CLC/TC 65X prEN 50770-1 to -6 OT profiles remain draft or PWI pending EN IEC 62443-4-2/-4-1 A11:2026 amendments; the smart meter gateway standard has no number. No CRA harmonised standard is cited in the OJ (the Commission index has no entry for Reg 2024/2847); observers expect first citations in 2027 at the earliest.
- ETSI: TC CYBER EUSR opened Public Enquiry on 17 final drafts (EN 304 617-627, 631-636) on 13 Aug 2026, windows closing mid-Sept to mid-Nov 2026 (e.g. EN 304 627 routers 16 Jul to 20 Sep; EN 304 626 OS 26 Aug to 25 Oct); plus EN 304 642 telecom functions tracked. Drafts share a common clause spine and carry an Annex A CRA mapping; the EN 304 627 enquiry draft did not yet reference EN 40000-1-3. The ETSI docbox "open" area now requires login. EN 303 645 remains at V3.1.3 (Sept 2024); no CRA-driven revision found.
- HAS/OJ process: mandatory HAS assessments at mature-draft and pre-formal-vote stages, Common Checklist and Annex ZZ, then Commission assessment under Reg 1025/2012 (CRA Art 27(6)). Under Art 32(2) and guidance paras 148-151 an important Class I product can use Module A only if it fully applies a cited harmonised standard (or common specification or substantial-level certification) covering its core-functionality risks; otherwise Module B+C or H, and NANDO was empty for CRA notified bodies as of Sept 2026 (Art 35(2) target 11 Dec 2026). RED precedent: Decision (EU) 2025/138 cited EN 18031-1/-2/-3 with restrictions.
- Common specifications (Art 27(2) / Art 28): none adopted or signalled; the Commission's June 2026 line was that standardisation is "progressing well"; its levers are deadline extension and a second request.
- Certification: EUCC (Implementing Reg (EU) 2024/482, amended 2024/3144; applicable 27 Feb 2025) is the only adopted EU scheme; the ENISA register shows 103 valid certificates from 8 CBs (ANSSI, atsec, BSI, Combitech, DEKRA, Applus/LGAI, SGS Brightsight, TrustCB); first "substantial" certificate 23 July 2025 (DEKRA, GMV GNSS crypto module). ENISA's Jan/Feb 2025 study maps Annex I to CC SFR/SARs (ALC_FLR, extended ALC_SBM.1, ALC_PSR.1; AVA_VAN.1-2 = substantial, AVA_VAN.3-5 = high) but finds CVD policy and free updates outside CC, PPs narrower than product scope, RDPS unaddressed, CC unsuited to cloud. Art 27(9) delegated act recognising EUCC: planned Q4 2026 (Commission factpage); content paper promised to the Expert Group June 2026; not adopted as far as could be verified. Commission said Feb 2025 it was not preparing Art 8 acts to make certification mandatory for critical products. EUCS: unadopted draft, deadlocked since 2024; COM(2026) 502 (3 June 2026) confirms "not yet adopted", work to resume alongside CSA2 (proposed 20 Jan 2026). EU5G: not adopted.
- Guidance inventory (table in the notes with dates and URLs): Commission Art 26 guidance C(2026) 5252 (27 July 2026); Commission FAQ v1.0 (3 Dec 2025) through v1.4 (4 Sept 2026), 66 pp; Implementing Reg (EU) 2025/2392 (technical descriptions, 28 Nov 2025); Delegated Reg (EU) 2026/881 (delaying dissemination, adopted 11 Dec 2025); Expert Group E03967 minutes (5th meeting 10/11 June 2026 is the richest primary source on standards and EUCC state of play); ENISA: JRC-ENISA standards mapping (4 Apr 2024), CRA-via-EUCC study (26 Feb 2025), Secure by Design and Default Playbook v1 (30 July 2026, 22 playbooks), package-manager advisory (10 Mar 2026), secure-update advisory (draft v0.6, consulted to 10 July 2026), CAB competence requirements (4 June 2026), SRP hub live 11 Sept 2026 with FAQ/glossary/AR manual/CSIRT list; BSI TR-03183-1 v1.0.0 (5 Aug 2026 per BSI page; the PDF says 31 Jul 2026), -2 v2.1.0, -3 v1.0.0, -H v1.1.0 (non-binding, no presumption). CRA-AI Act and CRA-DORA interplay guidance targeted for end-2026.
- Standards mapping (15 rows) with official gap findings: JRC-ENISA: "no single standard" covers all; weakest on hardware, implementation detail, generic products and Part II (SBOM, free and timely updates with notification, third-party component information sharing, testing cadence); ISO/IEC 30111 covers 5 of 8 and 29147 covers 4 of 8 Part II items; ISO/IEC 27002 covers 6 of 13 Part I items; EN 303 645 is high-level consumer IoT; IEC 62443 is IACS-only and not expected to be cited.
