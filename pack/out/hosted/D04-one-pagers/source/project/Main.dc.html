02 · Scope

Almost every connected product is in scope

Three tests, all required (Art 2(1)): a product with digital elements · made available in a commercial activity · with a direct or indirect data connection.

In scope

–  Hardware and software, including separately sold firmware, libraries, SDKs and chips

–  Companion apps and manufacturer-built backends a function needs (remote data processing)

–  Free or B2B-only products supplied commercially

–  Offline software on a networked host

Out of scope

–  Medical and IVD devices, type-approved vehicles, certified aviation, marine equipment

–  Products made only for national security or defence

–  Identical spare parts; internal tools never placed on the market

–  Websites and SaaS used only in a browser, unless a product’s backend

–  Open source that its publisher does not monetise

Units placed before 11 Dec 2027 need not meet Annex I unless substantially modified, but Art 14 reporting covers them all (Art 69).

03 · Roles

The brand owner carries almost every duty

Manufacturer · develops or has a product developed and markets it under its own name; Arts 13–14

Importer · EU-based; places non-EU-branded products; checks conformity and documents (Art 19)

Distributor · checks CE marking and documents; withholds non-conforming products (Art 20)

Authorised representative · optional; holds the DoC and file for 10 years; no design duties (Art 18)

Open-source steward · sustains FOSS for commercial use; light duties, no CE marking, no fines (Art 24)

Selling under your own brand, or substantially modifying a product, makes you its manufacturer (Arts 21–22).

04 · Classes and routes

Class decides whether you may self-assess

Default

A

Everything not listed in Annexes III–IV: self-assessment

Important · Class I · 19 categories

A*B+CH

*Self-assessment only with a cited harmonised standard applied in full; otherwise a notified body

e.g. browsers, password managers, VPNs, operating systems, routers, switches, smart locks, cameras, health wearables

Important · Class II · 4 categories

B+CHCert

A notified body, or EU certification at ‘substantial’ once a scheme is recognised

Hypervisors and container runtimes; firewalls, IDS and IPS; tamper-resistant microprocessors and microcontrollers

Critical · 3 categories

Cert

EU certification if an Art 8(1) act requires it (none adopted); otherwise as Class II

Hardware devices with security boxes; smart meter gateways; smartcards and secure elements

A internal control · B+C type examination, then conformity to type · H full quality assurance · Cert EU certification

Status as at 30 Oct 2026

No harmonised standard cited; no notified body in NANDO at the 8 Sep 2026 check: important and critical products cannot yet be assessed.

09 · Act now

Five moves to make now

1

Make reporting ready

Register at least two people on ENISA’s platform, fix the coordinator CSIRT, staff 24/7 triage and drill a weekend case.

2

Inventory and classify

List every product, version, variant, app and backend; record scope, class and conformity route per SKU.

3

Book notified-body capacity

Pre-contract candidate bodies for important and critical products: capacity, not engineering, sets the critical path.

4

Build once, apply per product

One PSIRT and disclosure policy, build-time SBOMs, signed updates and an evidence system kept for 10 years.

5

Keep a Module A record for every product

A complete Annex VII file and risk assessment: the fallback route, and the notified body’s application pack.
