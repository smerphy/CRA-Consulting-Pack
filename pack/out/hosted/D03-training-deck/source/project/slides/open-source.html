Treat open-source components as suppliers we cannot contract with

Component intake gate: the due-diligence record, Art 13(5)

FieldWhat to record
NeedFunction we use and security properties we rely on
MaintenanceCadence, fix times, maintainers, OpenSSF Scorecard
LicenceSPDX identifier; duties when we share fixes upstream
Vulnerability historyEUVD, NVD and OSV entries; fixes in supported branches
Support horizonUpstream end of life against our support period
Exit planReplacements, effort to swap, isolation if it stays
DecisionApproved, with conditions or rejected; owner and date

Survey: 51% of manufacturers rely passively on upstream projects for security fixes; integrating never transfers our duty.

Found a bug in a component?

1 · Check it is not already known: databases, advisories, issue tracker

2 · Report the version we integrate through the maintainer’s disclosure channel, Art 13(6)

3 · Share our fix as a licence-compatible patch, machine-readable where appropriate

4 · Log it in the PSIRT case; carry the patch on a vendor branch named by CVE

No duty that the fix is merged; with no maintainer, the product duty stays ours alone.

Source: CRA Art 13(5)–(6); guidance paras 222–229; Linux Foundation 2026 (survey, manufacturer module n = 194–219)

Illustrative · 14

Open source is most of what we ship, and integrating it never transfers our duty. Treat each component as a supplier you cannot contract with. Before a dependency enters the product, the intake gate records the need, maintenance signals such as release cadence and the OpenSSF Scorecard, the licence, vulnerability history, the upstream support horizon against our support period, and an exit plan. A component whose upstream support ends inside our support period needs that exit plan at intake. When we find a vulnerability in a component, Article 13(6) requires us to report it to the maintainer through their disclosure channel and to share any fix we develop. There is no duty that it is merged, and none where no maintainer exists, but then the product duty is ours alone. Keep patches as discrete commits on a vendor branch named by CVE; silent forks become our maintenance burden.
